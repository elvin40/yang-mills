## 4. Their status on the one-loop comparison lemma |Σβ⁰_l − k c₀| ≤ c₂ (task 4)

Statement: Lemma 4.2 "the one-loop coefficient and the finite part of the one-loop law; [B-CMP109 (1.22), Theorem 3], [B-CMP116 (2.41)]" [T0-I l. 3901–3925]. Part (b) [quoted]: "There is a constant c_2≥0, depending only on L and N, such that for every k≥0 |Σ_{l=1}^k β^0_l − k c_0| ≤ c_2." β⁰_l is defined [l. 3862–3880] by applying the increment coefficient "to the functional B ↦ F_{l−1}(U_l(e^{iB})) alone", F_{l−1} = log Z^{(l−1)} − log Z^{(l−1)}(1) (the Gaussian normaliser). This is our V1 / (H3) object.

Proof structure [l. 3933–4000, quoted]:
- "For (b), the telescoping identity and the two comparison bounds with k-independent constants are used as stated in [B-CMP109, Theorem 3] and [B-CMP116, (2.41)]; the derivation of (b) from them is as follows."
- Step 1, telescoping: "These three properties give the telescoping identity Σ_{l=1}^k β^0_l = B_k." Argued from linearity of the coefficient, its definition as the p² coefficient, and invariance under reading a finer functional through the coarser minimal configuration. (= our BETA_FP §3 telescoping S_k = Σβ_j(0), verified.)
- Step 2, comparison: "|B_k−B^ref_k| ≤ ½C_cmp(L,N)" with B^ref_k the coefficient of "−½ log det(D²_W+η²) + log det(Δ_0+η²), minus its value at the trivial background", η = L^{−k}. Text: "The first bound is the comparison of the composite Gaussian normaliser with the reference determinants at η=L^{−k}." No proof is given.
- Step 3, lattice one-loop: "|B^ref_k − (11C_g/24π²) log(1/η)| ≤ C_det(N)". Text: "The second is the lattice one-loop computation. There the gluon determinant contributes 5C_g/(12π²) per unit of log(1/η), and the ghost determinant contributes C_g/(24π²)." No proof is given beyond this sentence.
- Then c_2 := C_det(N) + ½C_cmp(L,N) and the triangle inequality.

Which step is "incomplete". The status line at l. 56363 reads [quoted]: "the finite part of the one-loop law (Lemma 4.2:lem-one-loop, whose proof is incomplete at one step)"; l. 56428–56429: "Lemma 4.2:lem-one-loop, and ... Lemma 4.2:lem-unmarked, each stated with its proof incomplete at the step marked (∗) there". But the text of Lemma 4.2 (l. 3901–4000) carries no (∗) marker (checked by grep), and Chapter 13 (their complete gap list) does not list Lemma 4.2; it lists only "Lemma 4.2:lem-unmarked · stated; proof incomplete" [T0-III l. 111368]. So their own bookkeeping is inconsistent. [V, grep]
My reading [inference, checked against sources]: the unproved step is Step 2, the comparison |B_k − B^ref_k| ≤ ½C_cmp with C_cmp independent of k, together with Step 3 (asserted, not computed). The cited sources do not contain Step 2:
- CMP 109 (1.22) is the definition of β_{j+1} as the p_μp_ν coefficient of the vacuum polarization, followed by [quoted, local balaban/1987_cmp109_rg_approach_I.txt l. 796–799] "It is a smooth function defined on the interval [0, γ] ... So Step 2 is exactly our (H-cmp): "block constraint as IR cutoff" versus "mass η = L^{−k} as IR cutoff", uniformly in k. Step 3 is our (H-ref) / Lemma 4.3 lattice one-loop value with an IR mass. Neither is proved in their account.

The (∗) step [quoted, l. 4078–4082]: "The argument is Bałaban's for [B-CMP116, Lemma 3], carried out with the small-field cutoff of [B-CMP119], at the radius δ_{l−1}/g_{l−1} ... The estimates of [B-CMP116] and [B-CMP109] that the proof combines are quoted, not re-derived." This is our (H0) C¹ bound (Balaban's inductive assumption), in an ε_1-small form, not g-small.
Consequence: their clause (0) lower bound λ_♯(j−i) − c_5 ≤ x_i − x_j and every clause downstream (iii-d, iv, v) rest on Lemma 4.2(b). Their first-passage device removes positivity and continuity requirements but does not remove (H). ### 5.2 Assessment
Their account and ours target the same object by the same route: Bałaban's RG with containment 0 < g_k ≤ γ, gauge-invariant curvature sources carried as a local coupling, and a compactness-form torus theorem. Their statement is far broader (infinite volume along subsequences, a two-point nontriviality witness, a one-parameter uniqueness family, conditional O(4)), and their text is about 400 times longer than ours.
