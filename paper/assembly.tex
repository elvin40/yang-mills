\section{The finite-torus existence theorem}\label{sec:torus}

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:L003}. The trajectory of Section~\ref{sec:flow} enters as the block (B-T2) (H-084). Section~\ref{sec:exists} fixes what ``exists'' means here. Section~\ref{sec:HU} states the uniform bound (H-U) (H-116) that the blocks must give. Sections~\ref{sec:existthm} and~\ref{sec:nontrivthm} state the theorems. Section~\ref{sec:blocks} proves them modulo the named blocks. Section~\ref{sec:deptable} gives the dependency table of the paper. Section~\ref{sec:numerics} collects the numerical evidence for the hypotheses.

The theorem is a reduction to named blocks. It is not unconditional. The notation is that of Sections~\ref{sec:lattice} and~\ref{sec:observables}.

\subsection{What ``exists'' means here}\label{sec:exists}
Jaffe and Witten write in footnote 2 of \cite{jaffewitten}: ``We specifically exclude weak-existence (compactness) as the solution to the existence part of the Millennium problem, unless one also uses other techniques to establish properties of the limit (such as the existence of a mass gap and the axioms).''

We use the following reading. ``Exists'' means: (i) the family $\{S_{n,\varepsilon}\}$ is relatively compact (uniform bounds); (ii) at least one limit point exists along a sequence $\varepsilon_k\to0$; (iii) every limit point has the properties (OS0)--(OS3) below, proved on the limit and not assumed. We claim no uniqueness. Convergence of the full sequence needs E13 (comparison of two trajectories; (E14-d) (H-099), D6), which this paper does not give.

This reading is weaker than the parenthesis of the footnote. The theorem below gives no gap and only the torus forms of four axioms. It is the finite-torus step of Jaffe--Witten \S6.5: ``One must then verify the existence of limits of appropriate expectations of gauge-invariant observables as the lattice spacing tends to zero and as the volume tends to infinity''. Only the first limit is taken here.

The problem description \cite{jaffewitten} does not state the OS axioms. It cites them as OS (CMP 31 (1973), CMP 42 (1975)) \cite{os1973,os1975} and requires ``axiomatic properties at least as strong as those cited''. We use OS II in the form stated in \cite{OAI26-O3}: ``The linear growth condition requires a bound $|S_n(F)|\le\sigma_n|F|_{s_n}$ with fixed $s$ and $\sigma_n\le A(n!)^D$ for fixed $A$, $D$''. Restriction to Schwartz tests that are flat on the coincidence diagonals gives the test class of that reference.

\subsection{Hypothesis delivered by the blocks}\label{sec:HU}
\begin{definition}[Uniform bound (H-U), H-116]\label{def:H116}
There are $s\in\mathbb N$ and $A,D<\infty$ such that for all $K\ge K_0$, all $n\ge1$, and all $F\in\mathcal D_n$:
\[|S_{n,\varepsilon}(F)|\le\sigma_n\|F\|_{sn},\qquad\sigma_n:=A(n!)^D,\quad\varepsilon=L^{-K}.\]
\end{definition}
The derivative order $sn$ grows linearly in $n$. The OS linear growth condition admits this. The blocks (B-T2) (H-084), (B-S) (R-035), and the bridge (B-N1) (R-045) of Section~\ref{sec:blocks} give (H-U).

\subsection{Theorem (finite-torus existence, compactness form)}\label{sec:existthm}
\begin{theorem}[finite-torus existence, compactness form]\label{thm:L013}
Let $G=SU(2)$, $d=4$, $L$ odd and large, $\ell\in2\mathbb N$ fixed. Let $\gamma>0$ be Ba{\l}aban's small constant and $g\in\,]0,\gamma]$ small. For $K\ge K_0$ let $\varepsilon=L^{-K}$, and let $g_0=g_0(\varepsilon,g)$ be a shooting solution (Lemma~\ref{lem:L005}) whose effective couplings satisfy $g_k\in\,]0,\gamma]$ for $0\le k\le K$ and $g_K=g$. Assume (H-U) (H-116). Then:

(a) Compactness. Every sequence $K_m\to\infty$ has a subsequence $K_{m_i}$ such that, with $\varepsilon_i=L^{-K_{m_i}}$, the limit $S_n(F):=\lim_iS_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F)$ exists for every $n\ge1$ and every $F\in\mathcal D_n$.

(b) Every limit family $(S_n)_{n\ge1}$ obtained in (a) has the following properties.
\begin{itemize}
\item (OS0) Regularity. $S_n$ is linear on $\mathcal D_n$, and $|S_n(F)|\le A(n!)^D\|F\|_{sn}$. In particular $S_n$ restricted to $C_c^\infty(T^{4n}\setminus\bigcup_{i<j}\{x_i=x_j\})$ is a distribution of order $\le sn$.
\item (OS1) Invariance. $S_n(F\circ\tau_a^{\times n})=S_n(F)$ for every $a\in T^4_\ell$ (all continuous translations), and $S_n(F\circ R^{\times n})=S_n(F)$ for every $R$ in the hypercubic group $B_4$ (signed permutations of the axes, acting about a lattice site).
\item (OS2) Reflection positivity. Let $\theta(x_0,\vec x)=(-x_0,\vec x)$ on $T^4_\ell$ and $T_+:=\{x:x_0\in\,]0,\ell/2[\}$. For every finite family $F_0\in\mathbb C$, $F_n\in\mathcal D_n$ with $\operatorname{supp}F_n\subset T_+^n$ ($1\le n\le N$):
\[\sum_{n,m=0}^NS_{n+m}(\Theta F_n\otimes F_m)\ge0,\quad(\Theta F_n)(x_1,\dots,x_n):=\overline{F_n(\theta x_n,\dots,\theta x_1)},\quad S_0:=1.\]
\item (OS3) Symmetry and reality. $S_n(F\circ\pi)=S_n(F)$ for every permutation $\pi$ of the $n$ arguments, and $S_n(F)\in\mathbb R$ for real $F$.
\end{itemize}

(c) Nontriviality is a separate statement, Theorem~\ref{thm:L014}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[inputs of (H-U)]
If (H-U) is derived from the blocks of Section~\ref{sec:blocks} and not assumed, the derivation also uses the recovery $\kappa\to\kappa/2$ of the decay rate in the sourced small-field step D2 (R-046). Ba{\l}aban does not state this recovery, and it does not follow from the other hypotheses. We reduce it to the large-field T-step rate loss R-020 (Section~\ref{sec:rem-sources}). The sourced blocks also use the unit-scale condition (U) (K-024), $\rho_1\le c_*r_0/M^a$ at $\lambda_K=1$, where $r_0$ is the Cauchy margin of the constant-source family. This condition is a choice of constants, made once after $r_0$ and before $K$. The bridge (B-N1) meets it by the rescaling $\lambda_i=2N_U(f_i)$ at a cost $C^n$; $s$ and $D$ do not change. This (U) is not the Route B hypothesis (U).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what is claimed about measures]
We claim no probability measure. The limit is a family of Schwinger functions on $\mathcal D_n$. A measure on distributions would need finite moments of smeared fields, and $\operatorname{Var}\sum_pf(x_p)\varphi_p\sim\varepsilon^{-4}$ (Section~\ref{sec:observables}). The properties (OS0)--(OS3) on $\mathcal D_n$ are the inputs of OS reconstruction.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Theorem (nontriviality), conditional on (NT)}\label{sec:nontrivthm}
\begin{theorem}[nontriviality, conditional on (NT) of Section~\ref{sec:BNT}]\label{thm:L014}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:L013} and Hypothesis (NT). Then every limit family has the following properties.
(i) $S_2\ne0$: there are $f_1,f_2\in C^\infty(T^4)$ with disjoint supports and $S_2(f_1\otimes f_2)\ne0$.
(ii) $S_2$ is not the two-point function of a scale-invariant (in particular free Maxwell) composite: $d^8S_2(x,y)$, $|x-y|=d$, is not asymptotically constant as $d\to0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{remark}[form of (ii)]\label{corr:nt}
The proof of (ii) uses only the two-sided bound (0.31). It gives the envelope $d^8S_2\to0$ and no asymptotic form. The stronger form $d^8S_2\sim c\,g(d)^4$ with $g(d)^2\sim(\beta\log(1/d))^{-1}$ would need the convergence $\beta_j\to\beta_\infty$, which we do not prove.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{(B-N1) Bridge: source-form bounds $\Rightarrow$ (H-U)}\label{sec:BN1}
The source form alone does not give $|S_n(F)|\le C^nn!\|F\|_{s,n}$ with a fixed $s$ for $F$ flat on the diagonals. The bridge uses the growth $sn$ of the OS index instead.

\begin{proposition}[bridge (B-N1)]\label{prop:BN1}
Assume the sourced Gevrey bound (S) (R-045) in the following form: for $f=\sum_iz_if_i$ with disjoint supports, the mixed $z$-derivatives of $\log Z_\varepsilon[f]-W_\varepsilon[f]$ of order $n$ are bounded by $C^n(n!)^{1+\eta}$ in units of the $C^1$ norm $\max(\|f\|_\infty/r,\|\nabla f\|_\infty/\rho_1)$, uniformly in the position and size of the supports and in $\varepsilon$. Then (H-U) holds with $s=6$ and some $D>1$. Under the fallback order $2+\eta$ in place of $1+\eta$, (H-U) holds with $s=6$ and some $D>2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof (sketch)]
We give the structure of the argument. The sketch uses the class $F_r$ with five derivatives; it gives the cruder exponents $\delta^{-5n}$ and $s\ge20$. The $C^1$ norm of the hypothesis gives $s=6$ by the same steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Moments from cumulants. $\langle\prod_i\phi(f_i)\rangle=\sum_{\text{partitions}}\prod_{\text{blocks}}\kappa(\cdot)$. Singleton cumulants vanish ($\varphi$ is centred). For disjoint supports the source form bounds every cumulant by $C^{|B|}|B|!\,r^{-|B|}$, so the moment by $C^nn!\,r^{-n}$.
\item Scale-$\delta$ tests. A bump of side $\delta\le1$ and amplitude $a$ has $|\partial^5f|\sim a\delta^{-5}$; it lies in $F_r$ if $a\le cr\delta^5$. So $n$ disjoint unit-amplitude scale-$\delta$ bumps have $|\text{moment}|\le C^nn!\,\delta^{-5n}$. This is polynomial in $\delta^{-1}$.
\item Whitney decomposition. Split $T^{4n}$ by a smooth dyadic partition in $\rho(x):=\min_{i<j}|x_i-x_j|\in[\delta,2\delta]$, $\delta=2^{-m}\ge\varepsilon$. On each piece cover by cubes of side $\delta/100$; cube tuples are pairwise disjoint. Expand $F\cdot$(cutoffs) in a Fourier series on each cube tuple; the coefficients decay faster than any power; each term is a product of disjointly supported scale-$\delta$ bumps. Flatness gives $|\partial^\beta F(x)|\le C_k\|F\|_{k+|\beta|}\rho(x)^k/k!$. There are $\le C^n\delta^{-4n}$ cube tuples. Derivatives of the cutoffs cost at most $(Cn^2/\delta)^M$; with $M=O(n)$ this is $\le C^n(n!)^c\delta^{-M}$. Complex Fourier modes against real sources cost a factor $2^n$, and the derivatives of the Fourier summation are absorbed in $s$.
\item Bound per dyadic scale: $\le C^n(n!)^{D'}\delta^{k-9n-M}\|F\|_{k+M}$. Choose $k=sn-M$ with $s$ large enough so that the $\delta$-exponent is $\ge n$; the sum over dyadic $\delta\in[\varepsilon,1]$ converges uniformly in $\varepsilon$.
\item Sub-lattice region $\rho(x)\lesssim\varepsilon$: there are $\le C^n\varepsilon^{-4n}$ plaquette tuples, each moment is $\le2^n$, and $|F|\le\|F\|_k\varepsilon^k$; this is $\le C^n\|F\|_{sn}$ for $k=sn\ge4n$.
\end{enumerate}
The full argument, with the $C^1$ norm, the order $(n!)^{1+\eta}$, and the smooth min-distance partition, gives $s=6$ and $D>1$.
\end{proof}
Section~\ref{sec:rem-sources} lists the inputs of (S): the uniformity in the support size and in $\varepsilon$, and the (E14-c) (H-098) clause, which reduces to (S). Given (FX) (R-043), the step (S$_{\mathrm{src}}$) $\Rightarrow$ (H-U) with $s=6$, $D>1$ holds (Section~\ref{sec:deptable}, row B-S.D4).

\subsubsection{(B-C) Compactness}\label{sec:BC}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:L015}
Assume (H-U). Then every sequence $\varepsilon_m\to0$ has a subsequence along which $S_{n,\varepsilon}(F)$ converges for every $n$ and every $F\in\mathcal D_n$. The limit $S_n$ is linear and satisfies $|S_n(F)|\le\sigma_n\|F\|_{sn}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
1. Separability. $C^{sn}(T^{4n})$ with $\|\cdot\|_{sn}$ is separable (trigonometric polynomials with rational coefficients are dense; Fej\'er means converge in $C^k$). A subset of a separable metric space is separable. So $\mathcal D_n$ has a countable subset $Q_n$ dense for $\|\cdot\|_{sn}$.
2. Diagonal extraction. $Q:=\bigcup_nQ_n\times\{n\}$ is countable; enumerate it as $(F^{(1)},n_1),(F^{(2)},n_2),\dots$. Each sequence $S_{n_t,\varepsilon_m}(F^{(t)})$ is bounded by $\sigma_{n_t}\|F^{(t)}\|_{sn_t}$ (H-U), so it has a convergent subsequence. Extract successively and take the diagonal subsequence $\varepsilon_i$.
3. Extension. Let $F\in\mathcal D_n$, $\eta>0$, and $G\in Q_n$ with $\|F-G\|_{sn}\le\eta$. By (H-U) and linearity, $|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F)-S_{n,\varepsilon_j}(F)|\le2\sigma_n\eta+|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(G)-S_{n,\varepsilon_j}(G)|$. The last term $\to0$. So the sequence is Cauchy; call the limit $S_n(F)$.
4. Linearity passes to pointwise limits, and $|S_n(F)|=\lim|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F)|\le\sigma_n\|F\|_{sn}$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
(i) The same subsequence serves all $n$; (OS2) needs this. (ii) The proof does not use E13. (iii) The bounded normalizations $B_K$ and $c_\varepsilon$ can be included in the extraction.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{(B-REG) Regularity (OS0)}\label{sec:BREG}
By step 4 of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:L015} the limit satisfies $|S_n(F)|\le\sigma_n\|F\|_{sn}$, $\sigma_n=A(n!)^D$. On the compact torus the norm $\|F\|_p$ replaces the Schwartz seminorm: the weights of the seminorm control decay at infinity on $\mathbb R^{dn}$, and the torus has no infinity. This is the quoted OS II form. Distribution property: for $F\in C_c^\infty$ of the off-diagonal open set, $F\in\mathcal D_n$, and the bound is continuity in the $C^{sn}$ topology. So $S_n$ is a distribution of order $\le sn$ there.

OS II is formulated on $\mathbb R^{dn}$. On $T^{4n}$ we use the axioms in their torus form (periodic test functions). This is a change of setting, not a consequence of OS II.

\subsubsection{(B-TI) Translation and hypercubic invariance (OS1)}\label{sec:BTI}
\emph{Lattice, exact.} Put the origin at a lattice site.
\begin{itemize}
\item Translations $a\in\varepsilon\mathbb Z^4$. The map $U\mapsto U^a$, $U^a(b):=U(b-a)$, permutes the links, preserves the product Haar measure and the Wilson action, and $P_p(U^a)=P_{p-a}(U)$. So $\langle\prod_i\varphi_{p_i+a}\rangle_\varepsilon=\langle\prod_i\varphi_{p_i}\rangle_\varepsilon$ and $S_{n,\varepsilon}(F\circ\tau_a^{\times n})=S_{n,\varepsilon}(F)$. The coupling $g_0(\varepsilon)$ is one number, so nothing in $\mu_\varepsilon$ depends on position.
\item Hypercubic $R\in B_4$ (signed permutation matrices, $|B_4|=384$). $R$ maps sites to sites, links to links (possibly reversed), plaquettes to plaquettes, and $x_{Rp}=Rx_p$. Define $(RU)(b):=U(R^{-1}b)$ with $U(-b)=U(b)^{-1}$. Haar measure is invariant under $U\mapsto U^{-1}$ and $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}W^{-1}=\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}W$ for $W\in SU(2)$, so $A$ and the measure are invariant and $P_{Rp}(RU)=P_p(U)$. Hence $S_{n,\varepsilon}(F\circ R^{\times n})=S_{n,\varepsilon}(F)$.
\item These identities hold at every $\varepsilon$. $F\circ\tau_a^{\times n}$ and $F\circ R^{\times n}$ are again in $\mathcal D_n$. They pass to the limit along the subsequence of Proposition~\ref{prop:L015}: $S_n(F\circ R^{\times n})=S_n(F)$, and $S_n(F\circ\tau_a^{\times n})=S_n(F)$ for $a\in\varepsilon_i\mathbb Z^4$ for all large $i$.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Continuous translations.} Let $a\in T^4_\ell$. Pick $a_i\in\varepsilon_i\mathbb Z^4$ with $|a-a_i|\le\varepsilon_i$ (componentwise rounding gives $\le\varepsilon_i\sqrt4/2=\varepsilon_i$). Write $F_a:=F\circ\tau_a^{\times n}$. The mean value theorem along the diagonal direction gives $\|F_a-F_{a_i}\|_{sn}\le4n|a-a_i|\,\|F\|_{sn+1}$. Then
\[|S_n(F_a)-S_n(F)|\le|S_n(F_a)-S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F_a)|+|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F_a)-S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F_{a_i})|+|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F_{a_i})-S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F)|+|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F)-S_n(F)|.\]
The first and last terms $\to0$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:L015}. The third is $0$ by lattice invariance. The second is $\le\sigma_n\cdot4n\,\varepsilon_i\|F\|_{sn+1}$ by (H-U) and linearity. Let $i\to\infty$. $F$ is smooth, so $\|F\|_{sn+1}<\infty$. Rotations outside $B_4$ are not available (Section~\ref{sec:notgiven}).

\subsubsection{(B-RP) Reflection positivity (OS2)}\label{sec:BRP}
\emph{Lattice theorem.} We use the theorem of Osterwalder and Seiler \cite{osterwalderseiler1978} in its usual form.\footnote{\label{corr:title}The reference list of Jaffe--Witten gives the title ``Gauge theories on the lattice''; the bibliography entry follows the title in Annals of Physics.} For the Wilson action with compact gauge group and any $\beta\ge0$, and $\theta$ the reflection in a lattice hyperplane that bisects the links orthogonal to it, $\langle\Theta(\Phi)\Phi\rangle\ge0$ for every function $\Phi$ of the link variables in the positive half-lattice, where $(\Theta\Phi)(U):=\overline{\Phi(\theta U)}$. Jaffe--Witten \cite{jaffewitten} write: ``Reflection positivity holds for the Wilson approximation [36], a major advantage''. The template \cite{OAI26-O3} uses the same input: ``At every cutoff the nearest-neighbour measure is reflection positive across microscopic site and link seams.'' The standard proof fixes the crossing links to $1$ by a gauge transformation. The action then splits as $A_++\theta A_++\sum_{\text{crossing}}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(X_p\theta(X_p)^*)$, and $\exp(\beta\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(XY^*))$ is a kernel of positive type (a positive combination of characters), which gives a sum of squares. On the periodic lattice there are two fixed planes, $x_0=\varepsilon/2$ and $x_0=\varepsilon/2+\ell/2$. Both are link planes, because $N_\varepsilon$ is even ($\ell$ even, Section~\ref{sec:observables}). We attribute the periodic two-seam version to \cite{osterwalderseiler1978}, as is standard; we do not quote it.

\begin{remark}[parity of $\ell$]\label{corr:ell}\label{sec:corrections}
The hypothesis that $\ell$ is even is sufficient, not necessary. With $L$ odd, $N_\varepsilon=\ell L^K$ is even if and only if $\ell$ is even. For odd $N_\varepsilon$ every time reflection has one site seam and one link seam. Osterwalder and Seiler \cite{osterwalderseiler1978} treat link-plane (between-site) reflections. Menotti and Pelissetto \cite{menottipelissetto1987} treat site-plane reflections. Their abstract states: ``We extend the proof by Osterwalder and Seiler of reflection positivity of lattice gauge theories with fermions to the case in which the reflection is performed with respect to planes containing sites.'' and ``Our proof applies to all observables and relies on the particular structure of the Wilson action.'' The mixed case (one site seam and one link seam on a periodic lattice) is very likely routine, because each mechanism is local to its seam, but we know no reference that states it. So odd $\ell$ is not excluded; the hypothesis $\ell$ even only avoids the mixed case.
\end{remark}

\emph{Passage to the limit.} We assume (H-U) and the lattice theorem.
Setup. $\theta_\varepsilon(x):=\theta(x)+\varepsilon e_0=\theta(x-\varepsilon e_0)$, with fixed planes $x_0=\varepsilon/2,\ \varepsilon/2+\ell/2$. The positive half $\Lambda_+$ consists of the links with both ends in $\{x_0\in\{\varepsilon,2\varepsilon,\dots,\ell/2\}\}$. Fix $F_0\in\mathbb C$ and $F_n\in\mathcal D_n$, $\operatorname{supp}F_n\subset T_+^n$, $1\le n\le N$. The supports are compact in the open set, so there is $\delta>0$ with $x_0\in[\delta,\ell/2-\delta]$ on all supports.

Step 1 (lattice form). For $\varepsilon<\delta/2$ every plaquette with centre in a support has all four links in $\Lambda_+$. Define $\Phi:=F_0+\sum_{m=1}^N\sum_{p_1\dots p_m}F_m(x_{p_1},\dots,x_{p_m})\prod_i\varphi_{p_i}$. $\Phi$ depends only on the links of $\Lambda_+$. Since $\varphi_p$ is real and $P_p(\theta_\varepsilon U)=P_{\theta_\varepsilon p}(U)$, re-indexing gives $\Theta\Phi=\bar F_0+\sum_n\sum_q\overline{F_n}(\theta_\varepsilon x_{q_1},\dots,\theta_\varepsilon x_{q_n})\prod\varphi_{q_i}$. Hence
\[0\le\langle\Theta\Phi\,\Phi\rangle_\varepsilon=\sum_{n,m=0}^NS_{n+m,\varepsilon}(\Theta_\varepsilon F_n\otimes F_m),\quad\Theta_\varepsilon F_n:=(\Theta F_n)\circ\tau_{-\varepsilon e_0}^{\times n},\ S_{0,\varepsilon}:=1.\]
(The argument order inside $\Theta F_n$ does not matter, by (OS3).) Each $\Theta_\varepsilon F_n\otimes F_m$ lies in $\mathcal D_{n+m}$: its two factors have supports at distance $\ge2\delta-\varepsilon>0$, and each factor is flat on its own diagonals.

Step 2 (seam shift). $\|\Theta_\varepsilon F_n\otimes F_m-\Theta F_n\otimes F_m\|_{s(n+m)}\le2^{s(n+m)}\cdot4n\,\varepsilon\,\|F_n\|_{s(n+m)+1}\|F_m\|_{s(n+m)}$. By (H-U) the difference of the $S_{n+m,\varepsilon}$ values is $\le\sigma_{n+m}$ times this, which $\to0$.

Step 3 (limit). Along the subsequence of Proposition~\ref{prop:L015}, $S_{n+m,\varepsilon_i}(\Theta F_n\otimes F_m)\to S_{n+m}(\Theta F_n\otimes F_m)$ for each of the finitely many $(n,m)$. So $\sum_{n,m}S_{n+m}(\Theta F_n\otimes F_m)=\lim_i\sum_{n,m}S_{n+m,\varepsilon_i}(\Theta_{\varepsilon_i}F_n\otimes F_m)\ge0$.

\begin{lemma}[positivity of a finite form survives pointwise limits]\label{lem:L016}
Let $a^{(i)}\in\mathbb C^{(N+1)\times(N+1)}$ with $\sum_{n,m}\bar c_nc_ma^{(i)}_{nm}\ge0$ for all $c$ and all $i$, and $a^{(i)}_{nm}\to a_{nm}$ for every $(n,m)$. Then $\sum\bar c_nc_ma_{nm}\ge0$ for all $c$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $c$ the left side is a finite linear combination of the entries, so it converges to $\sum\bar c_nc_ma_{nm}$; $[0,\infty)$ is closed.
\end{proof}
Step 3 is this lemma with $a^{(i)}_{nm}:=S_{n+m,\varepsilon_i}(\Theta_{\varepsilon_i}F_n\otimes F_m)$ (replace $F_n$ by $c_nF_n$), together with Step 2.

\subsubsection{(OS3) Symmetry and reality}\label{sec:OS3}
$S_{n,\varepsilon}(F\circ\pi)=S_{n,\varepsilon}(F)$: the $\varphi_p$ are commuting real random variables; re-index the sum. For real $F$, $S_{n,\varepsilon}(F)\in\mathbb R$. Both identities are linear and pass to the limit. $S_0=1$ by definition.

\subsubsection{(B-NT) Nontriviality: what is needed}\label{sec:BNT}
\begin{itemize}
\item A two-point function alone never excludes a Gaussian: every positive-definite $S_2$ is the covariance of some Gaussian generalized field.
\item The plaquette density is a composite ($\approx\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}F^2\times\varepsilon^4$). Even in the free theory its connected $n\ge3$ functions are nonzero (Wick squares). So $S_n^c\ne0$ for some $n\ge3$ does not prove interaction.
\item The meaningful test excludes (a) the zero theory and (b) the free Maxwell composite. The free composite has, at short distance, the scale-invariant form $c_0d^{-8}$. Asymptotic freedom predicts $c\,g(d)^4d^{-8}$ with a running coupling.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Free-theory prediction along this sequence.} At tree level with the bare coupling, $\langle\varphi_p\varphi_{p'}\rangle\approx c\,\varepsilon^8g_0^4|x-y|^{-8}$. Since $g_0(\varepsilon)^2\le(1/g^2+\beta\log\varepsilon^{-1})^{-1}\to0$ by (0.31), the bare-coupling prediction for $S_2$ at fixed separation tends to $0$. The claim is that the true $S_2$ does not.

\begin{hypothesis}[(NT), the fixed-layer expansion (E14-f)]
There are $c>0$ and $C$ such that for all $K$, all $f_1,f_2\in C^\infty(T^4)$ with disjoint supports at distance $\ge d$, $d\ge L\varepsilon$, and $k(d)$ the layer with $L^k\varepsilon\le d<L^{k+1}\varepsilon$:
\[S_{2,\varepsilon}(f_1\otimes f_2)=c\iint f_1(x)f_2(y)\,g_{k(|x-y|)}^4|x-y|^{-8}\,dx\,dy\,(1+R),\quad|R|\le C\bigl(g_{k(d)}^2+(d/\ell)^2+(\varepsilon/d)^2\bigr).\]
\end{hypothesis}
(NT) is a corollary of (E14-a) at a fixed layer, which (E14-f) would supply. The term $(\varepsilon/d)^2$ accounts for the lattice artifacts: the lattice ring differs from the continuum ring by a term of this size. Equivalently one may drop this term and require $K\ge K_0(d)$. The coefficient $c$ is the tree-level coefficient in the composite, $c\propto\sum(\text{free field-strength covariance})^2$, a sum of squares, so $c>0$. In Ba{\l}aban's normalization, with unoriented plaquettes, $c=9/\pi^4$.

\emph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:L014} from (NT) and (B-T2).}
(i) Take $f_1,f_2\ge0$, not identically $0$, with supports at distance $\ge d_0$ and with support diameters small compared with $\ell$. Then $g^2+(|x-y|/\ell)^2$ is small on the whole support pair, and $|R|\le\tfrac12$ for $\varepsilon$ small. By (0.31) at layer $k(d)$ with $d\in[d_0,\ell]$: $1/g_k^2\le1/g^2+\beta'\log(L^k\varepsilon)^{-1}\le1/g^2+\beta'\log(L/d_0)$, uniformly in $\varepsilon$. So $S_{2,\varepsilon}(f_1\otimes f_2)\ge\tfrac12c(1/g^2+\beta'\log(L/d_0))^{-2}\iint f_1f_2|x-y|^{-8}>0$ uniformly in small $\varepsilon$, and the limit has the same bound.
(ii) For bumps concentrated at separation $d\to0$ (with $d$ a fixed multiple of their width), $d^8S_2\le(1+Cg_{k(d)}^2)\,c\,(1/g^2+\beta\log(1/d))^{-2}\to0$ as $d\to0$ by the lower bound in (0.31), while $d^8S_2>0$ by (i). A scale-invariant composite would give a nonzero constant limit. So the limit is neither zero nor the free composite. The proof uses only the two-sided bound (0.31). \qed

\begin{remark}
Part (ii) needs (NT) uniformly down to $d\ge L\varepsilon$, with the remainder controlled by $g_{k(d)}^2$; that is, the fixed-layer expansion at every layer. If (E14-f) holds only at a fixed layer $j$, then (i) holds, and (ii) holds only in the weaker form ``$d^8S_2(d)$ lies between the two (0.31) envelopes for $d\in[L^{-j},1]$''.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What the theorem does not give}\label{sec:notgiven}
Each item needs a separate argument that this paper does not give.
\begin{itemize}
\item Rotation invariance. Only $B_4$ and the torus translations are proved. Full $O(4)$ is not a symmetry of $T^4_\ell$. On the torus the inclined comparison gives only the tilted-axis reflection symmetry $R_{2\theta}s$, never a rotation. The template \cite{OAI26-O3} reaches rotations only after the limit torus $\to$ plane, which it controls with its mass gap.
\item Infinite volume. $\ell$ is fixed. Section~\ref{sec:infvol} records one conditional statement for a joint limit, Theorem IV (L-196), outside this theorem. Jaffe and Witten tie the limit $T^4\to\mathbb R^4$ to a volume-uniform gap: ``New ideas are needed to prove the existence of a mass gap that is uniform in the volume of space-time. Such a result presumably would enable the study of the limit as $T^4\to\mathbb R^4$.'' We know no public mechanism without a gap. With the constants of this paper, a unit-scale cluster expansion uniform in $\ell$ would give exponential clustering at a rate of order one in unit-scale units, against the correlation length $\sim e^{1/(2b_0g^2)}$ that asymptotic freedom predicts. So such an expansion is expected to be impossible.
\item Mass gap. Not addressed. On a finite torus a ``gap'' of the transfer matrix is not the Clay mass gap.
\item Uniqueness. Different subsequences may give different Schwinger functions. Uniqueness needs E13, which Ba{\l}aban does not have. Uniqueness of the vacuum after reconstruction is also not given (no clustering on a torus).
\item\label{corr:thermal} Vacuum state. Time is periodic. So OS reconstruction from torus reflection positivity gives a transfer matrix on $T^3$ and a thermal state at inverse temperature $\ell$, not a vacuum state.
\item Also not given: clustering (OS4); a probability measure for the density field; Wilson-loop observables (class C3); the stress tensor and the operator product expansion (class C2 and beyond); independence of the limit from the block-averaging choices of Ba{\l}aban's RG (only the lattice measure enters $S_{n,\varepsilon}$, but $g_0(\varepsilon)$ is chosen through Ba{\l}aban's flow).
\item Status of the whole statement. Even with every block closed, the theorem is conditional on Ba{\l}aban's published 1984--89 proofs (GC1) (H-200). Under Jaffe--Witten footnote 2 it is a compactness (``weak-existence'') result on a torus with the torus forms of OS0--OS3. It is not a solution of the Millennium problem.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[OS4 for joint limits]\label{rem:GapU}
Theorem IV does not assert OS4. Its limits are joint limits $(K_i,\ell_i)\to(\infty,\infty)$. OS4 for such limits would need a hypothesis that is uniform in $K$, (Gap$_U$): for every pair of disjoint bumps $f_1,f_2$,
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\ \limsup_{K,\ell\to\infty}\ \sup_{R\le|a|\le\ell/4}\bigl|\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_{K,\ell}}(w_1,w_{2,a})\bigr|=0,
\]
where $\nu_{K,\ell}$ is the unit-scale probability measure on the torus of side $\ell$ at level $K$, and $w_1$, $w_{2,a}$ are the first-order responses to $f_1$ and to the translate $\tau_af_2$. The fixed-$K$ decay condition (Gap$_K$), even for every $K$, does not suffice for an arbitrary joint sequence. $K$-uniform (Gap$_K$) suffices for iterated joint limits ($\ell\to\infty$ at fixed $K$ first, then $K\to\infty$), and Theorem IV(a) can always be realized along such a sequence (proof sketch). (Gap$_U$) is the condition for every joint limit. This paper uses none of these statements.
\end{remark}
