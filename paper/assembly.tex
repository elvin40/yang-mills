\subsection{What ``exists'' means here}\label{sec:exists}
Jaffe--Witten footnote 2 \cite{jaffewitten} \status{verified quote}: ``We specifically exclude weak-existence (compactness) as the solution to the existence part of the Millennium problem, unless one also uses other techniques to establish properties of the limit (such as the existence of a mass gap and the axioms).''

Reading used here. ``Exists'' means: (i) the family $\{S_{n,\varepsilon}\}$ is relatively compact (uniform bounds); (ii) at least one limit point exists along a sequence $\varepsilon_k\to0$; (iii) every limit point has the properties (OS0)--(OS3) below, proved on the limit, not assumed. No uniqueness is claimed. Full-sequence convergence needs E13 (comparison of two trajectories; \file{SOURCES.md} (E14-d) (H-099), D6), which is not in this paper.

This reading is weaker than the footnote's parenthesis. The theorem below gives no gap and only the torus versions of four axioms. It is the finite-torus step of Jaffe--Witten \S6.5 \status{verified quote}: ``One must then verify the existence of limits of appropriate expectations of gauge-invariant observables as the lattice spacing tends to zero and as the volume tends to infinity'' (only the first limit is done here).

The problem description \cite{jaffewitten} does not state the OS axioms. It cites them as OS (CMP 31 (1973), CMP 42 (1975)) \cite{os1973,os1975} and requires ``axiomatic properties at least as strong as those cited'' \status{verified quote}. \subsection{Hypothesis delivered by the blocks}\label{sec:HU}
\begin{definition}[Uniform bound (H-U), H-116]\label{def:H116}
There are $s\in\mathbb N$ and $A,D<\infty$ such that for all $K\ge K_0$, all $n\ge1$, and all $F\in\mathcal D_n$:
\[|S_{n,\varepsilon}(F)|\le\sigma_n\|F\|_{sn},\qquad\sigma_n:=A(n!)^D,\quad\varepsilon=L^{-K}.\]
\end{definition}
The derivative order $sn$ grows linearly in $n$. The OS linear growth condition admits this. (H-U) (H-116) is the output of blocks (B-T2) (H-084) + (B-S) (ledger row R-035) + the bridge (B-N1) (ledger row R-045) of Section~\ref{sec:blocks}.

\subsection{Theorem (finite-torus existence, compactness form)}\label{sec:existthm}
\begin{theorem}[finite-torus existence, compactness form \status{proved modulo named blocks}]\label{thm:L013}
Let $G=SU(2)$, $d=4$, $L$ odd and large, $\ell\in2\mathbb N$ fixed. Let $\gamma>0$ be Balaban's small constant and $g\in\,]0,\gamma]$ small. For $K\ge K_0$ let $\varepsilon=L^{-K}$ and $g_0=g_0(\varepsilon,g)$ be a shooting solution (Lemma~\ref{lem:L005}) whose effective couplings satisfy $g_k\in\,]0,\gamma]$ for $0\le k\le K$ and $g_K=g$. Assume (H-U) (H-116). When (H-U) is taken from the blocks (Section~\ref{sec:HU}) rather than assumed, the chain also assumes the $\kappa\to\kappa/2$ decay recovery of D2 [R, R-046] (not stated, \file{SOURCES_D2_VERIFY.md} (5)), which does not follow from the other hypotheses. Then:

(a) Compactness. Every sequence $K_m\to\infty$ has a subsequence $K_{m_i}$ such that, with $\varepsilon_i=L^{-K_{m_i}}$, the limit $S_n(F):=\lim_iS_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F)$ exists for every $n\ge1$ and every $F\in\mathcal D_n$.

(b) For every limit family $(S_n)_{n\ge1}$ obtained in (a):
\begin{itemize}
\item (OS0) Regularity. $S_n$ is linear on $\mathcal D_n$, and $|S_n(F)|\le A(n!)^D\|F\|_{sn}$. In particular $S_n$ restricted to $C_c^\infty(T^{4n}\setminus\bigcup_{i<j}\{x_i=x_j\})$ is a distribution of order $\le sn$.
\item (OS1) Invariance. $S_n(F\circ\tau_a^{\times n})=S_n(F)$ for every $a\in T^4_\ell$ (all continuous translations), and $S_n(F\circ R^{\times n})=S_n(F)$ for every $R$ in the hypercubic group $B_4$ (signed permutations of the axes, acting about a lattice site).
\item (OS2) Reflection positivity. Let $\theta(x_0,\vec x)=(-x_0,\vec x)$ on $T^4_\ell$ and $T_+:=\{x:x_0\in\,]0,\ell/2[\}$. For every finite family $F_0\in\mathbb C$, $F_n\in\mathcal D_n$ with $\operatorname{supp}F_n\subset T_+^n$ ($1\le n\le N$):
\[\sum_{n,m=0}^NS_{n+m}(\Theta F_n\otimes F_m)\ge0,\quad(\Theta F_n)(x_1,\dots,x_n):=\overline{F_n(\theta x_n,\dots,\theta x_1)},\quad S_0:=1.\]
\item (OS3) Symmetry and reality. $S_n(F\circ\pi)=S_n(F)$ for every permutation $\pi$ of the $n$ arguments, and $S_n(F)\in\mathbb R$ for real $F$.
\end{itemize}

(c) Nontriviality is a separate statement, Theorem~\ref{thm:L014}.
\end{theorem}
The sourced blocks use the unit-scale condition (U) (K-024), $\rho_1\le c_*r_0/M^a$ at $\lambda_K=1$ ($r_0$ the Cauchy margin of the constant-source family, \file{SOURCES_D2C.md} (3.5)), as a constant choice (K-024), made once after $r_0$ and before $K$; B-N1 meets it by the rescaling $\lambda_i=2N_U(f_i)$ at a $C^n$ cost, $s$ and $D$ unchanged (\file{SOURCES_UN1.md}, \file{SOURCES_UN1_VERIFY.md}). This (U) is not the Route B hypothesis (U).
\emph{What is claimed about measures.} No probability measure is claimed. The limit is a family of Schwinger functions on $\mathcal D_n$. A measure on distributions would need finite moments of smeared fields, and $\operatorname{Var}\sum_pf(x_p)\varphi_p\sim\varepsilon^{-4}$ (Section~\ref{sec:observables}). (OS0)--(OS3) on $\mathcal D_n$ are the inputs of OS reconstruction.

\subsubsection{(B-N1) Bridge: source-form bounds $\Rightarrow$ (H-U) \status{derived given (S); the (E14-c) clause reduced to (S), verified}}\label{sec:BN1}
\file{SOURCES_VERIFY} item 5(b) \status{verified quote}: the source form ``does not give $|S_n(F)|\le C^nn!\|F\|_{s,n}$ for $F$ flat on diagonals with a fixed $s$ \dots\ Mark N1 for general $F$ as a gap.'' This paper does not close the gap. It gives a route \status{derived sketch}, which shows that the gap is removed by the OS index growth $sn$.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Moments from cumulants. $\langle\prod_i\phi(f_i)\rangle=\sum_{\text{partitions}}\prod_{\text{blocks}}\kappa(\cdot)$. Singleton cumulants vanish ($\varphi$ is centred). For disjoint supports the source form bounds every cumulant by $C^{|B|}|B|!\,r^{-|B|}$, so the moment by $C^nn!\,r^{-n}$.
\item Scale-$\delta$ tests. A bump of side $\delta\le1$ and amplitude $a$ has $|\partial^5f|\sim a\delta^{-5}$; it lies in $F_r$ if $a\le cr\delta^5$. So $n$ disjoint unit-amplitude scale-$\delta$ bumps have $|\text{moment}|\le C^nn!\,\delta^{-5n}$. This is lossy but polynomial in $\delta^{-1}$.
\item Whitney decomposition. Split $T^{4n}$ by a smooth dyadic partition in $\rho(x):=\min_{i<j}|x_i-x_j|\in[\delta,2\delta]$, $\delta=2^{-m}\ge\varepsilon$. On each piece cover by cubes of side $\delta/100$; cube tuples are pairwise disjoint. Expand $F\cdot$(cutoffs) in a Fourier series on each cube tuple; the coefficients decay faster than any power; each term is a product of disjointly supported scale-$\delta$ bumps. Flatness gives $|\partial^\beta F(x)|\le C_k\|F\|_{k+|\beta|}\rho(x)^k/k!$. The count is $\le C^n\delta^{-4n}$ cube tuples. Derivatives of the cutoffs cost at most $(Cn^2/\delta)^M$; with $M=O(n)$ this is $\le C^n(n!)^c\delta^{-M}$.
\item Bound per dyadic scale: $\le C^n(n!)^{D'}\delta^{k-9n-M}\|F\|_{k+M}$. Choose $k=sn-M$ with $s$ large enough ($s\ge20$ suffices on this count) so the $\delta$-exponent is $\ge n$; the sum over dyadic $\delta\in[\varepsilon,1]$ converges uniformly in $\varepsilon$.
\item Sub-lattice region $\rho(x)\lesssim\varepsilon$: $\le C^n\varepsilon^{-4n}$ plaquette tuples, each moment $\le2^n$, and $|F|\le\|F\|_k\varepsilon^k$; this is $\le C^n\|F\|_{sn}$ for $k=sn\ge4n$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{(B-C) Compactness \status{proved here, given (H-U)}}\label{sec:BC}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:L015}
Under (H-U), every sequence $\varepsilon_m\to0$ has a subsequence along which $S_{n,\varepsilon}(F)$ converges for every $n$ and every $F\in\mathcal D_n$. The limit $S_n$ is linear and satisfies $|S_n(F)|\le\sigma_n\|F\|_{sn}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
1. Separability. $C^{sn}(T^{4n})$ with $\|\cdot\|_{sn}$ is separable (trigonometric polynomials with rational coefficients are dense; Fej\'er means converge in $C^k$). A subset of a separable metric space is separable. So $\mathcal D_n$ has a countable subset $Q_n$ dense for $\|\cdot\|_{sn}$.
2. Diagonal extraction. $Q:=\bigcup_nQ_n\times\{n\}$ is countable; enumerate it as $(F^{(1)},n_1),(F^{(2)},n_2),\dots$. Each sequence $S_{n_t,\varepsilon_m}(F^{(t)})$ is bounded by $\sigma_{n_t}\|F^{(t)}\|_{sn_t}$ (H-U), so it has a convergent subsequence. Extract successively and take the diagonal subsequence $\varepsilon_i$.
3. Extension. Let $F\in\mathcal D_n$, $\eta>0$, and $G\in Q_n$ with $\|F-G\|_{sn}\le\eta$. By (H-U) and linearity, $|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F)-S_{n,\varepsilon_j}(F)|\le2\sigma_n\eta+|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(G)-S_{n,\varepsilon_j}(G)|$. The last term $\to0$. So the sequence is Cauchy; call the limit $S_n(F)$.
4. Linearity passes to pointwise limits, and $|S_n(F)|=\lim|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F)|\le\sigma_n\|F\|_{sn}$.
\end{proof}
Remarks. (i) The same subsequence serves all $n$; this matters for (OS2). (ii) Nothing here needs E13. (iii) The bounded normalizations $B_K$ and $c_\varepsilon$ can be included in the extraction.

\subsubsection{(B-REG) Regularity (OS0) \status{proved here, given (H-U)}}\label{sec:BREG}
By step 4 of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:L015} the limit satisfies $|S_n(F)|\le\sigma_n\|F\|_{sn}$, $\sigma_n=A(n!)^D$. On the compact torus the Schwartz seminorm is replaced by $\|F\|_p$ (the weights control decay at infinity on $\mathbb R^{dn}$; the torus has none). This is the quoted OS II form. Distribution property: for $F\in C_c^\infty$ of the off-diagonal open set, $F\in\mathcal D_n$ and the bound is continuity in the $C^{sn}$ topology, so $S_n$ is a distribution of order $\le sn$ there.

Caveat. OS II is formulated on $\mathbb R^{dn}$. On $T^{4n}$ the axioms are used in their torus form (periodic test functions). This is a change of setting, not a consequence of OS II.

\subsubsection{(B-TI) Translation and hypercubic invariance (OS1) \status{proved here, given (H-U)}}\label{sec:BTI}
\emph{Lattice, exact.} Put the origin at a lattice site.
\begin{itemize}
\item Translations $a\in\varepsilon\mathbb Z^4$. The map $U\mapsto U^a$, $U^a(b):=U(b-a)$, permutes the links, preserves the product Haar measure and the Wilson action, and $P_p(U^a)=P_{p-a}(U)$. So $\langle\prod_i\varphi_{p_i+a}\rangle_\varepsilon=\langle\prod_i\varphi_{p_i}\rangle_\varepsilon$ and $S_{n,\varepsilon}(F\circ\tau_a^{\times n})=S_{n,\varepsilon}(F)$. The coupling $g_0(\varepsilon)$ is one number, so nothing in $\mu_\varepsilon$ depends on position.
\item Hypercubic $R\in B_4$ (signed permutation matrices, $|B_4|=384$). $R$ maps sites to sites, links to links (possibly reversed), plaquettes to plaquettes, and $x_{Rp}=Rx_p$. Define $(RU)(b):=U(R^{-1}b)$ with $U(-b)=U(b)^{-1}$. Haar measure is invariant under $U\mapsto U^{-1}$ and $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}W^{-1}=\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}W$ for $W\in SU(2)$, so $A$ and the measure are invariant and $P_{Rp}(RU)=P_p(U)$. Hence $S_{n,\varepsilon}(F\circ R^{\times n})=S_{n,\varepsilon}(F)$.
\item These identities hold at every $\varepsilon$. $F\circ\tau_a^{\times n}$ and $F\circ R^{\times n}$ are again in $\mathcal D_n$. They pass to the limit along the subsequence of Proposition~\ref{prop:L015}: $S_n(F\circ R^{\times n})=S_n(F)$, and $S_n(F\circ\tau_a^{\times n})=S_n(F)$ for $a\in\varepsilon_i\mathbb Z^4$ for all large $i$.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Continuous translations.} Let $a\in T^4_\ell$. Pick $a_i\in\varepsilon_i\mathbb Z^4$ with $|a-a_i|\le\varepsilon_i$ (componentwise rounding gives $\le\varepsilon_i\sqrt4/2=\varepsilon_i$). Write $F_a:=F\circ\tau_a^{\times n}$. The mean value theorem along the diagonal direction gives $\|F_a-F_{a_i}\|_{sn}\le4n|a-a_i|\,\|F\|_{sn+1}$. Then
\[|S_n(F_a)-S_n(F)|\le|S_n(F_a)-S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F_a)|+|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F_a)-S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F_{a_i})|+|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F_{a_i})-S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F)|+|S_{n,\varepsilon_i}(F)-S_n(F)|.\]
The first and last terms $\to0$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:L015}. The third is $0$ by lattice invariance. The second is $\le\sigma_n\cdot4n\,\varepsilon_i\|F\|_{sn+1}$ by (H-U) and linearity. Let $i\to\infty$. $F$ is smooth, so $\|F\|_{sn+1}<\infty$. Rotations outside $B_4$ are not available (Section~\ref{sec:notgiven}).

\subsubsection{(B-RP) Reflection positivity (OS2)}\label{sec:BRP}
\emph{Lattice theorem} \status{cited}: Osterwalder--Seiler \cite{osterwalderseiler1978}, Ann.\ Phys.\ 110 (1978) 440--471. The paper is not in the project folder; no verbatim quote. Content as usually stated: for the Wilson action with compact gauge group and any $\beta\ge0$, and $\theta$ the reflection in a lattice hyperplane that bisects the links orthogonal to it, $\langle\Theta(\Phi)\Phi\rangle\ge0$ for every function $\Phi$ of the link variables in the positive half-lattice, $(\Theta\Phi)(U):=\overline{\Phi(\theta U)}$. Jaffe--Witten \cite{jaffewitten} line 546 \status{verified quote}: ``Reflection positivity holds for the Wilson approximation [36], a major advantage''. The template uses the same input \cite{OAI26-O3} \status{verified quote}: ``At every cutoff the nearest-neighbour measure is reflection positive across microscopic site and link seams.'' Proof idea (standard): fix the crossing links to $1$ by a gauge transformation; the action splits as $A_++\theta A_++\sum_{\text{crossing}}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(X_p\theta(X_p)^*)$, and $\exp(\beta\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(XY^*))$ is a positive-type kernel (positive combination of characters), which gives a sum of squares. On the periodic lattice there are two fixed planes, $x_0=\varepsilon/2$ and $x_0=\varepsilon/2+\ell/2$; both are link planes because $N_\varepsilon$ is even ($\ell$ even, Section~\ref{sec:observables}). The periodic two-seam version is standard \status{cited}; its text was not checked. For site-plane reflections see Menotti--Pelissetto \cite{menottipelissetto1987} (Section~\ref{sec:corrections}, item~\ref{corr:ell}).

\emph{Passage to the limit} \status{proved here, given (H-U) and the lattice theorem}.
Setup. $\theta_\varepsilon(x):=\theta(x)+\varepsilon e_0=\theta(x-\varepsilon e_0)$, fixed planes $x_0=\varepsilon/2,\ \varepsilon/2+\ell/2$. The positive half $\Lambda_+$ consists of links with both ends in $\{x_0\in\{\varepsilon,2\varepsilon,\dots,\ell/2\}\}$. Fix $F_0\in\mathbb C$ and $F_n\in\mathcal D_n$, $\operatorname{supp}F_n\subset T_+^n$, $1\le n\le N$. Supports are compact in the open set, so there is $\delta>0$ with $x_0\in[\delta,\ell/2-\delta]$ on all supports.

Step 1 (lattice form). For $\varepsilon<\delta/2$ every plaquette with centre in a support has all four links in $\Lambda_+$. Define $\Phi:=F_0+\sum_{m=1}^N\sum_{p_1\dots p_m}F_m(x_{p_1},\dots,x_{p_m})\prod_i\varphi_{p_i}$. $\Phi$ depends only on $\Lambda_+$ links. Since $\varphi_p$ is real and $P_p(\theta_\varepsilon U)=P_{\theta_\varepsilon p}(U)$, re-indexing gives $\Theta\Phi=\bar F_0+\sum_n\sum_q\overline{F_n}(\theta_\varepsilon x_{q_1},\dots,\theta_\varepsilon x_{q_n})\prod\varphi_{q_i}$. Hence
\[0\le\langle\Theta\Phi\,\Phi\rangle_\varepsilon=\sum_{n,m=0}^NS_{n+m,\varepsilon}(\Theta_\varepsilon F_n\otimes F_m),\quad\Theta_\varepsilon F_n:=(\Theta F_n)\circ\tau_{-\varepsilon e_0}^{\times n},\ S_{0,\varepsilon}:=1.\]
(Argument order inside $\Theta F_n$ is immaterial by (OS3).) Each $\Theta_\varepsilon F_n\otimes F_m$ lies in $\mathcal D_{n+m}$: its two factors have supports at positive distance and each factor is flat on its own diagonals.

Step 2 (seam shift). $\|\Theta_\varepsilon F_n\otimes F_m-\Theta F_n\otimes F_m\|_{s(n+m)}\le2^{s(n+m)}\cdot4n\,\varepsilon\,\|F_n\|_{s(n+m)+1}\|F_m\|_{s(n+m)}$. By (H-U) the difference of the $S_{n+m,\varepsilon}$ values is $\le\sigma_{n+m}$ times this, which $\to0$.

Step 3 (limit). Along the subsequence of Proposition~\ref{prop:L015}, $S_{n+m,\varepsilon_i}(\Theta F_n\otimes F_m)\to S_{n+m}(\Theta F_n\otimes F_m)$ for each of the finitely many $(n,m)$. So $\sum_{n,m}S_{n+m}(\Theta F_n\otimes F_m)=\lim_i\sum_{n,m}S_{n+m,\varepsilon_i}(\Theta_{\varepsilon_i}F_n\otimes F_m)\ge0$.

\begin{lemma}[positivity of a finite form survives pointwise limits \status{proved here}]\label{lem:L016}
Let $a^{(i)}\in\mathbb C^{(N+1)\times(N+1)}$ with $\sum_{n,m}\bar c_nc_ma^{(i)}_{nm}\ge0$ for all $c$ and all $i$, and $a^{(i)}_{nm}\to a_{nm}$ for every $(n,m)$. Then $\sum\bar c_nc_ma_{nm}\ge0$ for all $c$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $c$ the left side is a finite linear combination of the entries, so it converges to $\sum\bar c_nc_ma_{nm}$; $[0,\infty)$ is closed.
\end{proof}
Step 3 is this lemma with $a^{(i)}_{nm}:=S_{n+m,\varepsilon_i}(\Theta_{\varepsilon_i}F_n\otimes F_m)$ (replace $F_n$ by $c_nF_n$), plus Step 2.

\subsubsection{(B-NT) Nontriviality: what is needed}\label{sec:BNT}
\begin{itemize}
\item A two-point function alone never excludes a Gaussian: every positive-definite $S_2$ is the covariance of some Gaussian generalized field.
\item The plaquette density is a composite ($\approx\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}F^2\times\varepsilon^4$). Even in the free theory its connected $n\ge3$ functions are nonzero (Wick squares). So $S_n^c\ne0$ for some $n\ge3$ does not prove interaction.
\item The meaningful test excludes (a) the zero theory and (b) the free Maxwell composite. The free composite has, at short distance, the scale-invariant form $c_0d^{-8}$. Asymptotic freedom predicts $c\,g(d)^4d^{-8}$ with a running coupling.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Free-theory prediction along this sequence.} At tree level with the bare coupling, $\langle\varphi_p\varphi_{p'}\rangle\approx c\,\varepsilon^8g_0^4|x-y|^{-8}$. Since $g_0(\varepsilon)^2\le(1/g^2+\beta\log\varepsilon^{-1})^{-1}\to0$ by (0.31), the bare-coupling prediction for $S_2$ at fixed separation tends to $0$. The claim is that the true $S_2$ does not.

\emph{Statement (NT) needed} \status{hypothesis}; owned by (E14-f), a corollary of (E14-a) at a fixed layer. \emph{Derivation of Theorem~\ref{thm:L014} from (NT) and (B-T2)} \status{proved here, given (NT)}.
(i) Take $f_1,f_2\ge0$, not identically $0$, with supports at distance $\ge d_0$. \subsection{What the theorem does not give}\label{sec:notgiven}
Each item needs a separate argument that this paper does not give.
\begin{itemize}
\item Rotation invariance. Only $B_4$ and the torus translations are proved. Full $O(4)$ is not a symmetry of $T^4_\ell$. \file{LEMMAS.md} ledger item 5 \status{verified quote}: on the torus the inclined comparison ``gives only the tilted-axis reflection symmetry $R_{2\theta}s$, never a rotation''; the template reaches rotations only after the torus $\to$ plane limit, which it controls with its mass gap.
\item Infinite volume. $\ell$ is fixed. (Section~\ref{sec:infvol} records one conditional statement for a joint limit, Theorem IV (L-196), outside this theorem.) Jaffe--Witten tie the limit $T^4\to\mathbb R^4$ to a volume-uniform gap \status{verified quote}: ``New ideas are needed to prove the existence of a mass gap that is uniform in the volume of space-time. Such a result presumably would enable the study of the limit as $T^4\to\mathbb R^4$.'' No public mechanism without a gap is known to us (\file{LEMMAS.md} ledger item 8). Remark on scope: with the constants of this paper, a unit-scale cluster expansion uniform in $\ell$ would give exponential clustering at a rate of order one in unit-scale units, against the correlation length $\sim e^{1/(2b_0g^2)}$ expected from asymptotic freedom, so such an expansion is expected to be impossible (\file{INFINITE_VOLUME_IV12_VERIFY.md}).
\item Mass gap. Not addressed. On a finite torus a ``gap'' of the transfer matrix is not the Clay mass gap.
\item Uniqueness. Different subsequences may give different Schwinger functions. Uniqueness needs E13, which Balaban does not have. Also not given: uniqueness of the vacuum after reconstruction (no clustering on a torus).
\item Vacuum state. Time is periodic, so OS reconstruction from torus reflection positivity gives a transfer matrix on $T^3$ and a thermal state at inverse temperature $\ell$, not a vacuum state (Section~\ref{sec:corrections}, item~\ref{corr:thermal}).
\item Also not given: clustering (OS4); a probability measure for the density field; Wilson-loop observables (class C3); the stress tensor and the operator product expansion (class C2 and beyond); independence of the limit from the block-averaging choices of Balaban's RG (only the lattice measure enters $S_{n,\varepsilon}$, but $g_0(\varepsilon)$ is chosen through Balaban's flow).
\item Status of the whole statement. Even with every block closed, the theorem is conditional on Balaban's published 1984--89 proofs (GC1) (H-200). Under Jaffe--Witten footnote 2 it is a compactness (``weak-existence'') result on a torus with the torus forms of OS0--OS3; it is not a solution of the Millennium problem.
\end{itemize}

What separates Theorem IV (L-196) from an unconditional weak-existence statement is every open item of Theorem~\ref{thm:L013} (S1, the sub-items of R-039, R-042, (W4) (ledger row R-044), (Eps), (D)), the two readings I8 and (D) on $\tilde U^c$ of L-323 (its sourced form is written out in \file{FABLE_HARD.md} \S1.3--\S1.6 and verified relative to its inputs), a one-paragraph pointwise reading of Step A for L-320, and the (S$_\infty$) (H-169) bridge. \begin{remark}[OS4 for joint limits]\label{rem:GapU}
Theorem IV does not assert OS4. Its limits are joint limits $(K_i,\ell_i)\to(\infty,\infty)$. A derivation, \file{SOURCES_FH_U1_REPAIR.md} \S3.5, verified with one correction of scope by \file{FABLE_HARD_VERIFY.md} (\S C.2, \S D), states that OS4 for such limits would need a hypothesis that is uniform in $K$, (Gap$_U$): for every pair of disjoint bumps $f_1,f_2$,
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\ \limsup_{K,\ell\to\infty}\ \sup_{R\le|a|\le\ell/4}\bigl|\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_{K,\ell}}(w_1,w_{2,a})\bigr|=0,
\]
where $\nu_{K,\ell}$ is the unit-scale probability measure on the torus of side $\ell$ at level $K$, and $w_1$, $w_{2,a}$ are the first-order responses to $f_1$ and to the translate $\tau_af_2$. The same file states that the fixed-$K$ decay condition (Gap$_K$), even for every $K$, does not suffice for a joint limit. The verifier confirms this for an arbitrary joint sequence and corrects its scope: $K$-uniform (Gap$_K$) suffices for iterated joint limits ($\ell\to\infty$ at fixed $K$ first, then $K\to\infty$), and Theorem IV(a) can always be realized along such a sequence (\file{FABLE_HARD_VERIFY.md} \S C.2, \status{P-sketch}). (Gap$_U$) stays the condition for every joint limit. This paper uses none of these statements.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Corrections after verification}\label{sec:corrections}
The verifier confirmed the theorem statement, all in-chapter proofs (B-C, B-REG, B-TI, $B_4$, the RP passage, OS3, the finite-form lemma), the quotes of Jaffe--Witten, and the open status of B-N1. \begin{enumerate}
\item\label{corr:ell} \emph{$\ell$ even is sufficient, not necessary} (verifier \S5, grade 3). The parity count is right: $L$ odd gives $N_\varepsilon=\ell L^K$ even iff $\ell$ even; for odd $N_\varepsilon$ every time reflection has one site seam and one link seam. Osterwalder--Seiler (1978) \cite{osterwalderseiler1978} treat link-plane (between-site) reflections. Site-plane reflections were added by Menotti--Pelissetto, CMP 113 (1987) 369--373 \cite{menottipelissetto1987} \status{verified quote} (abstract, per the verifier): ``We extend the proof by Osterwalder and Seiler of reflection positivity of lattice gauge theories with fermions to the case in which the reflection is performed with respect to planes containing sites.'' and ``Our proof applies to all observables and relies on the particular structure of the Wilson action.'' The mixed case (one site seam, one link seam on a periodic lattice) is very likely routine, since each mechanism is local to its seam, but no citation states it. So odd $\ell$ is not excluded; the hypothesis $\ell$ even only avoids the mixed case. The source's phrase ``plausible but not cited'' stands. The periodic two-seam link version attributed to Osterwalder--Seiler is \status{cited}, not \status{verified quote}. Two minor points from the verifier: the support distance after the seam shift is $\ge2\delta-\varepsilon$ (the source's $2\delta-2\varepsilon$ is conservative); the factor $2^{s(n+m)}$ in Step 2 is unneeded but harmless.
\item\label{corr:title} \emph{Reference title.} The Jaffe--Witten reference list gives the title ``Gauge theories on the lattice''; the bibliography entry follows the Annals of Physics title.
\item\label{corr:nt} \emph{Nontriviality needs the two-sided envelope only and support diameters $\ll\ell$} (verifier \S6, grade 6). In Theorem~\ref{thm:L014}(i), $|R|\le\tfrac12$ needs $g^2+(|x-y|/\ell)^2$ small on the whole support pair, so the support diameters must be $\ll\ell$, not only the minimum distance $d_0$. The proof uses only the two-sided bound (0.31); the asymptotic needs $\beta_j\to\beta_\infty$ and is not proved. The statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:L014}(ii) above is the supported one. The overclaim was in the statement, not in the proof.
\item\label{corr:thermal} \emph{Periodic time gives a thermal state} (verifier \S6, grade 8). OS reconstruction from torus reflection positivity gives a transfer matrix on $T^3$ and a thermal state at inverse temperature $\ell$, not a vacuum. This remark is added to the list of non-results (Section~\ref{sec:notgiven}).
\end{enumerate}
