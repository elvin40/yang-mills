\section{The coupling flow and Theorem 2}\label{sec:flow}

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:L002}. Section~\ref{sec:needs} lists what Ba{\l}aban's proof of Theorem 1 uses of the coupling sequence; this is weaker than his (0.31). Section~\ref{sec:2w} states the weak form, Theorem 2w (L-001), that supplies exactly this. Sections~\ref{sec:shooting}--\ref{sec:H2} prove Theorem 2w from bounded window sums of the one-loop increments, give two routes to these window sums, and reduce the uniform part of both routes to the single inequality (H2) (R-025). Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} assembles the result. Its output, the trajectory conditions (b), (c-pair), K-018 (S), is the block (B-T2) (H-084) of Section~\ref{sec:torus}.

\subsection{What Ba{\l}aban's Theorem 1 uses}\label{sec:needs}

The statements of Theorems 1 and 2 are quoted in Section~\ref{sec:balaban}. The stated hypothesis of Theorem 1 is only $g_k\in\,]0,\gamma]$. The proof uses more. Its working form is \cite[pp.~255--256, (2.6)--(2.9)]{balaban119}, for scales $m<n$:
\begin{itemize}
\item (2.6) $g_n \le (1 + g_n^2\beta'(n-m))^{1/2} g_m$ and $g_m \le (1+\beta_0) g_n$;
\item (2.7) $(\log g_n^{-2})^p \le (1+\beta_0)(\log g_m^{-2})^p$ and $(\log g_m^{-2})^p \le (1 + g_n^2\beta'(n-m))^{\beta_0}(\log g_n^{-2})^p$;
\item (2.8), (2.9) the same for $\varepsilon_j = g_j p_0(g_j)$ and $R_j$.
\end{itemize}
Here $\beta_0$ is Ba{\l}aban's small constant ($1/7$ in \cite[p.~389]{balaban122b}), not a coupling.

\begin{remark}[a misnumbered reference]
Theorem 1 of \cite[p.~262]{balaban119} assumes ``(I.0.33)''. This is a misnumbered reference to (0.31) of \cite{balaban109}.
\end{remark}

In $u$-variables, (2.6)--(2.9) follow from two pairwise bounds:
\begin{itemize}
\item (P-up) (H-156) $u_m \le u_n + B'(n-m)$, with no additive constant;
\item (P-low) (H-155) $u_m \ge u_n + \beta(n-m) - D'$, with $\beta>0$, $D'\ge 0$ fixed,
\end{itemize}
together with the smallness condition
\begin{itemize}
\item K-018 (S) $\gamma^{-2} \ge c_{sw} D'$, $c_{sw} = \max(2, 1/(1-(1+\beta_0)^{-2})) = 64/15$ at $\beta_0 = 1/7$, and $\gamma \le \gamma_*(\beta_0,\beta,B',p_0,p_1,r,\nu,\kappa_0)$ (the conditions that literal (0.31) also needs). $\gamma_*$ is free of the $D'$-ratio conditions, but it depends on $R$ through $B'$: it contains $\gamma^2B' \le (1+\beta_0)^2 - 1$ with $B' = 5b/4 + 2R$. The order of constants in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} ($R$ before $\gamma$) handles this.
\end{itemize}
$\beta_0$ must be fixed before $\gamma$.

\subsection{The weak form}\label{sec:2w}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem 2w, weak form]\label{thm:L001}
Let $G = SU(2)$, $d=4$, $L$ odd, $L>11$ (or $L$ odd $\ge 3$, with (H0) (H-202) and Ba{\l}aban's construction of $\beta_j(g)$ at this $L$ as hypotheses). Assume (B2) (Section~\ref{sec:B2}), the fixed-level inputs (PI), (F), ($M_j$), V1, (G2) of Section~\ref{sec:routeK}, the history condition L5 (Section~\ref{sec:L5}), and the bound $|\beta_{k+1}(h,g)-\beta_{k+1}(0)|\le C_\beta g$ on every past $h$ (item 7 of Section~\ref{sec:remains}). Then there are $\gamma>0$, $g_1\in\,]0,\gamma]$, $\beta>0$, $B'\ge\beta$, $D'\ge 0$ such that for every $K\ge 1$ and every $g\in\,]0,g_1]$ there is a bare coupling $g_0 = g_0(K,g)$ with
\begin{itemize}
\item (b) $g_k\in\,]0,\gamma]$ for $0\le k\le K$, and $g_K = g$;
\item (c-pair) for all $0\le m\le n\le K$: $u_n + \beta(n-m) - D' \le u_m \le u_n + B'(n-m)$;
\item K-018 (S) holds for $\gamma$, $D'$.
\end{itemize}
In particular Ba{\l}aban's (0.31) holds up to the additive constant $D'$ on the lower side, with constants $\beta/\log L$ and $B'/\log L$. The two-sided bound fixes $1/g_k^2$ up to constants.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem K (Theorem~\ref{thm:L009}) gives the window bound (5.1) with $R=\kappa_*$. Lemma DT (Lemma~\ref{lem:L005}), applied in its history form (Theorem~\ref{thm:L030}), gives (b) and (c-pair). The choice of $\gamma$ in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} gives K-018 (S).
\end{proof}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:L012}, (H2) (R-025) implies (B2). A second route, Route B, gives the same conclusions from \ThmPW{} and Lemma DT, under the hypotheses (U), ($B\theta$) and the plane-wave defect bounds of Section~\ref{sec:routeB}. Both routes need (H0) at this $L$ and L5. See Section~\ref{sec:routes}.

\emph{Setting.} The torus obeys the volume hypothesis (Vol) (K-032) of Section~\ref{sec:lattice}: side $\ell>22M$ final-scale cells, or the even form $\ell=2L^{m_T}$, $m_T\ge m_M+1$. The proof of Theorem 2w does not use (Vol), because Lemmas DS and DT use the whole-lattice $\beta_j$. (Vol) enters where Theorem 2w is applied on the torus: in Theorem 1 through R-032, and in Theorem~\ref{thm:L003}.

\emph{Inputs.} By Route K, Theorem 2w uses one Ba{\l}aban-level statement, R-006 (the inductive assumption (H0)), the inputs R-025--R-031, and the external result X-005 \cite{Savvidy2019}. Section~\ref{sec:rem-ledger} lists these inputs.

\subsection{Literal (0.31)}\label{sec:literal}

\begin{itemize}
\item (L1) Literal (0.31) holds for every $K\ge K_0$ and every $g\in\,]0,\gamma]$ under eventual uniform positivity: there are $J$ and $\beta_*>0$ with $\beta_j(0)\ge\beta_*$ for all $j>J$, and $\sup_j|\beta_j(0)|<\infty$. This implication is Lemma~\ref{lem:L004} (d); it is formalized in Lean. Theorem K (L-009) and \ThmPW{} do not give eventual positivity: they bound window sums, not single increments. Eventual positivity would follow from the convergence route (Section~\ref{sec:convergence}), which is open.
\item (L2) Literal (0.31) holds for every $K\ge 1$ if $\inf_j\beta_j(0)>0$ and $\sup_j\beta_j(0)<\infty$ (Lemmas D and E). If $\beta_j(0)\to\beta_\infty>0$, then ``every $\beta_j(0)>0$'' is equivalent to $\inf_j\beta_j(0)>0$ (Lemma DS (L-004), Step 8). This implication is formalized in Lean. Its inputs are open.
\item Literal (0.31) is not needed for Theorem 1. Section~\ref{sec:needs} shows that the proof uses (c-pair) and K-018 (S). Literal (0.31) is used directly in one place only, the alternative choice of $\varepsilon_0$ in \cite[p.~248]{balaban119}, which Ba{\l}aban does not adopt.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[necessity of positive increments]
Literal (0.31) at every $K\ge 1$ forces $\beta_K(0)\ge\beta\log L>0$ for every $K$. To see this, take the window $k=K-1$ and let $g\to 0$, using (H0) (H-202); so $L$ is odd and $L>11$. So certified early steps are needed only for (L2), not for Theorem 1.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Shooting lemmas}\label{sec:shooting}

The shooting lemmas turn bounds on the increments $\beta_j$ into a trajectory with prescribed terminal coupling. Lemma~\ref{lem:L004} needs eventual positivity of $\beta_j(0)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:L005} needs only bounded window sums, and Theorem 2w (L-001) uses it. The notation is that of Section~\ref{sec:couplings}. Lemmas DS, DT, D, E are abstract statements about a sequence of functions $\beta_j$. Applied to Ba{\l}aban's $\beta_j(g)$, they need $L$ odd $>11$ (or (H0) (H-202) at this $L$) and L5.

\subsubsection{Lemma DS (shooting without early positivity)}\label{sec:Dprime}

\begin{lemma}[Lemma DS]\label{lem:L004}
\emph{Hypotheses.} (i) $\beta_{k+1}$ is continuous on $[0,\gamma_0]$ and $|\beta_{k+1}(g)-\beta_{k+1}(0)|\le C_1 g$, with $C_1$ independent of $k$, $K$. (ii) $\sup_j|\beta_j(0)|\le B$. (iii) There is $J$ with $\beta_j(0)\ge\beta_\infty/2$ for all $j>J$. (This is all the proof uses of ``$\beta_j(0)\to\beta_\infty>0$''.)

\emph{Constants.} $\gamma := \min(\gamma_0,\gamma_{Bal},\beta_\infty/(4C_1))$; $B' := B+\beta_\infty/4$; $D := J\cdot B'$; $D' := J(B+\beta_\infty/2)$; $g_1 := (1/\gamma^2+D)^{-1/2}$; $K_0 := J+\lceil 8D'/\beta_\infty\rceil$.

\emph{Conclusions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item (a) $T(k,K)\ge -D$ for all $k<K$ along any couplings in $]0,\gamma]$.
\item (b) For every $K\ge 1$ and $g\in\,]0,g_1]$ there is $g_0$ with all $g_k\in\,]0,\gamma]$ and $g_K = g$.
\item (c) $u_K + (\beta_\infty/4)(K-k) - D' \le u_k \le u_K + B'(K-k)$. The upper side has no constant.
\item (d) For $K\ge K_0$: literal (0.31) with lower constant $\beta_\infty/8$, and shooting for every $g\in\,]0,\gamma]$. If every $\beta_j(0)>0$: literal (0.31) for all $K$ (Step 8, that is, Lemmas D and E).
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof (sketch)]
The clamped flow $\tilde u_{k+1} = \tilde u_k - \beta_{k+1}(\varphi(\tilde u_k))$ is continuous in $u_0$. Its tail sums lie in $[-D, B'(K-k)]$ for every $u_0$, because clamped couplings lie in $]0,\gamma]$. The intermediate value theorem on $[t-D,t+B'K]$ gives $\tilde u_K = t$. Then $\tilde u_k\ge t-D\ge 1/\gamma^2$ for $g\le g_1$, so the clamp is never active. The bound is derived for the clamped flow first, so the argument is not circular. For history-dependent $\beta$ this proof needs $\beta_{k+1}$ defined and continuous on the whole cube $]0,\gamma]^{k+1}$; Section~\ref{sec:L5} removes this need.
\end{proof}

\emph{Lean.} Lemma DS is formalized in Lean as \texttt{YmLean.lemma\_DPrime} (\file{lean/YmLean/LemmaDPrime.lean}). The Lean hypotheses are weaker: continuity on the interval instead of $C^1$, and (iii) instead of convergence. Step 8 is \texttt{lemma\_DPrime\_step8} (\file{lean/YmLean/LemmaDE.lean}). These files treat one-argument $\beta_j(g)$; the history form is in Section~\ref{sec:L5}.

\begin{remark}[the condition $g\le g_1$]\label{rem:4}
Without $g\le g_1$, (b) can fail at small $K$: take $\beta_1=\beta_2=\beta_3=-0.8$, $K=3$, $g=\gamma$.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Lemma DT (tail-sum form; no index $J$)}\label{sec:Ddprime}

\begin{lemma}[Lemma DT]\label{lem:L005}
\emph{Hypotheses.} (i) as in Lemma~\ref{lem:L004}, and the window bound
\[
\text{(5.1)}\quad T_0(k,K) := \sum_{i=k+1}^{K}\beta_i(0)\in[\,b(K-k)-2R,\; b(K-k)+2R\,]\quad\text{for all }0\le k<K,
\]
with $b := c\log L>0$ and some $R\ge 0$. (Route K gives $R=\kappa_*$; Route B gives $R=C_B$.)

\emph{Constants.} $\gamma := \min(\gamma_0,\gamma_{Bal},b/(4C_1))$; $\beta := 3b/4$; $B' := 5b/4+2R$; $D=D' := 2R$; $g_1 := (1/\gamma^2+D)^{-1/2}$.

\emph{Conclusions.} (b) of Lemma~\ref{lem:L004}, and the pairwise bound
\[
\text{(c-pair)}\quad u_n+\beta(n-m)-D'\le u_m\le u_n+B'(n-m),\quad 0\le m\le n\le K,
\]
with no constant on the upper side.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$|T-T_0|\le C_1\gamma(K-k)\le(b/4)(K-k)$. Add (5.1). Steps 2--5 of the proof of Lemma DS (L-004) use only continuity and the bracket $\tilde T\in[-D,B'(K-k)]$; this bracket now comes from (5.1). The pairwise form is the same window argument with $K\to n$. For history-dependent $\beta$, Section~\ref{sec:L5} replaces the clamped flow by nested intervals.
\end{proof}

\emph{Lean.} Lemma DT is formalized in Lean as \texttt{YmLean.lemma\_DDoublePrime} (\file{lean/YmLean/LemmaDDoublePrime.lean}). The formal statement proves (a-tail), (b) with the clamp inactive on the solution, (c-pair) with $\beta=3b/4$, $B'=5b/4+2R$, $D'=2R$, and (d) below. Its hypotheses are weaker than those of Lemma~\ref{lem:L005}: continuity instead of $C^1$; any $\gamma$ with $0<\gamma\le\gamma_0$ and $C_1\gamma\le b/4$; $R\ge0$ derived; $K=0$ allowed. (c-pair) and (d) hold for every trajectory with couplings in $]0,\gamma]$.

In addition the following holds, and it is formalized in Lean. (d) For $n-m\ge8R/b$, $u_n+(b/2)(n-m)\le u_m\le u_n+B'(n-m)$. So (5.1) alone gives literal (0.31), with lower constant $b/2$, only for windows with $n-m\ge8R/b$. Short windows near $K$ are not controlled (see Section~\ref{sec:literal}).

\subsubsection{Lemma DT and Ba{\l}aban's proof: the cross-scale sweep}\label{sec:sweep}

We searched all twelve papers of Ba{\l}aban's series for uses of the coupling sequence at two different scales, and we read every hit that is not single-scale on the printed page. The comparisons A--E on the pages \cite[p.~192, p.~198 (1.94)]{balaban122a}, \cite[p.~381 (twice), p.~386]{balaban122b} are of this kind. Comparison A has the factor $(1+\beta_0)^2(k-j)^{\beta_0}$, and there $\delta'_j = g_jA_1p_1(g_j)$. The five comparisons give four rows of the list below: A is item 11, E is item 12, C and D are item 16, and B is item 18.

\begin{proposition}[cross-scale sweep, R-034]
There are 19 cross-scale uses of the coupling sequence, all in CMP 119 and CMP 122 I--II; there are none in CMP 95, 96, 98, 99, 102, 109, 116. Each is covered by (b), (c-pair) and K-018 (S) of Section~\ref{sec:needs}:
\begin{itemize}
\item items 1, 7, 10, 12, 17 need (P-up) (H-156) with no constant (for example, (2.6) line 1 is exactly (P-up));
\item items 2, 3, 11, 13, 15, 16, 18 need (P-low) (H-155) with fixed multiplicative slack $(1+\beta_0)$ or $(1+\beta_0)^2$; item 2 ((2.6) line 2) gives the constant $64/15$; item 11 needs only $2.42D'$;
\item items 4 ((2.8)--(2.9), $\varepsilon$ and $R$ ratios), 6 (CMP 119 (3.7), (3.8), (3.12), (3.13)), and 9 (CMP 122 I (1.52)), the last two of the form $\varepsilon_{k+1}\le(1+\beta_0)\varepsilon_k$, use both sides; they follow as items 1 and 3 do (item 4 from items 1--3);
\item item 14 (CMP 122 I p.\ 200, $R_{k-N_0+1}$ against $R_k$) uses the lower side; it follows as item 3 does;
\item item 5 (CMP 119 (2.31), (2.46): $\sum_j g_j^{\kappa_0}\le g_n^{\kappa_0-6}$) needs linear growth (P-low) and $\gamma^{-2}\ge 2D'$;
\item item 0 is CMP 119 Theorem 1 assuming (0.31); its proof works with (2.6)--(2.9);
\item item 8 (CMP 119 p.\ 284) is in a sketch and carries no load.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[scope of the sweep]
(1) The sweep is a search of the text together with a reading of each hit on the page. It is not a line-by-line reading of every proof. (2) Ba{\l}aban's sentence ``Similar inequalities hold for other constants, which will be introduced later'' \cite[p.~256]{balaban119} refers to constants that the sweep did not locate one by one; Proposition~\ref{prop:L023} gives the list. (3) Ba{\l}aban says that $\beta_0$ ``can be chosen arbitrarily small, if $g$ is sufficiently small''. Under (P-low) the floor is $\beta_0\gtrsim D\gamma^2/2$, so $\beta_0$ must be fixed first. We found no step that needs $\beta_0\to 0$.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Lemmas D and E}\label{sec:DE}

\begin{lemma}[Lemma D]\label{lem:L006}
If $b\le\beta_j(g)\le b'$ on $]0,\gamma]$ with $b>0$, then for every $K$ and $g\in\,]0,\gamma]$ shooting succeeds and $t+b(K-k)\le u_k\le t+b'(K-k)$.
\end{lemma}
This is literal (0.31). The proof is an intermediate value argument on the interval $[t+bK,\,t+b'K]$ of initial values. A nested-interval variant holds for $\beta$ defined only on admissible histories; it is not formalized in Lean. Lemma D is formalized in Lean as \texttt{lemma\_D} (\file{lean/YmLean/LemmaDE.lean}).

\begin{lemma}[Lemma E]\label{lem:L007}
If $\beta_0\le\beta_j(0)\le\beta_0'$ and $|\beta_j(g)-\beta_j(0)|\le Cg^2$, then for $\gamma\le\sqrt{\beta_0/(2C)}$: $\beta_0/2\le\beta_j(g)\le\beta_0'+\beta_0/2$.
\end{lemma}
Lemma E is formalized in Lean as \texttt{lemma\_E}. The same two-line proof gives the $C^1$ form: if $|\beta_j(g)-\beta_j(0)|\le C_1g$ and $\gamma\le\beta_0/(2C_1)$, then $|\beta_j(g)-\beta_j(0)|\le\beta_0/2$, and the same conclusion follows.

\subsubsection{History dependence (L5)}\label{sec:L5}

Ba{\l}aban's $\beta_{k+1}$ depends on $(g_0,\dots,g_k)$. Two facts make this dependence matter.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Domain.} Ba{\l}aban defines $\beta_{k+1}$ only on histories for which his Theorem 1 induction has run to step $k$. By the sweep (Section~\ref{sec:sweep}), that induction uses (2.6)--(2.9), that is (c-pair) and K-018 (S), on the history, not only $g_i\in\,]0,\gamma]$.
  \item \emph{The clamp.} The clamped shooting of Lemmas~\ref{lem:L004} and~\ref{lem:L005} evaluates $\beta$ on clamped histories $\tilde g_i=\varphi(\tilde u_i)$. A clamped history can violate the growth side of (c-pair): from $\max(a,c)-\max(b,c)\ge\min(a-b,0)$ one gets only $u_m\ge u_n-D'$, not $u_m\ge u_n+\beta(n-m)-D'$. So the clamped proof needs $\beta$ on the whole cube of histories, which Ba{\l}aban does not supply.
\end{itemize}
Theorem~\ref{thm:L030} (Lemmas DT-nest and DS-nest) removes the clamp. It uses nested intervals on true trajectories, with domain $D_k=\{u_0,\dots,u_k\ge\gamma^{-2}\}$ and the constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:L005}. On such trajectories, (c-pair) up to step $k$ follows from the tail-sum bound (a-tail) of Lemma DT, and K-018 (S) holds by the choice of $\gamma$. So every evaluation of $\beta$ stays inside Ba{\l}aban's domain. The continuity input (C-tr) (H-089) is the continuity of $\beta$ along true trajectories; it is proved relative to Ba{\l}aban's small-field step in Section~\ref{sec:src-T1}. The history forms are formalized in Lean as \texttt{lemma\_D\_hist}, \texttt{lemma\_DPrime\_hist}, \texttt{nested\_shoot}, \texttt{lemma\_DPrime\_hist\_adm} and \texttt{lemma\_DDoublePrime\_hist\_adm}. These need no continuity on the cube. A Lean counterexample, \texttt{$\beta$ce\_no\_shot}, shows that separate continuity in each argument is not enough.

\subsection{The exact structure of the one-loop sums}\label{sec:oneloop}

The window sums of the shooting lemma are sums of one-loop increments $\beta_j(0)$. This section expresses these sums as level-$k$ quantities of a coarse Gaussian theory. It isolates the remainder $\kappa_k$ whose boundedness is the content of Theorem K (L-009). It also lists the symbol facts, uniform in the level, that Section~\ref{sec:routes} uses.

\subsubsection{Telescoping}\label{sec:telescope}

Let $S_k$ be the $p\to 0$ $F^2$-coefficient of the composed $k$-step gauge-fixed one-loop integral (U1 (1.2)). Then $S_k=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_j(0)$ and $S_0=0$ (\file{BETA_FP} Prop.\ 1.1). The identity is exact for the $p\to0$ coefficient at fixed $k$. It does not hold for $\log Z_k$ at fixed $p$, because the plane wave is off-shell.

\subsubsection{Anchor-free identity}\label{sec:prop21}

\begin{proposition}[anchor-free identity; Prop.\ 2.1 of \file{BETA_FP_LEMMA43}]\label{prop:L008}
Under (G1) (H-194) $=\{\Delta D=0,\ \tilde QD=D_c\iota_a^T\}$:
\[
\log Z_\Lambda(V)=\bigl[\log\det X-\tfrac12\log\det K\bigr]-\bigl[\log\det(D_c^TD_c)-\tfrac12\log\det(\Delta_\Lambda(V)+D_cD_c^T)\bigr].
\]
\end{proposition}
In words: the constrained one-loop term equals the fine Wilson one-loop term in background Feynman gauge minus the one-loop term of the coarse Gaussian theory with kernel $\Delta_\Lambda(V)$. All point-anchor objects cancel, and the constant is exactly 0. A lattice computation with the CMP 109 averaging at random SU(2) backgrounds agrees with the identity to relative error $10^{-14}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\tilde QD=D_c\iota_a^T$ holds exactly at any background (lattice check: $10^{-15}$). The condition $\Delta D=0$ carries load: the raw Hessian gives $O(1)$ errors.
\item At the constant abelian field $V_f$ the background is critical. So $\Delta D=0$ holds exactly, and Proposition~\ref{prop:L008} holds exactly. At a plane wave it holds up to the defect $\mathfrak d_Y$ (\file{BETA_FP_PW} Lemma 2.3).
\item The identity concerns per-cell infinite-volume densities. On a torus the constant zero modes must be removed. This is the input (G2) (R-030), which holds at each fixed $j$ relative to the inputs listed in Section~\ref{sec:src-T1}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{The constant $c$}\label{sec:const}
$c=11N^2/(12\pi^2)=2N\cdot b_0/(8\pi^2)$, $b_0=11N/3$; for SU(2), $c=0.371511$. The normalization is $1/g_B^2=2N/g^2$. The log coefficient of the fine piece at constant field is $c_2=c/2$ per $\ln(1/\varphi)$; this agrees with Savvidy \cite[eq.~(1.4)]{Savvidy2019}. This is the input (F) (R-027). Theorem~\ref{thm:L041} proves it on the lattice by a proper-time argument.

$b:=c\log L$: $0.25751$ ($L=2$), $0.40815$ (3), $0.51502$ (4), $0.59793$ (5), $0.72293$ (7).

\subsubsection{The decomposition $S_k=ck\log L+\kappa_W^F-\kappa_k$}\label{sec:decomp}

As bookkeeping the split is empty: any $c'$ gives an exact identity if $\kappa_k$ is defined as the remainder. Boundedness of a remainder-defined $\kappa_k$ is a restatement of the problem. The content comes from a definition of $\kappa_k$ by the coarse theory alone.

\begin{proposition}[\file{KAPPA_BOUNDED} Prop.\ 1.1]
Assume (PI) (R-026), (F), ($M_j$), V1, (G2). With the Wilson level $j=0$ as reference,
\[
S_j=cj\log L-d_j,\qquad \kappa_j=\kappa_W^F+d_j,
\]
\[
d_j:=-\frac{1}{2n_2}\int_0^1dt\int_{\BZ}\frac{dK}{(2\pi)^4}\,\tr\bigl[b_{t,2}\,\delta\sigma_j^{(0)}+b_{t,1}\,\delta\sigma_j^{(1)}+b_{t,0}\,\delta\sigma_j^{(2)}\bigr],
\]
where $\delta\sigma_j^{(m)}=\sigma_j^{(m)}-\sigma_0^{(m)}$ are differences of the $f^m$ Taylor symbols of the coarse kernel at level $j$ and level 0, and $b_{t,m}$ are the Moyal inverse coefficients of the interpolated coarse operator. No limit symbols $\sigma_\infty$ are used.
\end{proposition}
\begin{itemize}
\item Numerical checks: $\rho(1)=\kappa_W^F$ (L\"uscher--Weisz (5.7) \cite{luscherweisz1995}); at $L=2$, $d_1=-0.372$ and $\kappa_1=1.114$; at $L=3$, $d_1=c\log3-\beta_1=-0.256\pm0.01$ (fits give $-0.254\dots-0.258$). These check the definition of $d_j$ through $S_j$. They do not evaluate (1.1) from the symbols independently.
\item Cor.\ 3.1 of \file{BETA_FP_LEMMA43}, $\mathcal C(\Lambda)=c\log\Lambda+\kappa_W^F-\rho(\Lambda)$, is an exact identity. The statement ``the whole $\log\Lambda$ sits in the fine piece'' is equivalent to $\rho(\Lambda)=o(\log\Lambda)$. It is not a separate fact.
\item Given convergence $\beta_j(0)\to\beta_\infty$, Ces\`aro gives $\beta_\infty=c\log L-\lim\kappa_k/k$. So the hypothesis (H$_o$) ``$\kappa_k=o(k)$'' is equivalent to $\beta_\infty=c\log L$, not weaker. Theorem K (Theorem~\ref{thm:L009}) gives $\kappa_k$ bounded, hence $\kappa_k=o(k)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Bounded window sums: two conditional routes}\label{sec:routes}

This section gives two routes to the window bound (5.1) of Lemma~\ref{lem:L005}, $|S_K-S_k-b(K-k)|\le 2R$, with $b=c\log L$. Lemma DT (L-005) then gives Theorem 2w (L-001), at odd $L>11$ (or with (H0) (H-202) at this $L$ as a hypothesis) and with L5. Neither route uses convergence of $\beta_j(0)$, a rate, (N), or certified early steps. The two routes have formally distinct named hypotheses. Their mathematical content is not independent: both reduce their uniform-in-level content to the same inequality (H2) (R-025) on the two-step minimizer map (Section~\ref{sec:H2}), together with facts of the type of Theorem A3 (L-071).

\subsubsection{Route K: Theorem K}\label{sec:routeK}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem K]\label{thm:L009}
Let $L$ be odd, $L\ge3$. Assume (B2) (Section~\ref{sec:B2}) and the fixed-level inputs (PI), (F), ($M_j$), V1, (G2) listed below. Then $\sup_k|d_k|\le\kappa_*<\infty$. So $\sup_k|\kappa_k|\le|\kappa_W^F|+\kappa_*$ and $|S_k-ck\log L|\le\kappa_*$ for all $k$. Window sums satisfy (5.1) with $R=\kappa_*$.
\end{theorem}
Theorem K is a statement about one-loop quantities. Its use in Lemma DT for Ba{\l}aban's $\beta_j(g)$ needs $L$ odd $>11$, or (H0) at this $L$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:L012}, (H2) implies (B2).

\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
\item By \file{KAPPA_BOUNDED} Prop.\ 1.1 (Section~\ref{sec:decomp}), $S_j=cj\log L-d_j$, under (PI) (R-026), (F) (R-027), ($M_j$), V1, (G2) (R-030).
\item Lemma 2.1: the only $K$-derivative of $\sigma^{(1)}$ in $d_j$ enters linearly with $f^0$ coefficients. Integration by parts moves it onto $f^0$ data. The boundary terms vanish: the integrand is periodic up to a common constant conjugation, and the inner sphere contributes $O(r^2)$. So $\sigma^{(1)}$ is needed only in $C^0$, which L-071 gives.
\item Prop.\ 2.2: with (E0) the $f^0$ bounds (Prop.\ B, O5/A0, Lemma M (L-219)), (E1) L-071 and Theorem W (L-308), and (B2), every term of the $d_j$ integrand is $\le C|K|^{-2}$, uniformly in $j$ and $t$. In $d=4$ this leaves two powers of infrared margin. So $|d_j|\le\kappa_*:=(C(L)/n_2)(1+Y_1^2+C_A(1+Y_1)+Y_2)\cdot I_2$.
\item Infrared: the degree $-4$ part of the coarse $f^2$ integrand is the same at every level (Lemma M and Theorem W). So the $\tau_c\log$ term cancels exactly in level differences.\qedhere
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
The constants are existential; $\kappa_*$ is not evaluated.

The fixed-level inputs of Route K are needed at each $j$ separately; no uniformity in $j$ is required.
\begin{itemize}
\item (PI) probe independence: the plane-wave $p^2$ coefficient equals the constant-field $f^2$ coefficient. It holds at each fixed level relative to the inputs listed in Section~\ref{sec:src-T1} (R-026).
\item (F) the fine log coefficient $c_2$ at constant field (Section~\ref{sec:const}). This is Theorem~\ref{thm:L041}.
\item ($M_j$) the formal $f^2$ coefficient $d_j$ equals the true one at fixed $j$. This is Theorem~\ref{thm:L029}, proved relative to the inputs listed there. A resolvent bound cannot give it directly, because of the Nielsen--Olesen zero at $k_\perp^2=|ef|$. Theorem~\ref{thm:L029} uses a direct bound on $\log|\det|$ instead.
\item V1: the Maxwell coefficient of the per-step determinant equals $\beta_{j+1}(0)$; that is, CMP 109 (1.3)--(1.5) is complete at order $g^0$. It holds at each fixed level, given (G2), Ba{\l}aban's inductive bounds, and the averaging (0.4) (Section~\ref{sec:src-T1}).
\item (G2) per-cell infinite-volume densities. It holds at each fixed $j$ relative to the inputs listed in Section~\ref{sec:src-T1}. We do not prove a $j$-uniform version, and Route K does not need one.
\item (H0) Ba{\l}aban's inductive assumption: $\beta_{j+1}$ is smooth on $[0,\gamma]$, ``uniformly bounded on this interval together with all derivatives'' \cite[pp.~263--264, (1.22)]{balaban109}. It is available at odd $L>11$ only. CMP 116 has no theorem statement and no $C^m$-in-$g$ bound in words. The smoothness in $g$ is the inductive assumption of CMP 109; CMP 109 Theorem 3 asserts it, and CMP 116 completes the proof of Theorem 3.
\item L5: the history-dependent $\beta$, its domain, and its continuity (Section~\ref{sec:L5}; Theorem~\ref{thm:L030}).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Route B: \ThmPW{}}\label{sec:routeB}

\begin{theorem}[\ThmPW{}; Theorem 3.2 of \file{BETA_FP_PW}]\label{thm:L010}
Let $L$ be odd, $L\ge3$. Assume
\begin{itemize}
\item (U) there are $p_*>0$ and $C_U$ with $|\beta_{j+1}(p)-\beta_{j+1}(0)|\le C_Up^2$ for all $j\ge0$, $0<p\le p_*$;

\item L\"uscher--Weisz (5.7) \cite{luscherweisz1995}.

\subsection{The hypothesis (B2) of Route K, and the single uniform inequality (H2)}\label{sec:H2}\label{sec:B2}

Both routes need a bound on the coarse symbols that is uniform in the level. This section states that bound for Route K and reduces it to the inequality (H2) (R-025). The numerical values of (H2) are in Sections~\ref{sec:numH2}--\ref{sec:cert}. At $L=13$, Theorem~\ref{thm:L046} (Section~\ref{sec:certH2}) proves (H2).

(B2): $\sup_{j\ge0}\sup_{K\in\BZ}|\sigma_j^{(2)}(K)|<\infty$. A weaker form suffices, (B2w) (H-184): $\sup_j\int_{\BZ}|K|^{-2}|\sigma_j^{(2)}(K)|\,d^4K<\infty$. The reason is that $\sigma^{(2)}$ enters $d_j$ only undifferentiated (\file{KAPPA_BOUNDED} Remark 2.3).

The symbol $\sigma^{(2)}$ obeys the one-step recursion (\file{U3_AW} (1.2)) $\sigma_j^{(2)}=L^{-2}\mathcal A_j\sigma_{j-1}^{(2)}+Q_j[\sigma_{j-1}^{(1)}]+g_j^{(2)}$, with $\mathcal A_js=L^{-2}H_j^\dagger(\oplus_l\,s(k_l))H_j$ and $H_j(K)$ the one-step gauge-fixed minimizer map. Notation: $\rho_1:=\sup_{j,K}\|H_j(K)\|/L$ (one step); $H_{(2),j}(K):=(\oplus_lH_{j-1}(k_l))H_j(K)$ (two steps), $\rho_2:=\sup_{j\ge2}\sup_{K\text{ real}}\|H_{(2),j}(K)\|/L^2$.

\subsubsection{Known bounds}\label{sec:B2proved}
\begin{itemize}
\item The $K=0$ bound: $\sup_j|\sigma_j^{(2)}(0)|\le C(|e|C_A+e^2)$. L-071 and scaling give $|\partial^2\sigma_j^{(1)}(0)|\le2C_A$ by polarization, and the slaving identity (W-2) (H-178) expresses $\sigma_j^{(2)}(0)$ through these second derivatives.
\item Each fixed $j$: $\sigma_j^{(2)}$ is analytic on a fixed strip (Prop.\ R-003), hence bounded. The content of (B2) is uniformity in $j$.
\item Prop.\ 4.1: if (h) $h:=\sup_{j,K}\|H_j(K)\|<L^2$ and ($C1\sigma$) $\sup_j\sup_K|\partial_K\sigma_j^{(1)}(K)|<\infty$, then (B2) holds. Proof: in the form norm $\pm s\le N(\Delta+1)$, $N_j\le(h^2/L^4)N_{j-1}+Z_2$, and $Z_2$ is finite by L-071, ($C1\sigma$), and the fixed $f^0$ vertices.
\item The mechanism of L-072 alone does not give (B2). The obstruction is the missing uniform $C^1$ bound on $\sigma^{(1)}$. It enters twice: in $Q_j$ (the term $L^{-3}\partial_K\sigma_{j-1}^{(1)}$) and in the longitudinal part of $\sigma^{(2)}$ given by (W-2). L-071 is $C^0$ only.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{The one-step bounds at $L=3$}\label{sec:refuted}
\begin{itemize}
\item The condition (HS) $h<L\sqrt{L-1}$ fails at $L=3$. The measured values $\max_K\|H_j(K)\|/L$ are $1.652,\,1.736,\,1.757,\,1.760$ for $j=1,2,3,\infty$, and the threshold is $\sqrt2=1.414$. A gauge choice cannot help: $H_j$ is already minimal-norm in its gauge orbit, for gauge fixings that select an orbit.
\item The one-step sup-norm $C^1$ recursion at $L=3$ has the factor $\rho_1^2/L=0.909,\,1.0046,\,1.0289,\,1.0325$ for $j=1,2,3,\infty$ (numerical). So it exceeds 1 for $j\ge2$ ($\rho_1>\sqrt3=1.7321$). At $K=0$ the factor is $L^{-1}$. The bad region is the face $K_3=K_4=\pi$, $K_1=K_2\approx1.86$--$1.92$. This shows that the one-step sup-norm bound fails at $L=3$. It does not show that a $C^1$ bound fails; a weighted norm is not excluded.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{(H2) and the two-step theorems}\label{sec:twostep}
\[
\text{(H2)}\quad\rho_2=\sup_{j\ge2}\sup_{K\in\BZ\text{ real}}\|(\oplus_lH_{j-1}(k_l))H_j(K)\|/L^2<L.
\]
(H2) is a plain operator-norm inequality on real $K$. It uses no strip, no $\Delta$-relative weight, and only $n=2$.
\begin{theorem}[L-011]\label{thm:L011}
(H2) implies ($C1\sigma$), with contraction factor $q_1=\rho_2^2/L^2$ on the derivative.
\end{theorem}
\begin{theorem}[L-012]\label{thm:L012}
(H2) implies (B2), hence (B2w), in plain sup norm, with factor $q_2=\rho_2^2/L^4$.
\end{theorem}
Here ($C1\sigma$) is $\sup_j\sup_K|\partial_K\sigma_j^{(1)}(K)|<\infty$ (Section~\ref{sec:B2proved}). The proofs (\file{B2_TWOSTEP.md} \S1--\S3) rest on the following points.
\begin{itemize}
\item The two-step map. $\mathcal A_j\mathcal A_{j-1}s=L^{-4}H_{(2)}^\dagger(\oplus s)H_{(2)}$, with $dk_{l,l'}/dK=L^{-2}$.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:L011}. The chain-rule gain is exactly $L^{-2}$ per two steps on real $K$. So the threshold is $\rho_2<L$, not $\sqrt{L^2-1}$: no strip-Cauchy loss enters. The large constants ($b(L)$ of O6, $\eta_1$, $\eta_2$, $G_1$) enter only the source term, not the contraction factor.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:L012}. This contraction needs only $\rho_2<L^2$, which (H2) implies. In one step it is the condition (h) of Prop.\ 4.1.
\item (H2) and (H$_A$). (H2) is the plain operator norm of the same matrix as the condition (H$_A$) of \file{U3_AW} at $n=2$. It is weaker: the threshold is $L$ (3 at $L=3$) against $\sqrt{L^2-1}$ (2.828), on real $K$ only, and for $n=2$ only. We do not use (H$_A$).
\item The form of $Q_j$ (G3). The proofs use the form of $Q_j$ in \file{U3_AW} (1.2)/(3.3), in which $\partial\sigma^{(1)}$ enters linearly. We take the formula (3.3) of \file{U3_LEMMA51} as given there; we do not rederive it from the Moyal rule here.
\item We do not need $C^2$ and $C^3$ bounds on $\sigma^{(1)}$ for (B2).
\item Submultiplicativity: $\rho_2\le\rho_1^2$ always. At $L=3$ the gain of the two-step map is that this inequality is strict by a factor 1.50 (2.064 against 3.098).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{One-step sufficiency}\label{sec:onestep}
$\|H_{(2),j}(K)\|\le\max_l\|H_{j-1}(k_l)\|\cdot\|H_j(K)\|\le L^2\rho_1^2$. So $\rho_1^2<L$ implies (H2). Then the one-step $C^1$ recursion of \file{B2_TWOSTEP} (2.2) contracts with factor $\rho_1^2/L$. The factorization is formalized in Lean as \texttt{BlockNorm.rho2\_le}.
At $L=3$ the measured $\rho_1^2$ exceeds $L$, so the one-step route fails and the two-step map is needed. At $L=5$ and $L=7$ the measured $\rho_1^2/L$ is below 1. The values are in Section~\ref{sec:numonestep}. At $L=13$ Theorem~\ref{thm:L046} gives $\sup_{j,K}\rho_1^2\le10<13$, so (H2) holds there with $\rho_2\le10$.

\subsubsection{The remaining inequality}\label{sec:remainingineq}
At a fixed odd $L\ge3$ the single uniform hypothesis is (H2).
\begin{itemize}
\item Route K: (H2) $\Rightarrow$ ($C1\sigma$) (L-011) $\Rightarrow$ (B2) (L-012).
\item Route B: in the possible approach of Section~\ref{sec:routeBinputs}, (H2) and (T1) give (U), and (H2) gives the vertex bound for $\gamma_{rec}$ in ($B\theta$) (\file{BETA_FP_U} Theorem 2.4, Prop.\ 3.2).
\item (H2) at $L=13$, inside Ba{\l}aban's range $L>11$: Theorem~\ref{thm:L046} gives $\rho_1(j,K)^2\le10<13$ for all $j\ge1$ (including $\infty$) and all $K$, hence $\rho_2\le10<13$, under Convention~\ref{conv:avg}. The same holds at $L=15,17,21$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:H2other}). At $L=3,5,9$ only floating-point values exist (at $L=9$ at one point).
\item Alternatives: ($C1\sigma$) and (h) imply (B2) (Prop.\ 4.1). For (H$_A$) $\Rightarrow$ (B2) there is only a plausibility argument. One can also aim at (B2w) directly. No per-momentum $d=4$ constant-field code exists, so (B2) itself has not been scanned numerically.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conclusion of the reduction}\label{sec:conclusion}

Theorem 2w (L-001) follows from the inputs of Route K, or from those of Route B. Precisely:

\emph{Route K.} (B2), which follows from (H2) (R-025) (Theorem~\ref{thm:L012}); the fixed-level inputs (PI) (R-026), (F) (R-027), ($M_j$), V1, (G2) (R-030); (H0) (H-202), Ba{\l}aban's inductive assumption at odd $L>11$, whose use here reduces to named inputs (Section~\ref{sec:src-r5}); and L5 (Theorem~\ref{thm:L030}). These give Theorem K (L-009) with $R=\kappa_*$, hence (5.1), hence Lemma DT (L-005), hence Theorem 2w. At $L=13$, Theorem~\ref{thm:L046} gives (H2).

\emph{Route B.} (U) and ($B\theta$) with $\theta_*<1$, L\"uscher--Weisz (5.7), bounded $\mathfrak d_Y$ and $\mathfrak d_{gf}$, the identification of $\beta$ (uniform in $k$), (G2), (H0) at odd $L>11$, and L5 give \ThmPW{} with $R=C_B$, hence (5.1), hence Lemma DT, hence Theorem 2w. The possible approach of Section~\ref{sec:routeBinputs} replaces (U) and ($B\theta$) by (T1), (H2), (DF) and L\"uscher--Weisz (5.7).

\emph{Single uniform hypothesis.} Both routes need (H2). Theorem~\ref{thm:L046} proves (H2) at $L=13$, an odd $L>11$. This gives (B2), the only uniform-in-level hypothesis of Route K (Theorems~\ref{thm:L011}, \ref{thm:L012}). In the possible approach to Route B it reduces (U) to (T1); (DF) and the identification of $\beta$ remain.

\emph{Then Ba{\l}aban's Theorem 1.} Theorem 2w gives (b) and (c-pair). With $\gamma$ shrunk so that K-018 (S) holds, every one of the 19 cross-scale uses in CMP 119 and CMP 122 I--II is covered (Section~\ref{sec:sweep}), and the single-scale uses need only $g_k\le\gamma$. So Theorem 1 holds for the bare coupling $g_0(K,g)$, for every $K$ and every $g\in\,]0,g_1]$, without Ba{\l}aban's coupling assumption. This needs L5 in the form of Section~\ref{sec:L5}: $\beta$ must exist on every history that the shooting visits. It also rests on Ba{\l}aban's published proof of Theorem 1 (R-013).

The sweep uses Ba{\l}aban's sentence that similar inequalities hold for the later constants (R-012). Proposition~\ref{prop:L023} lists these constants, with the order entries K-029--K-031. The step to Theorem 1 also needs the finite-volume identification R-032, which rests on R-015. Proposition~\ref{prop:L024} gives the torus coefficient within $C_jN_j^6e^{-m_jN_j/2}$ of the whole-lattice one, given (Loc) (H-141); exact equality cannot be derived. Proposition~\ref{prop:L027} gives the form of R-032 that we use, proved relative to K-032--K-038. So the step from Theorem 2w to Theorem 1 rests on 4 Ba{\l}aban-level statements (R-006, R-012, R-013, R-015), on the inputs R-025--R-032 and R-034, and on X-005.

\emph{Order of constants.} Fix $\beta_0,p_0,p_1,r,\nu,\kappa_0$ (Ba{\l}aban). Fix $L$ (odd, $>11$), then $R$ from Route K or B, then $b=c\log L$, then $\gamma\le\min(\gamma_0,\gamma_{Bal},b/(4C_1),\gamma_*)$ with $\gamma^{-2}\ge c_{sw}\cdot2R$. Then $g_1=(\gamma^{-2}+2R)^{-1/2}$. Ba{\l}aban's phrase ``for a sufficiently small positive $g$'' fits $g\le g_1$.

\emph{What this does not give.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Literal (0.31): only for windows of length $\ge8R/b$. Literal (0.31) at every $K$ needs (L1) or (L2) of Section~\ref{sec:literal}, which need individual increments, not window sums. Theorem 1 does not need it.
\item The value $\lim\beta_j(0)$ and any rate: Section~\ref{sec:convergence}.
\item Even $L$, or odd $L\le11$ without (H0) as an extra hypothesis (Ba{\l}aban assumes $L$ odd $>11$ throughout CMP 109); SU($N$) with $N>2$.
\item Monotone couplings: $g_m$ can exceed $g_n$ for $m<n$ by the bounded amount $u_m\ge u_n-D'$. Section~\ref{sec:sweep} shows that the cross-scale uses in Ba{\l}aban's proof tolerate this under K-018 (S), within the scope stated there.
\item A continuum limit. Section~\ref{sec:torus} treats it separately.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The convergence route}\label{sec:convergence}

Theorem 1 does not need this route. It would add: (i) existence of $\beta_\infty=\lim\beta_j(0)$; (ii) its value $c\log L$; (iii) a rate; (iv) eventual positivity, hence literal (0.31) for $K\ge K_0$ (Lemma DS (L-004) (d)); (v) with certified early steps, literal (0.31) at every $K$ (Section~\ref{sec:literal} (L2)). It also implies (B2), since convergence of $\sigma_j^{(2)}$ implies boundedness. So it needs strictly more than Route K.

\subsubsection{Components}
\begin{itemize}
\item U3 Theorem 4.1 with O5: $|\partial_K^\alpha(\Delta_M-\Delta_\infty)|\le CM^{-2}$, $|\alpha|\le2$, uniformly down to $K=0$, for odd $L$. Numerically $|\Delta_M-\Delta_\infty|=1.007,\,0.129,\,0.0147$ at $M=3,9,27$.
\item O6: $\|\mathfrak B_j(K)^{-1}\|\le b(L)$ for all $j$, $K$, for the one-step constraint at trivial background. The constant is crude ($b(3)\approx7.5\cdot10^{33}$). The measured corner values $103.5,\,176.7,\,193.0,\,195.3$ ($j=0,1,2,\infty$, $L=3$) use a surrogate $\Lambda$.
\item Theorem 6.1: assume V1. Then $\beta_{j+1}(0)=\Phi(\mathcal D_j)$ is Lipschitz in the level data, in the Taylor-data norm.
\item Lemma 5.1 (convergence of $\sigma_j^{(1)}$, $\sigma_j^{(2)}$ in $C^2$, $|\alpha|\le2$):
  \begin{itemize}
  \item in the neutral sector, the value bound holds with rate $L^{-4j}$; for the $K$-derivatives, \file{U3_LEMMA51} gives a possible approach;
  \item (W) holds (\file{U3_AW} Theorem W (L-308)); it removes the marginal direction of the $f^1$ recursion;
  \item (A) holds in qualitative form, and L-071 holds. (A) in full (strip/$C^3$ for $m=1$, all of $m=2$) would follow from (H$_A$), which is open. The route through (HS) fails at $L=3$ (Section~\ref{sec:refuted});
  \item for convergence of $\sigma^{(1)}$ at rate $jL^{-2j}$, \file{U3_AW} \S4.7 sketches a possible approach; convergence of $\sigma^{(2)}$ is open and needs (A) in full;
  \item the single-block-constraint version of Lemma 5.1 is false for $m=2$ in $d=4$. Ba{\l}aban's composed constraint is not affected.
  \end{itemize}
\item Prop.\ 3.6: assume V1 and (A) in full. Then $|\beta_{j+1}(0)-\beta_\infty|\le C(1+j)^2L^{-2j}$. The proof identifies the $\sigma$-Taylor data with the data of (6.1). The clean rate $L^{-2j}$ of (N) is not needed, and we do not expect it to hold.
\item Value of the limit. Given convergence, Theorem K (L-009) or \ThmPW{} gives $\beta_\infty=c\log L$ at once: $S_k/k\to\beta_\infty$ by Ces\`aro, and $S_k/k=c\log L+O(1/k)$. Two other approaches to the value exist. The first is the variant of Theorem 3.1 of \file{BETA_FP_H}, with (H) restated at the constant field; given (W) it reduces to Lemma 5.1 (1.2) alone, plus (PI), (F), and (M), where for (M) there is only a plausibility argument. The second is \file{BETA_FP_PW} Theorem 3.1 (limit form), given (U), ($C\theta$), and L\"uscher--Weisz (5.7), which we offer as a possible approach.
\item Rate of $\mathcal C(\Lambda)$: a plausibility argument through the slaving of \file{U3_AW} \S3.4(b) gives $O(\Lambda^{-2}\log\Lambda)$, given Lemma 5.1 with rates.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Early steps and $j_*$}\label{sec:jstar}
Under Prop.\ 3.6 and $\beta_\infty=c\log L$, $\beta_{j+1}(0)\ge(c\log L)/2$ for $j\ge j_*:=\min\{J:\,C(1+j)^2L^{-2j}\le(c\log L)/2\text{ for }j\ge J\}$. Heuristic sizes: $C\sim b^3$ gives $j_*\approx110$ ($L=3$) and $\approx70$ ($L=13$); $C\sim b^6$ (one-step inverses inside $C_{data}$) gives $\approx218$ and $\approx138$. With the measured $\|\mathfrak B^{-1}\|\approx195$: $j_*\approx10$ ($L=3$), $\approx4$ ($L=13$). A proof of $\beta_1(0),\dots,\beta_{j_*}(0)>0$ needs interval arithmetic at odd $L\ge13$. Dense methods need blocks of $\approx3.4\cdot10^5$ rows per momentum; the umklapp code reduces this to $12\times12$ blocks at $L^4$ momenta. This is needed only for (L2), not for Theorem 1.
