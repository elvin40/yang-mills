\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The problem}\label{sec:problem}

The Clay problem on quantum Yang--Mills theory asks for a four-dimensional quantum gauge theory with compact simple gauge group that satisfies axioms at least as strong as those of Osterwalder and Schrader \cite{os1973,os1975}, and that has a mass gap \cite{jaffewitten}. One constructive approach starts from Wilson's lattice action on a torus with lattice spacing $\varepsilon$ and takes $\varepsilon\to0$. The bare coupling $g_0(\varepsilon)$ must then go to zero at the rate that asymptotic freedom prescribes, so that the effective coupling at a fixed physical scale stays fixed. This paper treats the first limit only: lattice spacing to zero on a fixed torus. It does not treat the infinite-volume limit or the mass gap (Section~\ref{sec:notgiven}).

\subsection{Ba{\l}aban's programme and the missing Theorem 2}\label{sec:balaban}

Between 1984 and 1989 Ba{\l}aban built a renormalization-group (RG) analysis of lattice gauge theories \cite{balaban95,balaban96,balaban98,balaban99a,balaban99b,balaban102a,balaban102b,balaban109,balaban116,balaban119,balaban122a,balaban122b}. His main output for $d=4$ is Theorem 1 of CMP 122 II: uniform bounds on the effective densities at every RG scale, under the hypothesis that the sequence of effective couplings stays small. The hypothesis was to be discharged by his Theorem 2, which controls the running coupling. The two statements read as follows.

Theorem 2 (CMP 109 (1987), p.\ 259): ``Let $d = 4$, $G = SU(2)$, and let $\gamma$ be a sufficiently small positive constant, then for a sufficiently small positive $g$ there exists a bare coupling constant $g_0 = g_0(\varepsilon, g)$ such that the sequence of the effective coupling constants $g_k$ is contained in the interval $]0, \gamma]$, and $g_K = g$. Moreover, there exist constants $\beta, \beta'$, $0 < \beta \le \beta'$, such that $g_k$ satisfy the inequality $1/g^2 + \beta \log(L^k\varepsilon)^{-1} \le 1/g_k^2 \le 1/g^2 + \beta' \log(L^k\varepsilon)^{-1}$. (0.31)'' Balaban adds that the proof ``will be given in a separate paper''. It was never published (CMP 122 II p.\ 355, verbatim: ``has not been published yet, so we have Theorem 1 with the assumption'').

Theorem 1 (CMP 122 II p.\ 355): ``If the sequence of the effective coupling constants is contained in an interval $]0, \gamma]$ with a sufficiently small positive $\gamma$, then the effective densities $\rho_k$ have the form, and satisfy all the conditions and bounds, described in Sect.\ 2 [III].'' The bound (0.1) there reads $\chi_k \exp[-(1/g_k^2)A(U_k) - E_-|T_\eta|] \le \rho_k \le \exp E_+|T_\eta|$ (corrected reading, \file{BALABAN_EXCERPTS_CHECK} item 3).

Without Theorem 2, the hypothesis of Theorem 1 is an assumption on the unknown coupling flow. Every continuum statement built on Ba{\l}aban's bounds inherits this assumption.

\subsection{The two targets and the main results}\label{sec:main}

The paper has two targets. The first target is a proof of enough of Theorem 2 to remove the coupling hypothesis from Theorem 1. The second target is a finite-torus existence theorem for the continuum limit in compactness form, with the Osterwalder--Schrader properties proved on the limit. Both results are reductions: each holds under named hypotheses, and the paper records the status of every hypothesis.

\subsection{Status conventions}\label{sec:status}

Every result carries a status tag in small capitals. The tags mean the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item \status{proved+verified}: proved in a project file and confirmed by an independent verifier file.
\item \status{proved+verified, conditional on $X$}: the implication is confirmed; $X$ is open or assumed.
\item \status{proved, unverified}: proved in a project file; no verifier has graded it yet.
\item \status{Lean}: formalized in Lean 4 + Mathlib, no \texttt{sorry}, axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound only.
\item \status{verified quote}: read on the page image of Balaban's paper (\file{reports/BALABAN_EXCERPTS_CHECK.md} or a verifier file).
\item \status{numerical, reproduced}: floating-point result reproduced by a verifier. \status{numerical, run by the verifier}: run once, by a verifier. \status{numerical}: not reproduced. None is certified.
\item \status{argued}: a plausibility argument, not a proof. \status{open}: no proof. \status{refuted}: shown false.
\item \status{Balaban}: an input taken from Balaban's published papers, used as stated there.
\item \status{not written}: a proof plan; no project file contains the proof.
\item \status{cited}: a standard result; its text was not re-read. \status{cited, source checked}: the source was read, the transfer to the lattice is standard and not proved here.
\item \status{proved here}: a short proof given in full in this paper (Section~\ref{sec:torus}). \status{hypothesis}: assumed, and owned by a named block. \status{formal}: a formal step whose analytic justification is not written. \status{derived sketch}: a route with steps listed, not a proof.
\item \status{interval enclosure, verified}: a rigorous interval-arithmetic enclosure at a point, rerun by a verifier.
\item \status{certified (interval arithmetic, outward rounding; verified)}: a statement over a continuum of parameters (all $K$, all $j$), proved by an analytic majorant plus a branch-and-bound in interval arithmetic with outward rounding, and confirmed by an independent verifier file. It differs from \status{interval enclosure, verified} (one point) and from \status{numerical} (floating point, no rounding control).
\item \status{verified reduction}: a reduction confirmed by a verifier file; the inputs stay open.
\item \status{draft proof, verification pending}, \status{proved, unverified; verifier running}: \status{proved, unverified}, with an independent verifier started and not yet reported.
\item \status{proved modulo named blocks}: proved given the hypotheses owned by the named blocks.
\end{itemize}
A compound tag such as \status{proved+verified, conditional on R-025 (H2)} combines these meanings. Tags refer to the project files cited next to them. In \status{verified quote}, a quote of a source other than Ba{\l}aban's papers was checked verbatim against a copy of that source in the project. Project files are cited as \file{NAME.md}; a section sign after a file name (for example \file{KAPPA_BOUNDED_VERIFY} \S4) refers to a section of that file, not of this paper. ``Grade $n$'' and ``item $n$'' after a verifier file name refer to the numbered verdicts of that file. The paper as a whole is not verified as one document.
