\section{Hypotheses and open problems}\label{sec:remains}

This section states the hypotheses on which the two main theorems rest, and the open problems. The hypotheses are grouped by kind: the uniform inequality (H2), the fixed-level and structural inputs of Theorem 2w (L-001), the inputs of the sources block, and the readings of Ba{\l}aban's text. The item labels H, 1--13, B1, BH, and B3--B7 are those of the dependency table (Section~\ref{sec:deptable}). Section~\ref{sec:rem-ledger} lists every statement that the two main theorems use and that this paper does not prove: Ba{\l}aban-level statements, external results, our hypotheses, and conventions.

\subsection{The uniform inequality (H2)}\label{sec:rem-cert}

Both routes need one uniform hypothesis.
\begin{hypothesis}[item H; (H2), R-025]
$\rho_2=\sup_{j\ge2}\sup_{K\text{ real}}\|(\oplus_lH_{j-1}(k_l))H_j(K)\|/L^2<L$.
\end{hypothesis}
At $L=13$ this hypothesis is a theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:L046}): $\rho_1(j,K)^2\le10<13$ for all $j\ge1$ (including $\infty$) and all $K$, hence $\rho_2\le10<13$ by \texttt{rho2\_le}. The proof is computer-assisted (interval arithmetic with outward rounding). The same certificate gives (H2) at $L=15,17,21$ (Section~\ref{sec:numr7}). At $L=3,5,9$ we have numerical evidence only. At $L=3$ one point is enclosed: $j=\infty$, $K^*=(2.4035,2.4035,\pi,\pi)$, $\rho_2\in[1.9993,2.1273]$ (Section~\ref{sec:enclosure}). The paper fixes $L=13$ (K-003), so (H2) at other $L$ is not needed.

H-res. The premises of Theorem~\ref{thm:L046} (Section~\ref{sec:certH2}) are: the reading of Ba{\l}aban's (0.4) and the gauge choice; Prop.\ A at $M\ge27$ and $M=\infty$; L-166 (Lemma G) and the bound chain; the correctness of the interval code \texttt{bb\_arb.py}. The gauge-fixing function is a convention: the minimum-norm gauge among the gauges that select one point per residual orbit.

Consequences. Route K: (H2) $\Rightarrow$ ($C1\sigma$) $\Rightarrow$ (B2) (Theorems~\ref{thm:L011}, \ref{thm:L012}). Route B: (H2) and (T1) together give (U) by a possible approach (Section~\ref{sec:routeBinputs}). The cell certificate at $L=13$, mode $M=1$, covers the chamber outside $|K|_\infty\le0.32$ at $j=1$; it is a partial cross-check (Section~\ref{sec:numr8}). Positivity of the early steps $\beta_1(0),\dots,\beta_{j_*}(0)$ at odd $L>11$ is needed only for literal (0.31) at every $K$ (Section~\ref{sec:jstar}).

\subsection{Fixed-level and structural inputs of Theorem 2w}\label{sec:rem-inputs}

\emph{Route K.} All of the following must hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] (B2) $\sup_j\sup_K|\sigma_j^{(2)}|<\infty$, or (B2w) (H-184). (B2) follows from (H2) (item H). Two other implications hold: ($C1\sigma$) + (h) $\Rightarrow$ (B2) is proved, and (H$_A$) $\Rightarrow$ (B2) is a possible approach. The inequality (HS) is false.
\item[2.] (PI) (R-026), probe independence. It holds at each fixed level relative to listed inputs: (I1) (H-207) for the composed fine matrix at every $j$, (I2) (H-208), and (I3), the lattice-symmetry input, for the full hypercubic group. We claim no uniformity in $j$.
\item[3.] (F) (R-027): the lattice log coefficient at constant field equals the continuum one. It is proved (Theorem~\ref{thm:L041}), by proper time, with (L-LL) (H-137) (Theorem~\ref{thm:L040}); the proof needs neither Lemma 2 nor Step C. The continuum side is cited from Savvidy \cite{savvidy1977} (X-005). A separate numerical tension remains: the single-step $\beta_1$ at $L=5$--13 does not follow $c\log L$ (Section~\ref{sec:numr8}). It concerns a different family.
\item[4.] ($M_j$) (R-028): the formal $f^2$ coefficient equals the true one at each fixed level. The inputs are (S1) (H-244) $\mathcal K_t(f)\ge-C_1|f|$, (S2) (H-245) density of states $\le C_2|f|$ near 0, and (S3) (H-247) a relative bound of the level difference, all on the lattice. (S1), (S3), (S2)$|_{t=0}$, and (S2c) (H-246) hold at fixed $j$ given the Peierls input (I3). (I3) holds at every step, with constants independent of the step, given the identification of the one-step objects with the linearized averaging. (G$_{\mathrm{2D}}$) holds for $0<|f|\le((1-c)/100)^2/2$, with gap $2c|f|$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:L028}). ($M_j$) is proved at fixed $j$ relative to listed inputs (Theorem~\ref{thm:L029}); region 1 and the neutral sector are proved in the $o(f^2)$ form, and the proved $f$-range suffices.
\item[5.] V1, the order-$g^0$ completeness of CMP 109 (1.3)--(1.5): $\beta_{j+1}(0)$ is the Maxwell coefficient of the per-step Gaussian determinant. It is proved at fixed level, conditional on (G2) (R-030), on Ba{\l}aban's inductive bounds, and on the declaration that the averaging is (0.4). It is history-free.
\item[6.] (G2) (R-030), per-cell infinite-volume densities. At fixed $j$ the torus densities converge exponentially, and the $p^2$- and $f^2$-coefficients commute with $N\to\infty$. This is proved at fixed $j$ relative to listed inputs, including (Gap$_f$) and $\tilde Q\tilde Q^T>0$. We do not prove a $j$-uniform version.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Route B.} The following hypotheses replace items 1--5.
\begin{itemize}
\item[B1.] (T1) $\sup_j\sup_{q\text{ real}}|c^{(1)}_{T,j}(q)|<\infty$, the plane-wave form of L-071. A possible approach is sketched; the Landau-to-symmetric gauge conversion and Steps 2--5 of Theorem A3 (L-071) for (2.1) are not written.
\item[BH.] (H2) (R-025), that is, item H.
\item[B3.] (DF) $\sup_k|\mathfrak d_F(\theta_*;L^k)|<\infty$, the gauge-fixing difference at the plane wave. With the exact split $\Gamma^{ex}=\gamma_{rec}+\mathfrak d_F$ there is no $Y$-defect; all non-stationarity sits in $\mathfrak d_F$ (Section~\ref{sec:routeB}).
\item[B4.] L\"uscher--Weisz (5.7) \cite{luscherweisz1995}.
\item[B5.] A6 (not R-007): the plane-wave increment equals Ba{\l}aban's $\beta$ up to the expansion point, uniformly in $k$. This is a uniform-in-$k$ input of the same kind as (DF).
\item[B6.] (H0) (H-202), that is, item 7 (Lemma DT (L-005) (i)).
\item[B7.] $\theta_*<1$, so that $c_2\ln(1/\theta_*^2)\ge0$ in $C_B$. This is a trivial condition on the choice of $\theta_*$.
\end{itemize}
Route B also needs item 6 ((G2)), item 7, and items 8 and 10--13. It does not need (PI), ($M_j$), V1; it needs (DF) and A6 instead. In Route B, (U) is a hypothesis; a possible approach derives it from (T1) and (H2).

\emph{Both routes.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[7.] The own-slot bound $|\beta_{k+1}(h,g)-\beta_{k+1}(0)|\le C_\beta g$ on $\ioc{\gamma}^k\times[0,\gamma]$, for every past $h$, monotone or not. It follows from (H0) or from (G-gain) (H-111). (H0) is Ba{\l}aban's smoothness in $g$ with bounds uniform in $j$ and in the history (CMP 109 inductive assumption at odd $L>11$, R-006). ``Uniform in $j$'' is our reading of p.\ 264 and is not printed. Uniformity in the history is not printed. The shooting evaluates $\beta$ on clamped, non-monotone pasts. (G-gain) gives $C_\beta$ directly only on monotone pasts. The running maximum $G_k=\max_{i\le k}g_i$ removes this restriction with no change of constant: the only use of monotonicity is a comparison of bound sizes in L-164, and $G_k$ closes the induction for every past. The proof uses the Lipschitz form on $\ioc{\gamma}$ only. The use of (H0) reduces to Proposition H0-red (L-183), with residue N1--N9. N2 is Lemma G3 (L-174), proved relative to listed inputs (Theorem~\ref{thm:L025}). The rest of the residue (N5, N6 relative to the small-field step of R-013, N8 a reading, N9 a definition, N7 = R-031) is not proved, so R-006 is an input.
\item[8.] L5, history dependence. It is proved relative to listed inputs by Route 2, nested intervals on true trajectories with no clamp (Theorem~\ref{thm:L030}).
\begin{remark}
A clamp proof of Lemmas DS/DT would need $\beta_{k+1}(g_0,\dots,g_k)$ defined and continuous on the whole cube $]0,\gamma]^{k+1}$, and Ba{\l}aban does not supply this. Ba{\l}aban's $\beta_{k+1}$ exists only on histories where his induction has run; that domain must carry (c-pair) + K-018 (S), not only $g_i\in\,]0,\gamma]$. Clamped histories can violate the growth side of (c-pair). True trajectories with all $u_i\ge\gamma^{-2}$ inherit (c-pair) from (a-tail). This is why Route 2 uses nested intervals on true trajectories. Lean covers the one-argument $\beta$ (Lemmas DS, DT, D, E) and the history form (item 13).
\end{remark}
\item[9.] $K$-independence of $\beta_j(g)$. It holds. Item 9 is a volume statement, not a history identity: $K$ enters $\beta_j$ only through the torus size. CMP 119 (2.24), p.\ 249, and p.\ 279 define $\beta_j$ as the whole-lattice coefficient, so the $\beta_j$ of the recursion is $K$-free by definition. Lemmas DS/DT run at one fixed $K$ and never compare trajectories. The window bound needs only (K1) (history-free at zero own coupling), (K2) $C_\beta$ (item 7), and (K3) the window bound (5.1) of Theorem K (L-009). The residual volume question (the finite-volume step equals the whole-lattice step, R-015, R-032) is an input of Theorem 1, not of the shooting. Given (Loc) (H-141), the torus coefficient is within $C_jN_j^6e^{-m_jN_j/2}$ of the whole-lattice one (Proposition~\ref{prop:L024}); exact equality cannot be derived in general. (Loc) is proved (Theorem~\ref{thm:L026}), and the boundary reading and the replaced R-032 are proved under (Vol) (K-032) (Proposition~\ref{prop:L027}).
\item[11.] Odd $L$. All one-loop proofs need odd $L\ge3$ (centred blocks: U3 Lemma 1.2, O5/Prop.\ A, evenness, Lemma 4.1 of LEMMA43). Ba{\l}aban assumes $L$ odd $>11$ throughout CMP 109, so (H0) and $\beta_j(g)$ at $g>0$ exist only there. Theorem 2w (L-001) is stated for odd $L>11$, or with (H0) at the given $L$ as a hypothesis. Numerics: $\beta_1(0)$ up to $L=11$ (Section~\ref{sec:beta1}); (H2) at $L=3,5$ (scans), 7 (face diagonal), 9 (one point); $\rho_1$ at $L=3,\dots,13$. Only $L=13$ is inside Ba{\l}aban's range; there (H2) is a theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:L046}).
\item[12.] SU(2) only. The charged/neutral split of Lemma 5.1 and (W) are SU(2) statements. The value $c=11N^2/(12\pi^2)$ is general, but the chain is not established for $N>2$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why the mass gap is not on this route}\label{sec:rem-gap}

Write $\beta_W$ for the coefficient of $\sum_p(1-\tfrac1N\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}U_p)$ in the Wilson action. Ba{\l}aban's action is $g^{-2}\sum_p(1-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_p)$ with the normalized trace (CMP 109 (0.2)), so $\beta_W=1/g_B^2$. Shen, Zhu, and Zhu \cite{shenzhuzhu2023} prove a mass gap for SU($N$) lattice Yang--Mills with action $N\beta\operatorname{Re}\sum_p\operatorname{Tr}Q_p$ (their (1.2)) when $|\beta|<1/(16(d-1))$ (their (1.3)); with $\beta_W=N^2\beta$, $N=2$, and $d=4$, this is $\beta_W<1/12$. Ba{\l}aban's construction requires $-2(1+\beta_0)^{-1}p_0(g_k)+1+2\kappa(100R_k)^d\le-\tfrac32p_0(g_k)$ (CMP 122 II p.\ 389). With $d=4$ and $R_K\ge1$, this gives $p_0(g_K)>4\kappa\cdot10^8+2$ for every $\beta_0>0$ (twice this at Ba{\l}aban's $\beta_0=1/7$). The unit-scale threshold $g_Kp_0(g_K)$ must be below 2, the largest value of $\|U-1\|$ on SU(2); with $\kappa\ge1$ this gives $\beta_W=1/g_K^2>p_0(g_K)^2/4>4\times10^{16}$ at the unit scale. So the two regimes are separated by at least seventeen orders of magnitude in $\beta_W$. No choice of $L$ narrows this, because $L$ does not enter the condition. A proof of a mass gap needs a mechanism outside the renormalization-group construction, and Ba{\l}aban's papers do not address it.
