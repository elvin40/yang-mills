\subsection{Why the mass gap is not on this route}\label{sec:rem-gap}

Write $\beta_W$ for the coefficient of $\sum_p(1-\tfrac1N\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}U_p)$ in the Wilson action. Ba{\l}aban's action is $g^{-2}\sum_p(1-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_p)$ with the normalized trace (CMP 109 (0.2)), so $\beta_W=1/g_B^2$. Shen, Zhu, and Zhu \cite{shenzhuzhu2023} prove a mass gap for SU($N$) lattice Yang--Mills with action $N\beta\operatorname{Re}\sum_p\operatorname{Tr}Q_p$ (their (1.2)) when $|\beta|<1/(16(d-1))$ (their (1.3)); with $\beta_W=N^2\beta$, $N=2$, and $d=4$, this is $\beta_W<1/12$. Ba{\l}aban's construction requires $-2(1+\beta_0)^{-1}p_0(g_k)+1+2\kappa(100R_k)^d\le-\tfrac32p_0(g_k)$ (CMP 122 II p.\ 389). With $d=4$ and $R_K\ge1$, this gives $p_0(g_K)>4\kappa\cdot10^8+2$ for every $\beta_0>0$ (twice this at Ba{\l}aban's $\beta_0=1/7$). The unit-scale threshold $g_Kp_0(g_K)$ must be below 2, the largest value of $\|U-1\|$ on SU(2); with $\kappa\ge1$ this gives $\beta_W=1/g_K^2>p_0(g_K)^2/4>4\times10^{16}$ at the unit scale. The two regimes are therefore separated by at least seventeen orders of magnitude in $\beta_W$, and no choice of $L$ narrows this, because $L$ does not enter the condition. A proof of a mass gap needs a mechanism outside the renormalization-group construction, and Ba{\l}aban's papers do not address it.
