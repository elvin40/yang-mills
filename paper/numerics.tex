\section{Numerical results and the computer-assisted proof of (H2)}\label{sec:numerics}

This section collects the numerical results that bear on the hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:flow}. Sections~\ref{sec:beta1}--\ref{sec:routeKnum} give the one-loop increments and the per-level quantities of the Route K proof. Sections~\ref{sec:numH2}--\ref{sec:enclosure} give the values of (H2) (R-025), the one-step values, and one rigorous point enclosure. Section~\ref{sec:certH2} proves (H2) at $L=13$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:L046}). Section~\ref{sec:numr7} extends the proof to $L=15,17,21$ and gives the numerics of the 2D gap and of the neutral sector. Section~\ref{sec:numr8} treats the single-step $\beta_1$, a second certificate at $L=13$ on part of the Brillouin zone, and the numerics of (L-LL) (H-137) and of the free constant of (C) (H-057). Section~\ref{sec:numr9} gives the seam test of (C), (L-LL) at smaller flux, and the converged-grid values of $\beta_1$. Section~\ref{sec:cert} gives the design of the cell certificate.

All values are for SU(2), in infinite volume, in Ba{\l}aban's normalized-trace units. Except for Sections~\ref{sec:enclosure}, \ref{sec:certH2}, the first paragraph of Section~\ref{sec:numr7}, and the cell certificate of Section~\ref{sec:numr8}, all values are floating-point values without rounding control; they enter no proof. Only odd $L$ is inside the proofs. The values at $L=2,4$ use corner blocks and are outside them.

\subsection{One-step values}\label{sec:numonestep}

By Section~\ref{sec:onestep}, $\rho_1^2<L$ implies (H2). The measured one-step values are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item $L=3$: $\rho_1^2=3.10>3$. The one-step route fails (Section~\ref{sec:refuted}); the two-step map is needed.
\item $L=5$: $\rho_1=1.860$ ($j=1$, Wilson fine kernel, dense), $1.9565$ ($j=2$), $1.963$ ($j=\infty$). So $\rho_1^2\le3.853<5$ and $\rho_1^2/L=0.771$, as floating-point values.
\item $L=7$: $\rho_1\ge2.032$ (diagonal only), $\rho_1^2/L\approx0.59$.
\item $L=13$: measured $\sup\rho_1^2=4.37$; Theorem~\ref{thm:L046} gives $\sup_{j,K}\rho_1^2\le10<13$.
\end{itemize}
So at $L\ge5$ the one-step map suffices for L-011, as far as measured. For a computer-assisted proof this replaces $L^8$ blocks per $K$ (two-step map) by $L^4$ blocks per $K$ (625 times fewer at $L=5$).

\subsection{A rigorous point enclosure at $L=3$}\label{sec:enclosure}

The program \file{numerics/h2cert_proto.py} (\file{H2_CERTIFY.md}) uses ball arithmetic (python-flint). At $L=3$, $j=\infty$, and the face point $K^*=(2.4035,2.4035,\pi,\pi)$ near the argmax of Section~\ref{sec:numH2}, it gives
\[
\rho_2(\infty,K^*)\in[1.9993,\,2.1273]<3 .
\]
The width comes from the umklapp tail and scales as $N^{-4}$ in the truncation $N$. At $N=12$ the program gives $[1.5475,\,2.5792]$, which contains the floating-point value $2.063361$, in agreement with the $N^{-4}$ scaling. At $N=6$ the program stops with an assertion instead of returning a wrong interval. The enclosure holds under three premises P1--P3 on the input data. P1 states that the loop-family coefficients of the (0.4) row are exact rationals $k/648$, $k$ even; it is proved there. P3 is the fidelity of the loop family to Ba{\l}aban's paper, that is, Convention~\ref{conv:avg}. Further results:
\begin{itemize}
\item One-step factorization: $\rho_2(j,K)\le\rho_1(j,K)\cdot\max_l\rho_1(j-1,k_l)$, exactly. So two-step work is needed only where the right side is $\ge L$, near the face diagonal.
\item The Oishi--Rump bound gives $\|\mathfrak B^{-1}\|\in[42.7,\,43.6]$ at $K^*$. The values 177 and 195 of Section~\ref{sec:convergence} use the full $\Delta$ instead of $\tfrac14\Delta$; this factor reconciles them. No $\|\mathfrak B^{-1}\|$ enters $\rho_2$.
\item The enclosure of the umklapp tail holds for $M=\infty$. For finite $M$ its lower bound is 0, with class-wise factors $(\pi/2)^{12}$ for two or more large components.
\item A uniform Lipschitz grid is not a practical route: $\Lambda_K\approx2.8\cdot10^4$ would need about $10^{13}$ cells. The replacement is a per-cell invertibility test with a mean-value enclosure.
\end{itemize}
This is one point at one level. It does not bound the supremum over the Brillouin zone or over $j$. Section~\ref{sec:certH2} does this at $L=13$.

\subsection{(H2) at $L=13$}\label{sec:certH2}

Here $\rho_1(j,K)=\|H_j(K)\|/L$, with $H_j(K)$ the one-step gauge-fixed minimizer map of Section~\ref{sec:H2}. The map depends on Ba{\l}aban's averaging operator, which we fix as follows.

\begin{convention}[reading of the averaging operator (0.4) of CMP 109]\label{conv:avg}
The averaging operator is defined by the path family of \cite[(0.4)]{balaban109} as read in \file{NUMERICS} \S5, with the gauge choice fixed there. A different reading of the path family in (0.4) would change $H_j$. Theorem~\ref{thm:L046} and Proposition~\ref{prop:H2other} are statements about $H_j$ defined by this convention.
\end{convention}

\begin{theorem}[(H2) at $L=13$]\label{thm:L046}
Assume Convention~\ref{conv:avg}. For $L=13$, every level $j\ge1$ including $j=\infty$, and every coarse momentum $K\in\BZ$,
\[
\rho_1(j,K)^2\le10<13 .
\]
Hence $\rho_2\le\sup_{j,K}\rho_1(j,K)^2\le10<L$ by the factorization $\rho_2\le\rho_1(j)\max_l\rho_1(j-1,k_l)$ (Section~\ref{sec:onestep}). That is, (H2) (R-025) holds at $L=13$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (sketch; the full argument is in \file{H1_ANALYTIC.md} \S0--\S8)]
Write $s=L^{-2}$, $k_l=(K+2\pi l)/L$, $M=L^{j-1}$, $d^M$ for the Prop.\ A symbol at level $M$, $P_\perp$ for the projection onto $\hat K^\perp$, and $T$, $T_2$, $\sigma$, $Z=(P_\perp TP_\perp)^+$ as in \file{H1_ANALYTIC} \S1.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Exact split. $\rho_1(j,K)^2L^2=\lambda_{\max}\bigl(ZT_2Z+cc^\dagger/(\sigma|\hat K|^4)\bigr)$, with $c^\dagger=\hat K^\dagger(1-TZ)$. The first term is the transverse part, the second the orthogonal pure-gauge part.
\item Three exact lemmas. L-264 (Lemma S): the averaging symbol is $E_q(k_l)=E^0_l-\hat Kg_l^\dagger$, with $E^0_l$ diagonal (block average times straight path), $|E^0_{l,\mu\mu}|$ built from $a_{l,\rho}=\sin(K_\rho/2)/(L\sin(k_{l,\rho}/2))$, and $\sum_l\prod_\rho a_{l,\rho}^2=1$ (Fej\'er). Tower identity: $d^{ML}_\mu(K)=L^{-2}\sum_lw_{l,\mu}d^M_\mu(k_l)$. Hence $P_\perp TP_\perp=P_\perp\operatorname{diag}(d^{ML}(K))P_\perp$, and $Z$ is the next-level kernel of Prop.\ A. L-166 (Lemma G): a closed form of the gauge row $g_l$ from the path average, so the averaging operator is explicit.
\item Majorant. L-283 (Lemma T) (transverse): $\lambda_{\max}(ZT_2Z)/L^2\le\gamma_LF(K)$, with $F(K)=1/(|K|^2\underline d(K))$ and $\gamma_L=\operatorname{sinc}(\pi/2L)^{-2}$. This follows from $T_2\le d_{\max}T$, $ZTZ=Z$, $d_{\max}\le L^2\gamma_L/|K|^2$, and $\|Z\|\le1/\underline d$. Lemma Gauge (L-180): the gauge part is $\le(1+\tau)^2/(|\hat K|^2S_L)$, with explicit sums $V$, $U$ and $\tau=(V+|\hat K|U)/\underline d$. So $\rho_1(j,K)^2\le B_L(K):=\gamma_LF(K)+(1+\tau)^2/(|\hat K|^2S_L)$. By Cauchy--Schwarz $F\le F_{CS}$, a closed form made of one-dimensional factors in each $K_\rho$.
\item Uniformity in $j$. $B_L$ does not depend on $j$. For every $M\in\{1,L,L^2,\dots,\infty\}$ the fine symbol satisfies $d^M\le1/|\tilde q^M_0|^2\le1/|\hat k|^2$, because the Fej\'er weights sum to 1. The coarse symbol $d^{ML}$ dominates the truncated $M=\infty$ sum termwise. The proof uses no monotonicity in $j$ and no O5 rate.
\item Branch-and-bound. $B_L$ is invariant under the 384 signed permutations of $K$ by construction. So the supremum over the sorted chamber $0\le K_1\le\dots\le K_4\le\pi$ is the supremum over $\BZ$. The program \file{numerics/h1analytic/bb_arb.py} encloses $\gamma_LF_{CS}+\bar G_L$ on boxes $K=\pi u$ with exact dyadic $u$. It evaluates every one-dimensional value in arb (python-flint), and it rounds outward by one ulp after every floating-point operation, with Higham bounds on every sum. Result: $\sup_{\text{chamber}}B_{13}\le9.9995277$, on 25{,}169 boxes with 8{,}075 gauge evaluations and no failed leaf. Boxes that contain $K=0$ are enclosed directly; $\rho_1(j,0)=1$.\qedhere
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
\emph{Margins.} The measured $\sup\rho_1^2$ is 4.37. The majorant is tight to 2.6\% at $|K|\approx1.5$: $\min B_{13}/\rho_1^2=1.026$ over 896 cases and a local search. The program refines boxes only until each is $\le10$, so its margin to 10 is $5\cdot10^{-5}$; its margin to the threshold 13 is 23\%.

\emph{Consistency checks.} The split, Lemmas S and G, the tower identity, L-283 (Lemma T), and Lemma Gauge agree with the production objects to $\le5.3\cdot10^{-13}$, and $B_{13}\ge\rho_1^2$ holds at 896 $(j,K)$ cases. A second construction of $H_j(K)$ from the definitions (position-space (0.4) paths, sparse KKT, ghost-domain gauge) reproduces the production $\rho_1$ to $5.8\cdot10^{-9}$ ($10^{-14}$ with the same $d$ symbol). The two parts of the split agree to $3\cdot10^{-8}$, and the conventions of L-264 (Lemma S) agree to $1.2\cdot10^{-13}$. The invariance of $\rho_1$ under the 384 signed permutations (P5a, P5b) holds; the analytic proof does not need it, the cell sweep does.

\emph{Lean.} \file{lean/YmLean/Tower.lean} formalizes the algebraic core of the majorant: the tower identity in weighted form, the Rayleigh and rank-one bounds, \texttt{majorant\_core}, and \texttt{lemmaT\_core}. \texttt{BlockNorm.rho2\_le} is the one-step to two-step factorization.

\begin{remark}[inputs of the proof]
Besides Convention~\ref{conv:avg}, the proof uses the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item Prop.\ A at $M\ge27$ and $M=\infty$. Two implementations of the same formula agree, and an independent recursion agrees at $M=3,9$.
\item L-166 (Lemma G) and the chain of bounds, proved in \file{H1_ANALYTIC} and checked numerically at points. The Fourier and umklapp derivation, Lemmas S and G, and the identification of the split are not formalized in Lean.
\item The program \texttt{bb\_arb.py}, read line by line and compared with a floating-point program on 30 boxes. It is not formally verified. It relies on python-flint arb and on IEEE correctly rounded $+,-,\times,\div,\sqrt{\ }$.
\end{itemize}
The cell certificate of Section~\ref{sec:numr8} gives an independent bound at $j=1$ outside $|K|_\infty\le0.32$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The single-step $\beta_1$, the cell certificate, (L-LL), and the condition (C)}\label{sec:numr8}

\paragraph{The single-step $\beta_1$ against $c\log L$.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The quantity. The single-step $\beta_1(L)$ is one step from the unit lattice with block size $L$, for SU(2) at $p\to0$. Each $L$ is a different constraint family. This is not the fixed-$L$ composed family of Theorem 3.1 of \file{BETA_FP_H.md}, which concerns $\beta_\infty=\lim_k[\mathcal C(L^{k+1})-\mathcal C(L^k)]$ at fixed odd $L$.
\item The comparison value. $c=11N^2/(12\pi^2)=0.3715$ at $N=2$ is the theoretical coefficient, not a fit. No computation gives $\beta_\infty$.
\item The data. The converged values are in the table below. All local slopes are far below $c$, and they decrease with $L$. So the data do not show that the first-step constant $\beta_1-c\log L$ tends to a limit.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[a possible explanation of the slopes]
A possible approach (\file{SOURCES_BETA1_F_TENSION.md}, Proposition 3.1) splits the single-step increment as $\beta_1(L)-\beta_1(L')=c\log(L/L')-[B_c(L)-B_c(L')]$. Here $B_c(L)$ is the constant of the one-step coarse bracket, the constant-field one-loop term of the tree kernel $\Delta_1^{(L)}$. The input (F) (R-027) concerns only the fine bracket and gives the term $c\log L$; it says nothing about $B_c(L)$. In this approach $\beta_1(L)=c\log L+C+o(1)$ holds if and only if $B_c(L)$ converges, and the $\beta_1$ data neither contradict nor support (F). The local coefficient $dB_c/d\log L$ is $0.230$, $0.257$, $0.291$, $0.330$ for $L=5\to7$, $7\to9$, $9\to11$, $11\to13$ (\file{SOURCES_BCL_TEST.md}), and it shows no saturation. The region $|K|\sim1/L$ carries less than 1\% of the increment of $B_c$. At $f=0$ the scaled tree kernel tends to $\kappa^2H_\infty$, relative to U3 Theorem 4.1. The open part is the bulk behaviour of the single-step soft vertices.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Design of the cell certificate and the tail level $j_0$}\label{sec:cert}
At $L=13$ the analytic proof of Section~\ref{sec:certH2} needs no tail level $j_0$, no bound on $\|\mathfrak B^{-1}\|$, and no Lipschitz constant. The cell certificate below gives an independent bound at $L=13$ on part of the chamber (Section~\ref{sec:numr8}). A proof of (H2) by cells is a computer-assisted proof of substantial size. \file{H2_LARGE_L} \S4 gives the design. It has three parts.

(a) Tail $j\ge j_0$. From the resolvent identity, $\rho_2(j)\le R_\infty+(\rho_1^2/a_*)(\delta_{j-2}+\delta_{j-1})$. Here $R_\infty=\rho_2$ at $j=\infty$, $a_*=\inf_Ka_j(K)$ with $\|[\mathfrak B^{-1}]_{f,f}\|=1/a_j(K)$ exactly, and $\delta_m=\sup_K\|\Delta_{L^m}-\Delta_\infty\|$ (O5 decay). The 2-norm supremum of $\delta_m$ is at the corner: $\delta=179,\,92.0,\,18.7,\,2.39,\,0.317$ ($M=1,3,9,27,81$). The value of $a_*$ at $L=5$ is $0.0107$. With these inputs, $j_0\approx8$ ($L=3$) and $\approx6$ ($L=5$). With the O6 coercivity constant, $j_0=25$, which cannot be used in practice; with $\|\mathfrak B^{-1}\|\le b(L)\sim10^{19}$ one needs $j\gtrsim45$.

(b) Levels $2\le j<j_0$ and $j=\infty$. One needs a certified supremum over the Brillouin zone of $\|H_{(2),j}(K)\|$ (at $L\ge5$: of $\|H_j(K)\|$, Section~\ref{sec:onestep}). Plan: interval arithmetic with local mean-value enclosures on boxes in the sorted chamber (1/384 of the Brillouin zone), $4\times4$ and $5\times5$ verified inverses, and one $4\times4$ $\lambda_{max}$ per $K$. At $L=3$ this needs about $2.5\cdot10^3$ boxes of half-width 0.05, and the analytic form of $\Delta$ near $K=0$, where $\rho_2(0)=1$. At $j=\infty$ one also needs a certified tail bound for the Prop.\ A sum ($l_{max}$ truncation; observed effect $4\cdot10^{-4}$). No explicit global Lipschitz constant is known: the strip width of A0/O5 is existential, and the constant that can be made explicit is $\sim10^{40}$. Monotonicity in $j$ is not proved and is not used.

(c) Inputs to certify first: $\|\mathfrak B_j(K)^{-1}\|\le B_{num}$ for all $j$, $K$ (the O6 constant $b(L)$ is too large to use), the O5 constant $C_0$ explicitly (or certified $\delta_m$), and certified $a_*$, $\rho_1$, $R_\infty$.

At $L=13$ the cell certificate runs as a sweep (\file{numerics/h1cert_cell2.py}) over the sorted chamber, which is valid by P5.
