## 0. The problem in one paragraph

LEMMAS.md Lemma B defines an inclined gauge-covariant averaging Q_R on Λ' = 5R_θZ⁴ and proves gauge covariance (B1), reflection covariance (B2) and the common coarse frame (B3). The open step B4 (LEMMAS.md, "What Lemma B does NOT give") is: the density after the inclined first step belongs to the same class of effective densities as after an ordinary first step, and the subsequent ordinary steps contract the difference. This text (1) states B4 precisely as a claim about Balaban's objects, (2) reduces it to a finite list of Balaban estimates and decides for each whether the inclined version holds, (3) maps the template's mechanism step by step, and (4) gives the ledger. The main new facts are: the inclined trajectory has only the symmetry group C_4 × B_2 (order 32) inside the hyperoctahedral group B_4 (order 384); this group allows exactly three independent marginal gauge couplings instead of one; the template's 2D mechanism that forces isotropy of the marginal term by quarter turns does not exist in 4D; and the template's rotation argument does not need the inclined limit to equal the ordinary limit, so this anisotropy is a complication of the class, not an obstruction, provided the inclined trajectory stays admissible.

### 1.3 The inclined first step T_R

### 1.4 The symmetry group of an inclined trajectory [proved here]

Let G_R := {g ∈ B_4 : g Λ' = Λ'} = Stab_{B_4}(Λ'). numerics/inclined_stabilizer.py computes: |G_R| = 32, with 16 elements of determinant −1. G_R = C_4 × B_2: the quarter turns in the (1,2)-plane (which commute with R_θ) times the signed permutations of (x_3,x_4). No reflection in the (1,2)-plane is in G_R: the reflection s maps Λ'_θ to Λ'_{−θ} ≠ Λ'_θ (LEMMAS.md B-geo-3), and the diagonal swap x_1 ↔ x_2 maps (3,4,0,0) to (4,3,0,0) ∉ Λ'.

The whole trajectory (Z⁴, Λ', 5LΛ', …) is invariant exactly under G_R, because the later coarse lattices are coarsenings of Λ' in the coarse frame and the fine lattice is Z⁴. So every Balaban symmetry statement ("Euclidean transformations of the lattice T^{(j)}", "leaving the partition invariant") holds, after T_R, only for r ∈ G_R.

Consequence for the marginal term. The marginal gauge-invariant local operators of dimension 4 are the quadratic forms C on Λ²R⁴ applied to the field strength, Σ C_{μν,ρσ} tr F_{μν}F_{ρσ}, with C invariant under the symmetry group. Script result: the space of G_R-invariant symmetric bilinear forms on Λ²R⁴ has dimension exactly 3 (for B_4 it is 1). An explicit basis: the plaquette-class sums O_12 = Σ tr F_{12}², O_34 = Σ tr F_{34}², O_mix = Σ_{a∈{1,2}, i∈{3,4}} tr F_{ai}². No ε-tensor (θ-term) form is allowed, because G_R contains reflections. The one-parameter family c_12 c_34 = c_mix² inside this 3-space is a metric rescaling diag(α,α,γ,γ) of the isotropic action; the remaining direction is a genuinely Lorentz-violating marginal coupling (k_F-type). Search over all integer matrices A with AA^T = mI, m ∈ {5, 9, 13, 17, 25, 29, 37}, excluding finite-order rotations: the minimum number of invariant forms is 3; no inclined sublattice of these sizes forces isotropy. Mirror symmetry of the three couplings. Let σ' := R_{2θ} s, the reflection across the coarse x'_1-axis (the line at angle θ in the (1,2)-plane). σ' preserves Λ' but not Z⁴. ### 1.5 What "same class" and "difference contracted" must mean

(a) "Same class" cannot mean 𝒟_1 literally. Balaban's class contains the symmetry assumption (p. 263) for the full group of the lattice T^{(k)}, which after T_R is B_4 in the coarse frame; the inclined density has only G_R. And the β-function definition (1.22) presupposes one coefficient, which rests on the B_4 reduction (Sect. 5, see E9 below); with G_R there are three.

Definition (reduced class). 𝒟̃_k := the set of densities on the k-th coarse lattice of the inclined trajectory that satisfy Balaban's inductive assumptions (CMP 109 Sect. 1, CMP 119 Sect. 2) with the modifications
(M1) geometry at scales j ≥ 1 in the coarse frame, at scale 0 in the fine frame (as in (R5));
(M2) the Euclidean symmetry assumption replaced by G_R-invariance; the covariances (2.29), (2.32), (5.2)–(5.4) hold for r ∈ G_R only;
(M3) the marginal term −(1/g_k²)A^η(U_k) replaced by −Σ_{P∈{12,34,mix}} (1/g_{k,P}²) A^η_P(U_k), where A_P is the Wilson sum over plaquettes in the plane class P; the counterterms −β_j A^η in (0.23) replaced by −Σ_P β_{j,P} A^η_P; (1.22) replaced by three second moments of the G_R-covariant transverse tensor (one per plane class);
(M4) all bounds ((1.18), (0.25), (0.29), (2.31), the regularity constants) unchanged in form, with constants allowed to depend on R;
(M5) admissible couplings: g_{k,P} ∈ ]0,γ] for all P and |g_{k,P}²/g_{k,P'}² − 1| ≤ a_0 with a_0 small (near-isotropy).

(b) "Difference contracted" must mean the template's (5.2): a weighted discrepancy u of the non-marginal data (localized terms E^{(j)}, V^{(j)}, R^{(j)}, B^{(j)}, large-field activities, in Balaban's norms) satisfies u' ≤ q u + (forcing) with q < 1 per ordinary step, and the forcing from the history difference decays geometrically. The marginal couplings are not contracted. In Balaban's bounds, the natural contraction rate is (0.29): a term created at scale j and viewed at scale k carries (L^jη)^{4+α} = L^{−(k−j)(4+α)} per j-cube, i.e. L^{−(k−j)α} per k-cube. So the expected q is L^{−α} with Balaban's α > 0, not the template's L^{−2+υ}.

(c) What the template's rotation argument actually compares. This is the decisive point and it is in the template's text. The field is microscopic: template (1.3), ϕ_N(f) = B_N a_N² Σ f(a_N x) q_x. Prop 10.2 (line 4620) [quoted]: "Finally, let O_+ be the rotation with cosine 3/5 and sine 4/5. The plane limits obtained with regulator orientations 1 and O_+² have identical finite-dimensional laws when tested in the same physical coordinates." Proof (lines 4699–4707) [quoted]: "Put one microscopic regulator in the standard axes and the other in axes O_+². In their respective axes use the inclined first steps O_+ and O_− = O_+^{−1}. Their frames then agree, because O_+²O_− = O_+ […]. Both regulators have the same β_N, a_N, B_N and compatible periods." So both laws are laws of the same microscopic field under the same microscopic measure in two orientations; the inclined blockings are only the device that computes them. No comparison between an inclined and an ordinary trajectory is used for rotation invariance (that comparison appears only in Part II, Prop 14.4 (14.13), for canonical normalization, which Yang–Mills does not have; LEMMAS.md ledger item 5). The scalar normalizations are the ordinary B_N for both (10.7), "Reflection covariance of the two inclined prescriptions makes the multipliers in Equation (10.7) identical" (line 4717).

Translation to the σ'-language [proved here, elementary]. Write C_1 = (fine Z⁴, inclined +θ, ordinary steps in frame R_θ) and C_2 = (fine R_{2θ}Z⁴, inclined −θ in its own axes, ordinary steps). By LEMMAS.md B2–B3, C_2 = σ'(C_1) with σ' = R_{2θ}s: s maps C_1 to (Z⁴, −θ), and R_{2θ} maps that to C_2. The microscopic measure (Wilson action on Z⁴) is s-invariant; the measure of C_2 is the R_{2θ}-image of the measure of C_1. Let L_1 be the limit law of the gauge-invariant smeared observables computed along C_1 and L_2 along C_2. Exact covariance gives L_2 = (R_{2θ})_* L_1. The template step "L_1 = L_2" therefore gives (R_{2θ})_* L_1 = L_1. At the level of the first-step densities: ρ_2 = σ'ρ_1 exactly (both are densities on Λ'); the ordinary steps are σ'-covariant (they act in the coarse frame, where σ' is a lattice reflection); so the claim L_1 = L_2 is the claim that T_1^k ρ_1 and σ'T_1^k ρ_1 define the same observables in the limit k → ∞. Their marginal data (three couplings) are identical by §1.4 (σ'-invariance of the G_R-invariant forms); their scalar data are identical by B2; only the non-marginal data differ. This is the exact analogue of the template's "their scalar field normalizations agree. After the first step, common regular blockings erase the difference of their remaining data" (Figure 2 caption, lines 4665–4668).

So, for Yang–Mills, the anisotropy of §1.4 does not have to be removed. It must be carried (M3), and the inclined trajectory must remain admissible (M5).

### 1.6 Statement of Lemma B4

Lemma B4 (claim, not proved). Let ρ_0 = exp[−(1/g_0²)A^ε(U) − E] be Balaban's initial density on T_ε (CMP 119 Theorem 1, p. 262). Let 𝒟̃_k be the reduced class of §1.5. Then:
(B4-i) [membership] There is γ_R > 0 such that for g_0 ∈ ]0,γ_R], T_R ρ_0 ∈ 𝒟̃_1, with one-step data E^{(1)}, R^{(1)}, B^{(1)} satisfying (1.18), (2.31) with constants E_0(R), κ, and with three one-loop increments β_{1,P}(0), P ∈ {12, 34, mix}, defined by the G_R-covariant version of (1.22).
(B4-ii) [propagation] T_1 maps 𝒟̃_k into 𝒟̃_{k+1} with constants independent of k, as long as the couplings stay in the admissible set (M5). (This is Balaban's Theorem 1 for the reduced class.)
(B4-iii) [comparison and contraction] There are a norm u on the non-marginal data of 𝒟̃_k, a constant q < 1, and constants C, r_0 < 1 such that for ρ, ρ' ∈ 𝒟̃_k with the same marginal couplings and scalar normalizations, u(T_1ρ, T_1ρ') ≤ q u(ρ,ρ') + C r_0^{k}, and the marginal couplings of T_1ρ, T_1ρ' differ by at most C u(ρ,ρ') + C r_0^k. (Template (5.2); the r_0^k term is the history forcing ε_h, template (4.3).)
(B4-iv) [admissibility] Along the inclined trajectory started at the bare coupling g_0(ε, g) of the ordinary shooting (CMP 109 Theorem 2, assumed as in Theorem 1 of CMP 122 II), the three couplings satisfy (M5) for all k ≤ K, uniformly in K.
(B4-v) [sources] The exact transport of sources for the chosen gauge-invariant observables through T_R and T_1 (template Prop 9.1) holds in 𝒟̃ with the same bounds.

Proposition (rotation by 2θ, conditional) [proved here modulo B4 and LEMMAS.md ledger items 1, 2, 5]. Assume B4-i…v, the fixed-torus continuum limit of the ordinary scheme (LEMMAS.md ledger items 1–2), and the fixed-torus moment bounds. Then δ_{k+1} = δ_k + g_k²(Λ_k δ_k + O(g_k²|δ_k|)), with Λ_k a 2×2 matrix determined by the one-loop response of the step-k vacuum polarization to an anisotropic background action (the same Gaussian objects as THEOREM2.md §8, differentiated with respect to the anisotropy). Along Balaban's trajectory 1/g_k² ≈ 1/g² + β(K−k), so Σ_{k≤K} g_k² ≈ (1/β) log K diverges. Hence: if an eigenvalue λ of the limiting Λ_∞ is positive, |δ_K| grows like K^{λ/β} and (M5) fails at the terminal scale for large K; if all eigenvalues are ≤ 0, |δ_K| ≤ C|δ_1| and (M5) holds with a_0 = O(g²|δ_1|), where δ_1 = the anisotropy of the inclined one-loop increment, an O(1) number depending only on the cell geometry. The metric-type combination (a rescaling diag(α,α,γ,γ)) has vanishing universal one-loop running in the continuum (it is a coordinate change), so its eigenvalue is expected to be 0 up to scheme corrections summing to a finite amount; the genuinely Lorentz-violating combination has a nonzero universal one-loop anomalous dimension whose sign I do not know and do not assert. Both numbers are computable by the lattice perturbation theory recipe of THEOREM2.md §8 extended to an anisotropic background action. This is the one place where the inclined construction could fail for a reason that has no template analogue. Verdict: open, computable.
