## 1. Judgment on the pointer CMP 122 II (1.73)–(1.75)

CMP 122 II p. 380 (PDF 26), checked on the page image [V: page image]. The three inequalities read: "|T′_k(X,(U,J))F| = |T′_k(X,(U,0))e^σF| ≤ T′_k(X,(U,0))|e^σF| ≤ (T′_k(X,(U,0))1) sup e^{|σ|}|F|, (1.73)", "|T′_k(X,(U,J))1| … ≥ (T′_k(X,(U,0))1)e^{−2 sup|σ|}, (1.74)", "|(T′_k(X,(U,J))1)^{−1}T′_k(X,(U,J))F| ≤ e^{3 sup|σ|} sup|F|. (1.75)", where "σ is the small term of the first order in A′, J". They are positivity bounds for a large-field integration operation; they bound a normalized expectation by sup|F|.

Judgment (one line): (1.73)–(1.75) carry no information on the f-dependence of Q̃_f; they do not mention the averaging, the background, or any kernel entry, so this file does not use them and works from CMP 109 (0.4), CMP 98 (42), (124) and the constant-flux background instead [V: page image].

## 2. Balaban's averaging: the printed definitions used

All quotes checked on page images.

(B1) CMP 109 p. 253 (PDF 5), (0.4) [V: page image]:
 "Ū(c) = M(U, c) = exp[ i Σ_{x∈B(c_−)} L^{−d} Σ_{Γ∈G(c_−,x)} 1/|G(c_−,x)| Σ_{Γ′∈G(c_+,x′)} 1/|G(c_+,x′)| × (1/i) log U(Γ ∪ [x,x′] ∪ (−Γ′) ∪ (−c)) ] U(c). (0.4)"
 CMP 109 p. 252 (PDF 4) [V: page image]: "For a point x ∈ B(y) we take a family G(y, x) of shortest contours with the initial point at y, and the final point at x." G(y,x) is generated by all permutations of the coordinate directions with n_μ ≠ 0, "|n_μ| ≤ L − 1/2" [as printed; for odd L this means |n_μ| ≤ (L−1)/2]. "For a bond c and a point x ∈ B(c_−), let [x, x′] denote a contour which is obtained by a parallel transport of c to the point x."
 Consequence used below: each loop ℓ = Γ ∪ [x,x′] ∪ (−Γ′) ∪ (−c) is a closed lattice path of length ≤ 2·4·(L−1)/2 + 2L = 6L − 4 in d = 4, contained in B(c_−) ∪ B(c_+) ∪ c. The set of loops entering Ū(c) is finite and depends only on the coarse bond c and the block geometry.

(0.10)"; averaged contour variables "U(y,x) = M({U(Γ)}_{Γ∈G(y,x)}). (0.11)" And CMP 109 p. 254 (PDF 6), (0.12): Ū(c) = exp[i Σ_{x∈B(c_−)} L^{−d} (1/i) log U(c_−,x)U([x,x′])U(x′,c_+)U(−c)] U(c) [V: page image; the text layer of (0.12) is garbled, the image is clear]. (B3) CMP 98 p. 23 (PDF 7), (42), the one-step averaging of the earlier papers [V: page image]: "Ū_c = exp[ i Σ_{x∈B(c_−)} L^{−d} (1/i) log U(Γ_{c,x})U(c)^{−1} ] U(c), c ⊂ Ω^{(1)}. (42)" CMP 109 (0.4) is this formula with the single loop Γ_{c,x} replaced by the uniform mean over the symmetric loop family.

(B4) CMP 98 p. 22 (PDF 6), (33): "g(z) = Σ_{n≥0} (−1)^n/(n+1)! z^n = (e^{−z} − 1)/(−z) for z ≠ 0, g(0) = 1"; p. 23 (PDF 7): "the function g^{−1}(z) = 1/g(z) is an analytic function in a neighborhood of 0, more exactly for z ≠ 2kπi, k = ±1, ±2, …" (34); (38): "(1/i) log e^{iX}e^{iY} = Y + g^{−1}(−i ad_Y)X + 𝓕(X;Y)"; (41): "(1/i) log e^{iX+iY}e^{−iY} = g(−i ad_Y)X + O(|X|²)" [V: page image].

(B5) CMP 98 p. 36 (PDF 20), (124), the linearization of the one-step averaging at a background V_0 [V: page image]. In words: (Q(V_0)A)_c is the main term (Q_0A)_c = Σ_{x∈B(c_−)} L^{−(d+1)}(R_{0,c_−}A)([x,x′]) (125), plus four correction terms. Each correction term is a block sum, with weights L^{−(d+1)} or L^{−d}, of transported fluctuations (R_{0,c_−}A)(Γ_{c_−,x}), (R_{0,c}A)([x,x′]), (R_{0,c}(R_{0,c+}A))(Γ_{c+,x′}), (R_{0,c_−}A)(c), multiplied by one of the operator factors g(−i ad_Y)g^{−1}(−i ad_{Y_x}) − 1, g(−i ad_Y)g^{−1}(i ad_{Y_x})e^{−i ad_Y} − 1, e^{i ad_Y} − g(−i ad_Y)Σ_x L^{−d}g^{−1}(i ad_{Y_x}). Here (p. 34–35, (114), (117)) Y_x = (1/i) log V_0(Γ_{c,x} ∪ (−c)) is the background loop angle and Y = Σ_x L^{−d}Y_x [text layer, l. 900–911; formula images p. 34–35 not reopened]. Balaban's comment, p. 36: "The remaining terms are small because the functions g(−z), g^{−1}(z), e^{iz} are equal to 1 for z = 0." Prop. 3, p. 36: "the function Q(V_0, A) = (1/i) log V̄_1 is an analytic function of A satisfying the equalities and bounds (122)–(124)." So Balaban writes the coefficients of the linear form explicitly as background transports R_0 and analytic functions of the background loop angles. He does not discuss their dependence on a parameter of the background. That dependence is what §5 supplies.

(B6) CMP 99 p. 393 (PDF 5), (3.15) [V: page image]: "Q_j(U) = Q(Ū^{j−1})·…·Q(Ū)Q(U), (3.15) where Q(V) is given by the explicit formula (124) in [5]. These definitions extend straightforwardly to configurations U, A with values in the complexified group G^c and algebra g^c, see Sect. E in [5]." So the j-step linearization is the composition of one-step linearizations at the successive averaged backgrounds. This is what makes "one step, then each of the j steps" the right structure.
