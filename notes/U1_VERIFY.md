### 2.4 Prop. 5.1 [CORRECTED]

What is proved. At fixed k the constrained gauge-fixed form at a = 0 is positive with a gap (Balaban's γ_0 at U = 1; measured 0.085 at n = 6, L = 3), the kernels have finite range, so w_k(p) is analytic in p near 0; gauge invariance of log Z_k^{(1)} (manifest covariance of Δ, D_k, Q̃; checked numerically in NUMERICS §4) makes the quadratic form transverse, hence Π_k(0) = 0 and Π_k(p) = O(p); the x_1-reflection makes Π_{22}(pe_1) even, hence O(p²). So S_k(p) is analytic and even near p = 0 and S_k is finite. Confirmed. Given (3.3) and (4.1) at p ≠ 0, r_k(p) = S_k(p) − c_2 ln(1/p²) − κ_W^F + O(p² ln p) and the coefficient of ln(1/p²) in r_k(p) is −c_2. Confirmed, and trivial: r_k is defined as the difference.

What is not proved, and what the text claims. (5.2) reads S_k = c_2 ln(L^{2k}) + κ_W^F − κ_k with κ_k := lim[r_k(p) + c_2 ln(1/(p²L^{2k}))]. Two issues.
- Sign: with this definition, r_k + c_2 ln(1/p²) = [r_k + c_2 ln(1/(p²L^{2k}))] + c_2 ln L^{2k} → κ_k + c k log L, so S_k = c k log L + κ_W^F + κ_k. The numbers of §5 (κ_1(L = 2) = 1.4868 − 0.3725 = 1.114) use κ_k = κ_W^F − C_k, the opposite sign. Pick one.
- Content: replacing L^{2k} by L^{2c′k/c} for any c′ gives S_k = c′k log L + κ_W^F − κ_k′ with κ_k′ equally well defined. The identity isolates no coefficient. Remark (a), "The universal coefficient of the coarse log is forced by finiteness; it does not need a separate universality proof for the coarse theory", is true only of the ln p² coefficient at fixed k. The coefficient of k log L in S_k is sup/lim of (S_k − c k log L), i.e. the boundedness of κ_k, i.e. Lemma U2′ (open). The physical argument that the coarse pieces are "the coarse-lattice regularization at spacing L^k of the same one-loop integral" with log ln(1/(p²L^{2k})) is the standard heuristic; it is not made exact by (5.1)–(5.2).
Net: Prop. 5.1 = (finiteness of S_k at fixed k, proved) + (a definition). U1′(w) ⇔ sup|κ_k| < ∞ is a tautology. The ledger item P5 should say so.

## 3. The gap between "exact decomposition" and "the coarse terms carry −c_2 ln p²"

Answer to the two questions posed.
- Is finiteness of S_k at fixed k proved? Yes, modulo the infinite-volume rigor already flagged: positivity with a gap of the constrained gauge-fixed form at a = 0 (Balaban at U = 1; the extra D_kD_k^T only helps), finite range of all kernels, hence analyticity of w_k(p) near 0; transversality from gauge invariance, hence w_k = O(p²); hypercubic/reflection symmetry, hence evenness. This is solid at fixed k.
- Does it force the coefficient? It forces the coefficient of ln(1/p²) in the sum of the three coarse pieces at fixed k to be −c_2. That follows from (3.3) at p ≠ 0 plus the fine piece's universal log (LW (5.7), tied to b_0). It does not force the coefficient of ln(L^{2k}), because at fixed k the number ln(L^{2k}) is a constant and is indistinguishable from κ_k. The identity (5.2) therefore reduces U1′ to the boundedness of a sequence of constants that is defined by (5.2) itself. The reduction has content only through the heuristic that the coarse pieces are a lattice regularization at spacing L^k of the same loop integral, whose proof is Lemma U2′.
- A second, smaller gap: (3.3) is applied to the infinite lattice where Lemma 3.2's hypothesis (X invertible) holds only in the sense of an injective operator with 0 in the spectrum; the per-cell densities of the three coarse terms are each IR-singular at q → 0 in the symbol and finite only after the Brillouin-zone integral. No per-cell limit theorem is given.
