## 2. Derivation: walk expansion of G from G_0 [P unless tagged]
2.0 Inputs, all per WALK TERM, with the (3.108) form (whole-term bounds only, per SOURCES_A1_WALK §6.2), in rescaled units (weight m, S-rescaling of A1_KERNEL §8; derivative norms carry (L^jη)^{−1}, absorbed the same way [I, bookkeeping as in (3.131)]):
 (I1) G_0 = Σ_ω T^0_ω (Thm 3.10 [R, printed sketch]); (I2) G′ = Σ T′_ω (Thm 3.7 + Cor. 3.8 [R, proof by reference to CMP 95 Prop 1.2, CMP 96 Prop 2.2]); (I3) (Q′G′²Q′*)^{−1} = Σ R′_ω (Thm 3.9 [R, printed sketch]). Each obeys (L1)–(L2) and
 |T_ω(y,y′)| ≤ B_0 ρ^{|ω|} m(y,y′) e^{−δ d(ω,y,y′)}, ρ := c_0M^{−1}, δ := ½δ_0, B_0 = B_0(d,L),
 AND the same bound for the derivative norms ((3.108) "corresponding inequalities for norms … (3.42)–(3.47)"; Cor. 3.8 "Similar estimates hold for the other norms").
 (I4) Lemma 2.1 of CMP 96 [R, printed proof]: sup_y Σ_{y′} e^{−κ d(y,y′)} ≤ c_1(κ).
 (I5) J = D*η^{−2}Im ∂U is a function on bonds, local in U, |J(b)| ≤ O(1)Mα_0(L^jη)^{−3} by (3.36) [V p. 419, p. 422].
2.1 Elementary blocks. Call a "block" any one of: a walk term of G_0, G′, or (Q′G′²Q′*)^{−1}; a local factor Q, Q′, Q*, Q′*, D, D* (supported on one block domain, [I] Q-locality as in A1_WALK §8); the J-insertion ι_b (multiplication by J(b) with the commutator structure of (3.120), domain = the cube of 𝒟 containing b). Each block has a domain list, (L1)–(L2), and a (B1) bound.
2.2 V as a walk sum. By 1.2, V = −Δ′_π = Σ_b Σ_{k=1}^{3} L_{b,k}* ι_b M_{b,k}, with L, M ∈ {1, G′RD*, DG′RD*} restricted to b. Write R = I − P, P = G′Q′*(Q′G′²Q′*)^{−1}Q′G′. Expanding every G′ and (Q′G′²Q′*)^{−1} by (I2)–(I3) gives V = Σ_ν V_ν, each V_ν an ordered product of blocks. Number of block-type patterns per V: 3 terms × at most 2 chains × 2 (I or P) per chain ≤ 12.
2.3 Concatenation lemma [P]. Let T = Φ_1Φ_2···Φ_N be an ordered product of blocks with bounds |φ_k(z,z′)| ≤ b_k ρ^{|ω_k|} m(z,z′)e^{−δ d(ω_k,z,z′)}. Then:
 (a) T = 0 unless the last domain of Φ_k meets the first domain of Φ_{k+1} for all k (kernel supports, (L1)). So the concatenated domain list ω := ω_1 ∘ ··· ∘ ω_N is a walk (consecutive domains intersect).
 (b) T depends on U only on the union of the X̃^5 of all its domains (L2).
 (c) Fix θ ∈ (0,1). Then |T(y,y′)| ≤ (Π_k b_k) c_1(θδ)^{N−1} ρ^{Σ|ω_k|} m(y,y′) e^{−(1−θ)δ d(ω,y,y′)}.
 Proof of (c). Write the kernel as a sum over junction points z_1, …, z_{N−1} with weights μ(z_k). The weights telescope: μ(z)m(x,z)m(z,x′) = m(x,x′) (A1_WALK §5.5). The exponent is δ Σ_k d(ω_k, z_{k−1}, z_k) (z_0 = y, z_N = y′). Two lower bounds hold: (i) Σ_k d(ω_k,z_{k−1},z_k) ≥ d(ω,y,y′), because the concatenated paths pass through every domain of ω, and z_k lies in the last domain of ω_k and the first of ω_{k+1}, so it serves both; (ii) d(ω_k,z_{k−1},z_k) ≥ d(z_{k−1},z_k) (triangle inequality). Use (i) with weight (1 − θ) and (ii) with weight θ. Then sum z_1, z_2, … in order: each sum is ≤ c_1(θδ) by (I4). ∎
 This is Balaban's own split ((3.92) → (3.94): "use part of the exponentials to control the sum over y_i's").
 (d) Derivatives. V contains D, D* on the outside of its chains (1.4: one derivative falls on the neighbour). In a product, merge each D or D* into the adjacent G_0-, G′-walk block and use that block's derivative-norm bound (I1)–(I2). The left neighbour needs the derivative in the SECOND argument: use G_0 = G_0* and the adjoint walk term (reversed domain list, adjoint factors, same locality) [I-D: Balaban does this step without comment on p. 423 ("∇G_0DRG′D*…"); low risk].
2.4 The Neumann series. (3.130): G = Σ_{n≥0} G_0(Δ′_πG_0)^n = Σ_{n≥0} G_0(−VG_0)^n. Insert (I1) for every G_0 and 2.2 for every V. A term is
 T_ω = T^0_{ω_0} V_{ν_1} T^0_{ω_1} ··· V_{ν_n} T^0_{ω_n},
 an ordered product of blocks. By 2.3(a)–(b) it is a walk term with (L1)–(L2) (interior-support refinement of A1_WALK_VERIFY §9(1) inherited from the blocks).
2.5 Bound per term. Apply 2.3(c) with θ = ½. Each V_ν contains one J-factor, so one factor O(1)Mα_0 (I5), and at most 2·(3 + 2) = 10 internal junctions [I count: chain G′Q′*R′Q′G′ = 5 blocks plus D, D* merged]. Let N_V ≤ 11 be the block count per V and K_V := 12·B_0^{N_V}c_1(¼δ_0)^{N_V}. Then
 |T_ω(y,y′)| ≤ B_0 (K_V B_0 c_1(¼δ_0)² · O(1)Mα_0)^n ρ^{Σ internal steps} m(y,y′) e^{−¼δ_0 d(ω,y,y′)}.
 Write ε_V := K_V B_0 c_1(¼δ_0)²·O(1)Mα_0. Each V-insertion creates exactly 2 junction steps of ω without an M^{−1} factor (into V and out of V) plus its internal steps (each with ρ). So
 |T_ω| ≤ B_0 ρ_*^{|ω|} m e^{−¼δ_0 d(ω,y,y′)}, ρ_* := max{ρ, ε_V^{1/2}}.
2.6 Branching (B2). Given the current domain X, the next domain of ω is either the next domain inside the same block (≤ C_𝒟 choices, Balaban's count) or the first domain of a new block that meets X (≤ C_𝒟 choices of domain × ≤ 12 block patterns × finitely many factor types). So the branching is ≤ C′_𝒟 := 12·C_𝒟·(#types), a constant of (d, L).
2.7 Convergence. Σ_ω |T_ω(y,y′)| ≤ B_0 m e^{−¼δ_0d(y,y′)} Σ_{|ω|}(C′_𝒟 c_1 ρ_*)^{|ω|} ≤ 2B_0 m e^{−¼δ_0d(y,y′)} if C′_𝒟c_1ρ_* ≤ ½. This needs (i) M ≥ M_* (d,L) (ρ small) and (ii) ε_V ≤ (2C′_𝒟c_1)^{−2}, i.e. Mα_0 ≤ a_*(d,L). Condition (ii) is the Neumann condition: it is the walk-weighted form of ‖VG_0‖ < 1. Both are absolute: "the series … convergent for α_0 restricted by a small, absolute constant" (p. 423) [V], read as Mα_0 ≤ a_* (the factor M is explicit in (3.131)).
2.8 Result (Theorem WG-G) [P given (I1)–(I5) and I-D]. For M ≥ M_*, Mα_0 ≤ a_*, U with (3.35)–(3.36): G = Σ_ω T_ω, a walk expansion in the sense (L1)–(L2), (B1)–(B2) of A1_WALK §1.2, with B = B_0, step factor ρ_*, rate ¼δ_0 (any (1 − θ)½δ_0, θ > 0, with c_1(θ½δ_0)). The rate loss from ½δ_0 is the fixed factor (1 − θ). Balaban absorbs exactly such losses by redefining δ_0 as "a common, best possible, decay rate for all these operators" (p. 423 [V]).
2.9 Form caveat [P]. Literal (3.108) form needs a factor cM^{−1} per step. The two junction steps of each V carry ε_V^{1/2} = O((Mα_0)^{1/2}) instead. This is ≤ cM^{−1} iff Mα_0 ≤ c′M^{−2}, i.e. α_0 ≤ c′M^{−3}. Balaban's order (M fixed first, Thm 3.10; then "α_0 sufficiently small", Thm 3.12) allows this. The project convention Mα_0 ≤ a_0 with a_0 fixed (A1_KERNEL §2) does NOT give it. Consequence: under Mα_0 ≤ a_0 only, the step factor is ρ_* = max{c/M, O(a_0^{1/2})}; counting still converges, but the "free" M^{−½|ω|} of (3.108) is not available at V-junctions. A1-W (A1_WALK §5–§6) uses only C_𝒟c_1ρ ≤ ¼ for its G-blocks, so it is unaffected; consumers that use the free M^{−½|ω|} factor (CMP 116 p. 3 s-rule) must take α_0 ≤ c′M^{−3} or use the exponential instead [I, open point WG-1].

## 3. Correction to 2.5, and the G_1 step [P unless tagged]
3.0 Correction to 2.5 (step count). Merge every local factor (Q, Q′, Q*, Q′*, D, D*) into an adjacent walk block [I Q-locality]. Then a V_ν is a product of at most N_V ≤ 6 blocks (two chains G′·[1 or Q′*R′Q′G′]·…, plus ι_b), so it has at most N_V − 1 internal junctions and 2 outer junctions; none of these N_V + 1 steps carries ρ. The correct step factor is ρ_* := max{ρ, ε_V^{1/(N_V+1)}}, with ε_V the full V-weight (one O(1)Mα_0 times B_0^{N_V}c_1^{N_V+1}·12). 2.6–2.8 hold verbatim with this ρ_*. In 2.9 the literal-form condition becomes Mα_0 ≤ c′M^{−(N_V+1)}, i.e. α_0 ≤ c′M^{−11} [corrected per SOURCES_WG_VERIFY: N_V ≤ 9] (N_V = 6). Still admissible in Balaban's order (α_0 after M); still not implied by Mα_0 ≤ a_0. Open point WG-1 unchanged in kind.
3.1 The G_1 identity [V 1.7]. G_1^{−1} = G_0^{−1} − Δ′_π − Δ_π^{(2)} = G^{−1} − Δ_π^{(2)}. Two equivalent Neumann series:
 (3.138) G_1 = Σ_n G_0((Δ′_π + Δ_π^{(2)})G_0)^n [printed route], or G_1 = Σ_n G(Δ_π^{(2)}G)^n [brief's route].
 Use the second: it reuses Theorem WG-G (2.8) as the input block for G. Both give the same sum (absolute convergence, rearrangement).
3.2 Structure of Δ_π^{(2)} [V (3.134)–(3.136); p. 427 text]. Δ_π^{(2)} = (I − DRG′D*)Δ^{(2)}(I − DG′RD*) by (3.135) (expand the product: four terms, matches (3.135) exactly). Δ^{(2)}A(·) = Σ_j Σ_{b∈Λ_j}(L^jη)^{d+1} tr[(δ/δA)C_j^{(2)}(A,b)]·w(b), w := H*J. Here:
 (i) (δ/δA)C_j^{(2)}(A,b) is linear in A and local: C^{(2)}_j is the second-order term of the block average Q_j at block bond b [I: support = the corridor of b, inside a few j-blocks; matches (3.137) "sup over several j-blocks"]. Call this local factor κ_b (domain = the 𝒟-cube of b).
 (ii) w(b) = Σ_{b′} H*(b,b′)J(b′), H* = (QGQ*)^{−1}QG (H = GQ*(QGQ*)^{−1}, (3.126); QGQ* symmetric).
 So each term of Δ^{(2)} is: [left chain] κ_b [right chain], times the scalar w(b), which is a THIRD chain from b to the J-bond b′. Balaban, p. 427 [V]: "three chains of operators … meet in a localization domain determined by C^{(2)}(A) … ω having a tree-like structure … main line and at some vertices additional lines spring off … d(ω,y,y′) is now a length of a shortest tree intersecting all domains".
3.3 Walk input for the branch. w(b) needs a walk expansion of (QGQ*)^{−1}. Balaban does NOT print one (p. 422 "will not repeat"; A1_WALK_VERIFY §5). Our source for it is Theorem A1-W (SOURCES_A1_WALK §7.1) [P given (W_G) for G, (C), Lemma 2.1, O2/Q-locality/κ_1], rate δ_W = ½ε, step factor K_W/M_1. Its (W_G) input for G is Theorem WG-G (2.8). Order: G_0 → G (2.8) → (QGQ*)^{−1} (A1-W) → w = H*J → Δ_π^{(2)} → G_1. No loop: A1-W for (QGQ*)^{−1} uses G, not G_1.
3.4 Branch bound [P]. A branch term β (walk of G, then Q, then walk of (QGQ*)^{−1}, ending at J(b′)) has, by 2.3(c), |β(b,b′)| ≤ B_β ρ_β^{|β|} m e^{−(1−θ)δ_β d(β,b,b′)}, δ_β := min{¼δ_0, ½ε} = ½ε (ε ≤ ½δ_G < ¼δ_0 [I: δ_G < ½δ_0 by its definition in A1_WALK §4.0]). Sum over b′ with |J(b′)| ≤ O(1)Mα_0(rescaled): Σ_{b′}e^{−θδ_β d(b,b′)}|J(b′)| ≤ c_1(θδ_β)O(1)Mα_0. So each branch carries one factor Mα_0 and the decay of its own path. Per term of (3.136), |(Δ^{(2)} part)| reproduces (3.137) O(1)Mα_0(L^jη)^{−2} after summing the branch [consistency check against [V] (3.137)].
3.5 Tree terms and bound [P]. Insert into G_1 = Σ_n G(Δ_π^{(2)}G)^n: G-walks (2.8), the chains DRG′D*, DG′RD* (2.2-type blocks), κ_b, and the branch at each κ_b. A term is a tree τ: main line = ordered product of blocks from y to y′; at each κ_b vertex one branch line to some b′. Concatenation 2.3 extends to trees: the junction sums on the main line are as before; the branch sum is 3.4; the exponent bound (i) becomes "sum of path lengths ≥ length of a shortest tree meeting all domains" (the main path ∪ branch paths is a connected set meeting every domain); (ii) is unchanged. Result:
 |T_τ(y,y′)| ≤ B_0 ρ_{**}^{|τ|} m(y,y′) e^{−(1−θ)δ_β d(τ,y,y′)}, |τ| = number of domains − 1,
 with ρ_{**} = max{ρ_*, K_W/M_1, ε_2^{1/(N_2+1)}}, ε_2 = O(1)Mα_0·(constants of d, L, a, a_0), N_2 = block count per Δ_π^{(2)} insertion excluding branch-internal steps (which carry ρ_β).
3.6 Branching for trees [P]. At each domain the next domain is chosen among ≤ C″_𝒟 options (as 2.6), and at a κ_b vertex there is one extra choice: start a branch (≤ C_𝒟 first domains). A tree with |τ| + 1 domains and one branch per κ-vertex is counted by its depth-first sequence of domains: ≤ (2C″_𝒟)^{|τ|} trees. So (B2) holds with C″′_𝒟 = 2C″_𝒟, and Σ_τ converges when 2C″_𝒟c_1ρ_{**} ≤ ½. This needs M ≥ M_*, M_1 ≥ M_W (A1-W), and Mα_0 ≤ a_{**}(d,L,a,a_0). This is the Neumann condition for Δ_π^{(2)}G: (3.137) gives the O(Mα_0) size.
3.7 Result (Theorem WG-G1) [P given WG-G, A1-W and the [I] items]. G_1 = Σ_τ T_τ, a tree expansion with (L1)–(L2) on every domain, (B1) with tree distance, (B2), rate (1 − θ)·½ε. This is the p. 427 statement with the rate made explicit. The rate of G_1 is set by A1-W (½ε), NOT by δ_0: the branch carries the (QGQ*)^{−1} decay. Balaban's (3.132) δ_1 has the same role [V p. 422: (3.133) for H has e^{−½δ_1d}].
