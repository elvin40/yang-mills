## 7. Does GGAIN prove the first-derivative part of the p. 264 assumption?
- What p. 264 assumes (THEOREM2 §3a, verbatim): E^{(j)} is C^∞ (or analytic) in g_{j−1} ∈ [0, γ], with only the sup bound (1.18); β_{j+1} is smooth on [0, γ], "uniformly bounded … together with all derivatives". The uniform derivative bound is assumed for β only.
- Vacuum subtraction: CMP 109 p. 258 says the vacuum counterterm is in the normalization factors, "E^{(j)}(1) = 0". ∂_g[E(U) − E(1)] is bounded by 2S_0 by the same argument. Covered.
- Coupling subtraction: CMP 109 (4.45) p. 291: β_j(g_{j−1}) is defined as the coefficient of Π's (4.41)-type expansion; the β-counterterms cancel (4.42), (4.44) in the action, E^{(j)} is not modified. β_{k+1} = Π(E^{(k+1)}) linear, g-free, infinite-volume limit of a uniform bound. Covered.
- Not covered: (i) the closed interval: GGAIN proves the bound on (0, γ]; a uniform Lipschitz bound extends β continuously to 0, but C¹ at g = 0 needs ∂_gβ to have a limit (likely true, since the χ-terms are flat e^{−c/g²}; not shown). (ii) large-field contributions (CMP 119/122): GGAIN lists them as open; if the final β_j is taken from the large-field-completed E^{(j)}, they are needed. (iii) the inputs E^{(j)}, j ≤ k, are taken at Balaban's inductive bounds; that is consistent (own-slot derivative only). (iv) G3 and (M_r).
- Verdict: the first-derivative part of the β-assumption is proved for the small-field step map on (0, γ], given G3 (reduced) and (M_r). "Now proved" overstates it by items (i), (ii), and G3.
