## 3. Two general lemmas on joint holomorphy [P]

Setting. Ω_1 ⊂ C^a and Ω_2 ⊂ C^b are open. The lattice is finite, so every term of S1(c) is a function of finitely many complex variables: the complexified link variables and J on X (an open set U_T(X) ⊂ C^a, by the strict inequalities of V1), and the plaquette values of the source on X (an open set P_ρ(X) ⊂ C^b).

### 3.1 Lemma J1 (separate holomorphy and a bound give joint holomorphy) [P]
Let F: Ω_1 × Ω_2 → C. Assume (i) z_1 ↦ F(z_1, z_2) is holomorphic on Ω_1 for every z_2 ∈ Ω_2; (ii) z_2 ↦ F(z_1, z_2) is holomorphic on Ω_2 for every z_1 ∈ Ω_1; (iii) F is locally bounded. Then F is holomorphic on Ω_1 × Ω_2.
Proof. Fix (p_1, p_2) and closed polydiscs Δ_i = Δ(p_i, 2s) ⊂ Ω_i with |F| ≤ M on Δ_1 × Δ_2. Step 1 (continuity). For z_1, z_1′ ∈ Δ(p_1, s) and any z_2 ∈ Δ_2, the Cauchy estimate for the holomorphic function F(·, z_2) on Δ(p_1, 2s) gives |F(z_1, z_2) − F(z_1′, z_2)| ≤ (aM/s)|z_1 − z_1′|_∞. The same holds in z_2. So F is Lipschitz, hence continuous, on Δ(p_1, s) × Δ(p_2, s). Step 2 (Cauchy formula). For fixed z_2, apply the Cauchy formula on the distinguished boundary ∂_0Δ(p_1, s) to F(·, z_2). Then apply it on ∂_0Δ(p_2, s) to each F(w_1, ·). The integrand F(w_1, w_2)/Π(w_1 − z_1)(w_2 − z_2) is continuous on the compact torus ∂_0Δ(p_1, s) × ∂_0Δ(p_2, s), so the iterated integral is one integral (Fubini). The kernel is holomorphic in (z_1, z_2) on the open polydisc and the integrand is bounded, so differentiation under the integral sign shows that F is holomorphic in all a + b variables there. ∎
Remark. This is the bounded case of Hartogs' theorem; (iii) makes the proof elementary. In S1(c) the bound b_T gives (iii).

### 3.2 Lemma J2 (six operations keep joint holomorphy) [P]
Let Ω = Ω_1 × Ω_2 as above (field variables × source variables). Call h "admissible" if h is jointly holomorphic on Ω and locally bounded. Then:
 (O1) Pull-back. If W: Ω_1 × A_0 → C^{a′} is holomorphic in z_1 for each real auxiliary point A ∈ A_0, does not depend on z_2, and maps Ω_1 × {A} into the domain Ω_1′ of an admissible h′ on Ω_1′ × Ω_2, then (z_1, z_2) ↦ h′(W(z_1, A), z_2) is admissible for each A. [chain rule]
 (O2) Algebra. Sums, products, exp(h), and p(h_1, …, h_m) for a polynomial p of admissible functions are admissible. A function of z_2 alone that is holomorphic on Ω_2 (a weight, a vertex coefficient) is admissible. [standard]
 (O3) Parameter integrals. Let μ be a positive measure on a set of real auxiliary variables A, independent of (z_1, z_2). Let h(z; A) be admissible for each A, measurable in A, and |h(z; A)| ≤ H(A) on compact subsets of Ω, with ∫H dμ < ∞. Then ∫h(z; A)dμ(A) is admissible. Proof: continuity by dominated convergence; for each fixed z_2 (resp. z_1) Morera's theorem in each coordinate with Fubini gives separate holomorphy; local boundedness by ∫H dμ; then Lemma J1. ∎
 (O4) Limits. If h_n are admissible and Σ_n sup_K|h_n| < ∞ for each compact K ⊂ Ω, then Σ_n h_n is admissible. [Weierstrass]
 (O5) Quotients and logarithms. If g is admissible and g ≠ 0 on Ω, then 1/g is admissible. If g_0 is admissible with an admissible logarithm (for example g_0 > 0 constant) and |g/g_0 − 1| ≤ q < 1 on Ω, then log g := log g_0 + Σ_{n≥1} (−1)^{n+1}(g/g_0 − 1)^n/n is admissible. [O4]
 (O6) Cauchy integrals in auxiliary complex parameters. If h(z; t) is admissible on Ω × {|t| < R′} and R < R′, then (2πi)^{−1}∮_{|t|=R} h(z; t)t^{−n−1}dt is admissible. [O3 with the contour as the measure space]
Consequence. A function built from admissible inputs by finitely many applications of (O1)–(O6), with absolutely and locally uniformly convergent sums and integrals, is jointly holomorphic. The only facts that must be checked are: (1) no ingredient depends on the source in a non-holomorphic way; (2) no divisor vanishes and no logarithm leaves its branch; (3) the sums and integrals converge locally uniformly on the product domain. Item (3) is a bound, not a holomorphy question. [P]

### 6.3 Lemma J3 (a convergent polymer expansion of a logarithm removes the divisor question) [P], and the two open entries
Lemma J3. Let P ⊂ C^b be open and connected, with P ∩ R^b ≠ ∅ open in R^b. Let G be holomorphic on P, and let S = Σ_Y a_Y be a series of holomorphic functions on P that converges locally uniformly. At a point x of that set all real partial derivatives of H vanish, and for a holomorphic function the complex partial derivatives equal the real ones (∂/∂z_i = ∂/∂x_i on holomorphic functions). So the Taylor series of H at x is zero, H = 0 near x, and H = 0 on P by connectedness. ∎
