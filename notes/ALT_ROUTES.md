## 1. Part A(1) — Chatterjee's theorem, sourced [V: arXiv 2006.16229v5 text extracted this session (pdftotext), §2 and §3, §12]

Bibliographic: S. Chatterjee, "A probabilistic mechanism for quark confinement", arXiv 2006.16229 (v5, 26 Mar 2021, final), Comm. Math. Phys. (2021), DOI 10.1007/s00220-021-04086-y, 35 pp. [V, abstract page].

### 1.1 Setup (§2.1–2.2) [V]
- G a closed CONNECTED subgroup of U(n); d ≥ 2; Ω = G^E on Z^d with product Haar λ; formal Hamiltonian H(ω) = −Σ_p Re Tr(ω_p); measure "dμ = Z^{−1} e^{−βH} dλ" read as a specification; β = "the inverse of the squared coupling strength" (so for SU(2) with Wilson normalization (2N/g²)(1 − N^{−1}Re Tr), β = 2/g² up to the constant; this convention is [I], the project's g is the unit-scale coupling). Gibbs measures on Z^d exist by compactness (Georgii Thm 4.22). G, β, d fixed throughout: the theorem is at ONE fixed β; there is no coupling range and no β → ∞ statement in the paper.
- Wilson loop (2.2): W_ℓ = χ_π(ω_{e_1}⋯ω_{e_k}), π a fixed finite-dimensional irreducible unitary representation. Area law (2.3): |⟨W_ℓ⟩| ≤ C_1 e^{−C_2 area(ℓ)} for rectangular ℓ. Weaker "confinement" (his §2.2): limsup_{T→∞} T^{−1} log|⟨W_{ℓ_{R,T}}⟩| ≤ −V(R) with V(R) → ∞.

### 1.2 Center symmetry (§2.3) [V]
- Slab S = {0,…,N} × Z^{d−1}, boundary = top and bottom layers; boundary condition δ ∈ G^{∂E(S)}; specification α on Ω(S, δ) with Hamiltonian −Σ_{p∈P(S)} Re Tr ω_p.
- Center transform τ: for g_0 ∈ Z(G), multiply every vertical edge from layer 0 to layer 1 by g_0. It leaves every ω_p invariant, so it is a symmetry of α (Georgii Ch. 5).
- "α has unbroken center symmetry if every Gibbs measure of α is invariant under each center transform."
- Definition 2.1: the theory on Z^d has unbroken center symmetry if for SOME N ≥ 1, the theory on the slab of thickness N, under ANY boundary condition δ, has unbroken center symmetry in the above sense.

### 1.3 The two main theorems [V]
- Theorem 2.2 (one direction only). Unbroken center symmetry (Def. 2.1) + π acts nontrivially on Z(G) ⇒ there is V: Z_+ → R with V(R) → ∞ such that for every rectangular loop with sides R ≤ T and every Gibbs measure on Z^d, |⟨W_ℓ⟩| ≤ e^{−V(R)T}.
- Theorem 2.4. "Exponential decay of correlations under arbitrary boundary conditions" (Def. 2.3: constants K_1, K_2 depending on G, β, d only, such that for every cube B, every boundary condition δ on B, and every pair of local functions f, g supported on edges of B with values in [−1, 1], |⟨fg⟩_{B,δ} − ⟨f⟩_{B,δ}⟨g⟩_{B,δ}| ≤ K_1 e^{−K_2 dist(f,g)}) ⇒ unbroken center symmetry; and, for π nontrivial on Z(G), every Gibbs measure satisfies the area law (2.3).
- There is NO converse in the paper (no "area law ⇒ unbroken center symmetry"). The word "equivalence" in the task brief does not match the printed theorems. What is printed is the chain: strong exponential decay (Def. 2.3) ⇒ unbroken center symmetry ⇒ V(R) → ∞ (and, with Def. 2.3, the area law).
- Finite vs infinite volume: Def. 2.1 and Thm 2.2 are infinite-volume (Gibbs measures on Z^d and on infinite slabs); the proof (Lemma 3.1) uses finite boxes S_R = {0,…,N} × {−R,…,R}^{d−1} with arbitrary boundary conditions and a compactness argument. The output V(R) is NON-QUANTITATIVE: it comes from "limsup_{R→∞} sup_{f,δ}|⟨f⟩_{R,δ}| = 0", proved by contradiction with Prokhorov (§3, Lemma 3.1).
- Where the area law's constants come from (§12, (12.1)) [V]: Def. 2.3 gives V(R) ≥ C_2 R − C_1 ("at least linear growth", via uniqueness of the slab Gibbs measure and exponential convergence of ⟨f⟩_{S_{M,N}} to 0); the proof then removes the C_1T term with Lemma 12.1 (a density-ratio argument on one group factor). So the area-law string tension of Thm 2.4 is of the order of K_2 (the correlation-decay rate), not of a separately identified quantity.
- Chatterjee's own remark (§2.4 end) [V]: theories believed gapped and center-symmetric but not confining at large β (finite gauge groups; adjoint Higgs). By Thm 2.2 their center symmetry must break; Def. 2.3 fails for them because they have multiple Gibbs measures (long-range order) at weak coupling. He also writes that Def. 2.3 "is quite strong" and that it is "not clear whether" the Yang–Mills mass gap conjecture "includes the strong version stated above."

## 2. Part A(2) — the quantitative inequality that Chatterjee's route relocates the problem to

### 2.1 What his proof actually consumes [P, by inspection of §3 [V]]
Lemma 3.1 gives sup_{f,δ} |⟨f⟩_{R,δ}| ≤ e^{−V(R)} for the vertical chain variable f on the central chain of S_R = {0,…,N} × {−R,…,R}^{d−1}, under every boundary condition δ. Lemma 3.2 iterates it over [T/N] independent slabs (conditioning on the layer variables). The Wilson loop bound is then (printed) |⟨W_ℓ⟩| ≤ m^{2(R+T)} e^{−V(R)[T/N]}. So the ONLY input is the function V, and the area law with string tension σ follows if and only if V is at least linear:

(CS_σ)  there are N ≥ 1 and σ > 0 such that for all R ≥ 1, all boundary conditions δ on S_R, and all vertical chain variables f on the central chain,  |⟨f⟩_{R,δ}| ≤ e^{−σ N R + c}.

Given (CS_σ): |⟨W_{R×T}⟩| ≤ exp(−σ RT + σ N R + 2(R+T) log m + c T/N), i.e. the area law with string tension σ up to perimeter-order corrections (Chatterjee's Lemma 12.1 removes the C_1 T term when a two-sided density bound holds on one factor) [P]. Conversely, if V(R) = o(R) then the bound of Thm 2.2 is NOT an area law: Thm 2.2 by itself gives only V(R) → ∞ at an unspecified rate. The quantitative content of the route is therefore entirely (CS_σ).

Interpretation [P]: −log sup_δ |⟨f⟩_{R,δ}| is the free energy of a static charge in representation π crossing a slab of thickness N inside a box of radius R with the worst boundary condition; (CS_σ) says it grows like σ × (minimal area N R spanned between the charge line and the box boundary). That is the linear static potential itself, written for one line observable in a finite box under arbitrary boundary conditions. It is not a weaker statement than confinement; it is confinement with a uniformity in boundary conditions added.

### 2.2 The torus / 't Hooft form [I]
On a periodic torus the center transform on one full layer leaves every plaquette invariant and is itself a symmetry of the measure, so ⟨P⟩ = 0 for the vertical Polyakov line at every β: Def. 2.1 needs non-periodic boundary conditions to be a nontrivial condition. The periodic-torus analogue is the twist (vortex) free energy: Z_tw(ℓ) with 't Hooft twisted boundary conditions z_{μν} ∈ Z(G) on a coclosed set of plaquettes dual to a 2-plane, e^{−F_v(ℓ)} := Z_tw/Z; by 't Hooft's Z(G) Fourier duality the electric-flux free energy e^{−F_e} = Σ_z χ(z) e^{−F_v(z)}/Σ_z e^{−F_v(z)}; confinement in d = 4 is F_e(ℓ) ≈ σ ℓ² and F_v(ℓ) ≈ C e^{−ρ ℓ²} ("vortex condensation"). Tomboulis–Yaffe (CMP 100, 1985) prove rigorously that an exponential bound on the vortex free energy implies the Wilson-loop area law [I: statement recalled, constants not re-read]. In either formulation the hypothesis is one exponentially small number per torus size, and nobody has a bound on it at weak coupling.

### 2.3 The required scaling, written out [P arithmetic on the standard two-loop formula; the formula itself is R]
Continuum area law needs σ_phys = σ(β)/ε² fixed as ε → 0, with ε tied to g by asymptotic freedom (lattice Λ-parameter, two loops):
  ε Λ_L = (b_0 g²)^{−b_1/(2b_0²)} exp(−1/(2 b_0 g²)) (1 + O(g²)),  b_0 = 11N/(48π²),  b_1 = 34N²/(3(16π²)²).
Hence
  σ(g) = (σ_phys/Λ_L²) · (b_0 g²)^{−b_1/b_0²} · exp(−1/(b_0 g²)),   b_1/b_0² = 102/121 ≈ 0.843 (N-independent).
For SU(2): 1/b_0 = 24π²/11 ≈ 21.5, so σ(g) ≍ exp(−21.5/g²)(g²)^{−0.84}. In Chatterjee's β (coefficient of Re Tr ω_p; for SU(2) Wilson action β = 2/g² [I convention]): σ(β) ≍ exp(−(12π²/11) β) β^{0.84} ≈ e^{−10.8 β} β^{0.84}. So (CS_σ) must be proved with σ = σ(β) of exactly this exponentially small size, for all β ≥ β_0, with N = N(β) allowed to grow (N ~ 1/σ^{1/2} is the natural slab thickness, one correlation length).

### 2.4 Why this is the confinement problem, not a reduction of it [P]
(a) At fixed β the lattice statement (CS_σ) with any σ > 0 is open for every β outside the Osterwalder–Seiler strong-coupling region; Chatterjee adds no mechanism to prove it, his Thm 2.4 derives it from a stronger hypothesis (Def. 2.3, see §3 and §8).
(b) The exponent is exponentially small in β. Every tool available at weak coupling (Balaban's small-field expansions, the project's walk and polymer bounds) is a perturbation around V_p ≈ 1 with constants polynomial in 1/β per unit cell; such a tool can produce an exponent exp(−c/g²) only by iterating the RG for ≈ 1/(2 b_0 g² log L) further steps until the running coupling leaves ]0, γ]. That is the project's L15/L17 wall (FABLE §7.3 item 2): there is no small parameter at the scale ξ_g where σ becomes visible.
(c) Uniformity in boundary conditions is an EXTRA requirement relative to the project: Balaban's construction (TRUST_LEDGER §0) is on the periodic torus only; no version with fixed boundary conditions on a slab exists in the project or, to my knowledge [I], in Balaban's papers. Theorem IV's unit-scale measure ν_{K,ℓ} is a periodic-torus object. So (CS_σ) asks for strictly more than the project's wall: the wall plus boundary conditions.
Conclusion: Route A relocates "area law" to (CS_σ) with σ(β) ≍ e^{−β/b_0}; the relocation is exact (no loss, no gain) and the new inequality is the static potential under arbitrary boundary conditions, which is the confinement problem.

## 3. Part A(3) — area law versus mass gap: what the gap needs beyond the area law

Definitions used here [P/standard].
- Area law (string tension): σ := −lim_{R,T→∞} (RT)^{−1} log|⟨W_{R×T}⟩| > 0 for a representation π nontrivial on Z(G). It is a statement about the sector with two external static charges; by the transfer-matrix reading, ⟨W_{R×T}⟩ ≈ e^{−V(R)T} with V the static potential, and σ > 0 ⇔ V(R) ~ σR.
- Mass gap (Jaffe–Witten/OS): the infinite-volume vacuum state is unique and the Hamiltonian has m := inf (spec H ∖ {0}) > 0 in the chargeless sector; equivalently [R, Osterwalder–Schrader + Fröhlich], exponential clustering of the truncated correlations of local gauge-invariant operators with rate m, uniformly in volume. In the project this is L17: decay of ν_∞-correlations of the plaquette density (FABLE §7.2).

What is proved between them [V, Chatterjee Thm 2.4; P for the rest].
(i) Def. 2.3 (exponential decay under ARBITRARY boundary conditions on cubes) ⇒ unbroken center symmetry ⇒ area law. Def. 2.3 is a strictly stronger hypothesis than the mass gap: it is a Dobrushin–Shlosman-type complete-analyticity condition (uniform in boundary conditions), and Chatterjee himself notes it is "quite strong" and that it is unclear whether the YM gap conjecture includes it [V]. Its failure modes are long-range order and boundary-condition dependence, not only a vanishing gap.
(ii) Area law ⇒ gap: no theorem. The area law constrains only V(R); it says nothing about the chargeless spectrum. 
(iii) Gap ⇒ area law: no theorem in the two-point/one-boundary-condition sense; Chatterjee's §2.4 remark [V] lists gapped center-symmetric theories believed deconfined at large β (finite groups, adjoint Higgs).
(iv) Both are infrared statements at the same scale. With ε tied to g by asymptotic freedom (§2.3), the lattice-unit gap is m(g) = m_phys ε ≍ (b_0g²)^{−b_1/(2b_0²)} exp(−1/(2b_0g²)), and √σ(g) has the same exponential form; m_phys/√σ_phys is an O(1) number (≈ 3.5 for the lightest SU(2)/SU(3) glueball in lattice data [I]). So neither inequality is easier in the exponent: both must produce exp(−c/g²) from a weak-coupling expansion.

What the gap needs beyond the area law, itemized [P]:
(G1) a two-point statement: |⟨φ(x)φ(0)⟩ − ⟨φ⟩²| ≤ C e^{−m|x|} for the plaquette density (and every local gauge-invariant composite), for |x| up to and beyond ξ_g = 1/m; the area law gives no bound on any truncated correlation.
(G2) volume uniformity of (G1) (Jaffe–Witten's "mass gap uniform in the volume"); ASSEMBLY §4 [V]: a gap of the finite-torus transfer matrix is not the Clay gap.
(G3) uniqueness of the vacuum / OS4 clustering, which the project lacks (FABLE L15, L17) and the area law does not supply.
(G4) the continuum form: m(g)/ε → m_phys ∈ ]0, ∞[ along the asymptotic-freedom trajectory g_0(K, g); the project's Theorem IV fixes g at the unit scale and never renormalizes scales above 1, so m(g) in Theorem IV's units would have to be exhibited directly as the decay rate of ν_∞ — an infrared computation at scale ξ_g.
Conversely, what the area law needs that the gap does not: an observable outside the project's class (Wilson lines; the center transform acts TRIVIALLY on every plaquette observable, so no bound on plaquette densities, however sharp, can detect broken or unbroken center symmetry [P, immediate from the definition of τ]).

### 4.2 Loops below unit scale (ε ≪ r ≤ 1): the strongest statement that would follow, and what blocks it
Target statement (what perturbation theory predicts and what one would like to prove): for a rectangular loop R × T, ε ≪ R ≤ T ≤ 1, in the fundamental representation of SU(2) (C_F = 3/4),
  log⟨W_{R×T}⟩ = −Σ_{k≤K−log_L R} c_k g_k² P_k  [perimeter self-energy; P_k = perimeter in scale-k units; divergent as ε → 0]  + (corner terms) + (C_F g(R)²/(4π)) T/R (1 + O(g(R)²)) + O(1),
and, free of renormalization, the Creutz-ratio form  χ(R, T) := −log [W(R,T)W(R−ε,T−ε)/(W(R,T−ε)W(R−ε,T))] = −∂_R∂_T[(C_F g(R)²/4π) T/R]·ε² (1 + O(g(R)²)), in which perimeter and corner terms cancel exactly. "Perimeter law + Coulomb correction with the running coupling" is the second line.
What it needs, step by step [P as a list; each item is checked against the project's inventory]:

Balaban prints no such expansion; the project has none. (W2) Gauge fixing along the loop. ### 4.3 The one reachable partial statement: block-averaged Wilson loops AT unit scale carry no area law below perimeter e^{p_0(g)} [P-sketch; inputs listed]
Let γ be a loop in the unit lattice of T⁴_ℓ, |γ| its length, a(γ) its minimal area (unit plaquettes), and W_γ(V) = Tr Π_{b∈γ} V_b the holonomy of the unit-scale field. Then
  E_{ν_{K,ℓ}}[W_γ] ≥ 2 − C_1 g p(g) a(γ)       (4.1)
and, for the lattice measure μ_{K,ℓ} and 𝒜_K the block-averaging map,
  ⟨W_γ(𝒜_K U)⟩_{μ_{K,ℓ}} ≥ 2 − C_1 g p(g)(a(γ) + |γ|) − C_2 |γ| e^{−p_0(g)},   uniformly in K ≥ K_0 and even ℓ > 22M.   (4.2)
Proof sketch. (4.1): on supp ν every unit plaquette satisfies the small-field bound |1 − ½Tr V_p| ≤ C(g p(g))² and |V_p − 1| ≤ C g p(g) [I: the form of Balaban's unit-scale small-field condition; the project uses it as "0 ≤ 1 − ½Re tr V_p ≤ C p(g)² g²", ATTEMPT §2.5 item 1]; the holonomy around γ differs from 1 by at most Σ over the plaquettes of a minimal spanning surface, |W_γ − 2| ≤ 2 Σ_{p ⊂ surface}|V_p − 1| ≤ C a(γ) g p(g) (non-abelian Stokes, telescoping along a surface tiling [P]). (4.2): write Z = 𝒩 ∫dV Σ_Z ∫ρ(Z, V) (Step A input [V, IV12 (L2)]). Terms with Z ∩ γ = ∅ evaluate W_γ at a small-field V on γ and are bounded below by (4.1) times their weight; terms with Z ∩ γ ≠ ∅ have total weight ≤ C_2|γ| e^{−p_0(g)} by the per-component bound e^{−p_0(γ_Bal)} of FX Step A / (1.79)–(1.80) and the entropy count Step D [R]. The difference between W_γ(V) and W_γ(𝒜_K U) is the block-constraint smearing, O(g p(g)) per link on the small-field support [I: Balaban's averaging constraint is a Gaussian of width O(g_k) around the averaged field; if it is an exact δ this term is 0]. Gauge invariance of W_γ(𝒜_K U) under fine gauge transformations follows from the covariance 𝒜_K(U^h) = (𝒜_K U)^{h|blocks} of Balaban's averaging [I: CMP 109 §1 property, not re-read]. ∎(sketch)
Consequence [P from (4.2)]: for a(γ) ≤ c/(g p(g)) and |γ| ≤ c e^{p_0(g)} = c exp(A_0 (log g^{−2})^{p_0}), ⟨W_γ(𝒜_K U)⟩ ≥ 1. So the block-averaged Wilson loop has no area law and no perimeter law with O(1) rate at these sizes, uniformly in ε and ℓ. This is weak-coupling deconfinement at distances between 1 and e^{p_0(g)} unit cells, far below ξ_g = e^{c/g²}; it is expected and it points AWAY from confinement (lower bound). ## 6. Part B(2) — why d = 4 is blocked for regularity structures: the scaling computation [P; the counting rule is Hairer's subcriticality criterion, R]

### 6.1 The equation
Stochastic YM with DeTurck term (survey (1.3)–(1.4) [V]): ∂_t A = −d_A^* F_A − d_A d_A^*(A − Z) + ξ, schematically ∂_t A = ΔA + A∂A + A³ + ξ, ξ = g-valued space-time white noise on R × T^d.

### 6.2 Regularities (parabolic scaling, |(t,x)| = |t|^{1/2} + |x|; dimension of R × T^d is d + 2)
- White noise: ξ ∈ C^{−(d+2)/2 − κ} = C^{−1 − d/2 − κ} for every κ > 0, and no better (survey [V]).
- Linear solution Ψ = (∂_t − Δ)^{−1}ξ: Schauder gains 2, so Ψ ∈ C^{α}, α := 1 − d/2 − κ. (d = 2: α = 0−; d = 3: α = −1/2−; d = 4: α = −1−.)
- Products f g for f ∈ C^{a}, g ∈ C^{b} are defined iff a + b > 0 (survey [V]). So A∂A and A³ are ill-defined for every d ≥ 2.

### 6.3 Subcriticality
Hairer's criterion: an equation is (locally) subcritical if, when A is replaced by Ψ in each nonlinear term, the term is more regular than the noise, i.e. the nonlinearity is a perturbation of the stochastic heat equation at small scales. Equivalently: under the scaling (t, x) → (λ²t, λx), A → λ^{−α}A that fixes the linear equation and the noise, the coefficients of the nonlinear terms go to zero as λ → 0.
- Term A∂A: regularity 2α − 1 = 1 − d − 2κ. Condition 1 − d > −1 − d/2  ⇔  d < 4.
- Term A³: regularity 3α = 3 − 3d/2 − 3κ. Condition 3 − 3d/2 > −1 − d/2  ⇔  d < 4.
- Scaling version: with A_λ(t, x) = λ^{−α}A(λ²t, λx), the equation becomes ∂_t A_λ = ΔA_λ + λ^{2−d/2}... explicitly, both A∂A and A³ pick up the factor λ^{(4−d)/2} = λ^{−α−1}·λ² ... [P: A∂A scales as λ^{−2α−1+2} = λ^{−2α+1} relative to λ^{−α+2}·λ^{−2} = λ^{−α} for the linear part; the ratio is λ^{−α+1} = λ^{d/2}... one checks both terms acquire exactly g_eff(λ) = g λ^{(4−d)/2}]. The clean statement is dimensional: the YM coupling g has mass dimension (4 − d)/2, so g_eff(λ) = g λ^{(4−d)/2} → 0 as λ → 0 iff d < 4.
- Conclusion [P]: d = 2, 3 subcritical (super-renormalisable; finitely many divergent trees, finitely many counterterms: the mass counterterm C A of CCHS, with its uniqueness proved by Chevyrev–Shen); d = 4 CRITICAL (both nonlinearities exactly marginal: g dimensionless); d ≥ 5 supercritical. The survey states exactly this: subcriticality "happens if and only if d < 4 and ... parallels the notion of super-renormalisability" [V].

### 6.4 What asymptotic freedom would have to supply, and whether anyone has tried [P for the structure; I for the literature judgement]
In regularity structures the model is built from finitely many trees τ with homogeneity |τ| > −(d+2)/2 ... more precisely from the trees of negative homogeneity, whose number is finite iff the equation is subcritical. At criticality (d = 4) the homogeneity of the n-th order trees does not increase with n: each additional vertex of A∂A or A³ adds regularity (2α − 1) − α + 2 = α + 1 = 1 − d/2 + 2 − ... = 0 at d = 4 (with κ = 0). So infinitely many trees have homogeneity ≤ 0, and every one of them carries a logarithmically divergent renormalisation constant. The BPHZ renormalisation group is then infinite-dimensional and, worse, the counterterms do not stay of the form "constant × A": the marginal couplings themselves run. What is needed is a renormalisation-group flow for the SPDE: an effective equation ∂_t A = ΔA + g(λ)A∂A + g(λ)²A³ + (counterterms up to scale λ) + ξ_λ with g(λ)² = g²/(1 + 2b_0 g² log(1/λ)) → 0 as λ → 0 (asymptotic freedom), together with a proof that the remainders are summable in λ. That is: Balaban's RG, in the time-dependent SPDE setting, with the flow of g carried through the model. Asymptotic freedom is what would make the scale-by-scale constants decrease; it does not remove the infinite number of them. In the project's terms, this is exactly the UV content of Balaban's Theorem 1 (the K-step flow g_k with β_k ≈ b_0 log L), redone for the parabolic equation.
Literature: no paper attempts the 4D stochastic YM equation (three searches this session; the Cao–Sheffield 2024 overview calls local existence in 4D "a major open problem"). The closest work on CRITICAL singular SPDEs is in weak-coupling or anisotropic KPZ / Φ⁴₄-type models [I: Cannizzaro–Erhard–Toninelli and successors; Φ⁴₄ is marginally IRRELEVANT in the wrong direction (triviality, Aizenman–Duminil-Copin 2021), YM is asymptotically free, so no existing critical-SPDE method transfers]. Duch's flow-equation approach and Kupiainen's RG for SPDEs are subcritical-only as published [I].
So in d = 4 the SPDE route must first REPRODUCE Balaban's UV theorem in parabolic form before it can start on the infrared; it does not bypass it.
