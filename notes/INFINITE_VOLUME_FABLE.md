### 2.3 Lemma ν-cum (cumulants through a probability measure) [P]
Let ν be a probability measure, w(t, V) holomorphic in t ∈ C^n near 0 with w(0, V) = 0 and all t-derivatives at 0 bounded uniformly in V. Put G(t) := log E_ν[e^{w(t, ·)}]. Then for B ⊂ [n]:
  ∂^B G(0) = Σ_{π ⊢ B} κ^ν( w_{B_1}, …, w_{B_k} ),   π = {B_1, …, B_k},  w_S(V) := ∂^S w(0, V),      (2.2)
where κ^ν is the joint cumulant under ν. Proof. Write w(t, ·) = Σ_{α ≠ 0} t^α w_α/α! (Taylor in t; w_α := ∂^α w(0, ·)). For any family of bounded random variables X_α and scalars s_α, log E exp(Σ_α s_α X_α) = Σ_{k ≥ 1} (1/k!) Σ_{α_1, …, α_k} s_{α_1}⋯s_{α_k} κ^ν(X_{α_1}, …, X_{α_k}) (the cumulant generating function; convergent for small s). Put s_α = t^α/α! and take the coefficient of Π_{i∈B} t_i. The only contributing multi-indices have α_j ∈ {0, 1}^B with α_1 + … + α_k = 1_B, i.e. ordered partitions of B into k nonempty blocks, each with α_j! = 1. The 1/k! cancels the ordering. ∎
Bound for joint cumulants of bounded variables [P]: for k ≥ 2, κ^ν is invariant under shifting any argument by a constant, and
  |κ^ν(X_1, …, X_k)| ≤ Σ_{σ ⊢ [k]} (|σ| − 1)! Π_{S ∈ σ} E|Π_{j∈S} X_j| ≤ F_k Π_j ‖X_j‖_∞ ≤ 2^k k! Π_j osc(X_j),
F_k the Fubini number (F_k ≤ 2^k k! for all k; F_1 = 1, F_2 = 3, F_3 = 13). Here osc(X) := sup X − inf X after shifting X by a constant so that ‖X‖_∞ ≤ osc(X). For k = 1, κ^ν(X) = E_ν X.

### 3.2 Where the rate stops [P]

### 3.3 Compactness: the subsequential limit exists [P]
Two routes, both standard, give the same conclusion.
(i) Functional route (as ASSEMBLY §2.4 with one more index). (H-U∞) bounds S_{n,ε,ℓ} on D_n(B_j) uniformly in (K, ℓ). (ii) Measure route (identifies the limit). For R ∈ N let ν_{K,ℓ}^{(R)} be the marginal of ν_{K,ℓ} on the unit links inside [−R, R]⁴ (ℓ > 2R + 2). It is a probability measure on the compact space SU(2)^{links in the box}. By Tychonoff and a diagonal argument there is a subsequence along which ν^{(R)} converges weakly for every R, to the marginals of one probability measure ν_∞ on SU(2)^{links of Z⁴}. The functionals ∂^SΔ_∞(0, ·) are bounded, continuous on the small-field set, and depend on the links near the supports up to tails ≤ Σ_{d ≥ R} e^{−κd}·(count), so their ν_{K,ℓ}-expectations and joint cumulants converge along the subsequence (approximate uniformly by functions of finitely many links). ## 5. OS1: what symmetry the limit has

### 5.1 Translations [P given (H-U∞)]
Exact at every (ε, ℓ): translation by a ∈ εZ⁴ (mod ℓ) preserves Haar measure, the Wilson action, and the plaquette set, and c_{ε,ℓ} is p-independent. So S_{n,ε,ℓ}(F ∘ τ_a) = S_{n,ε,ℓ}(F) for all a ∈ εZ⁴. The RG blocks and their origin play no role for the symmetry; they enter only through (H-U∞), which must be, and is, uniform in the position of the supports relative to the blocks (D4D5 Lemma C Step 4 "for every position of the support relative to the grid"; B21_S §4 "position uniformity" [V]). So the answer to the brief's question is: translations by the FINE lattice εZ⁴ are exact, not only by multiples of the coarsest block. Continuous translations: for a ∈ R⁴ pick a_i ∈ ε_iZ⁴ with |a − a_i| ≤ 2ε_i; |S_{n,ε_i,ℓ_i}(F ∘ τ_a) − S_{n,ε_i,ℓ_i}(F ∘ τ_{a_i})| ≤ A_B^n (n!)^D · 8 n ε_i ‖F‖_{sn+1} by (H-U∞) (mean value theorem, B enlarged by 1), → 0. Hence S_n(F ∘ τ_a) = S_n(F) for all a ∈ R⁴ in every limit. (ASSEMBLY §2.6 verbatim with ℓ_i → ∞ [V].)

### 5.2 Hypercubic group [P]
The origin is a site of every lattice L^{−K}Z⁴ (nested, L odd, origin fixed). The group B_4 of signed permutations of the axes about the origin is an exact symmetry of each μ_{ε,ℓ} and of the plaquette set. So S_n(F ∘ R) = S_n(F) for R ∈ B_4 in every limit; with §5.1, B_4 about every point of R⁴. The time reflection θ of §4 is an element of B_4 (times a translation by ε e_0). Nothing beyond B_4 ⋉ R⁴ is available.

The dimension-6 part is bounded, never shown to be small in the O(4)-breaking direction specifically; "irrelevant" in Balaban's bookkeeping means "carries a power of L^{−j} relative to scale k", which is exactly the claim one needs, but for the SYMMETRY-BREAKING projection of the whole effective action, including the large-field and R-terms, along the whole flow.
