## 0. The gap in Balaban's own words

- CMP 109 (1987), p. 259, after Theorem 2 [quoted, local OCR]: "A proof of this theorem, based on perturbative calculations, will be given in a separate paper, where more precise asymptotic behavior will be proved. The restriction to the group SU(2) is superficial and is done only to simplify calculations."
- CMP 109, p. 259, on Theorem 1 [quoted]: "It is interesting to notice that we do not assume any special asymptotic behavior of the coupling constants, like asymptotic freedom. Hence in principle arguments could be applied to models with different behavior of running coupling constants, for example to the so-called asymptotically safe models, or finite models."
- CMP 122 (1989) 355–392, p. 355 [quoted]: "Theorem 2 of [I] allows us to remove the assumption on the effective coupling constants in the above theorem, because this assumption follows from the basic inequality (0.31) [II], which is the result of Theorem 2. The proof of Theorem 2, which is based on second order perturbative calculations, is very awkward and long in the context of the renormalization group approach to lattice gauge field theories, and has not been published yet, so we have Theorem 1 with the assumption."
- Citation detail [quoted, reference list of CMP 122 II]: there "[I]" = CMP 109 and "[II]" = CMP 116. The inequality (0.31) is in CMP 109, not CMP 116. So "(0.31) [II]" looks like a citation slip in the original [inference].
- CMP 119 (1988) and CMP 122 I (1989) both state without proof [quoted]: "For d=4 the bare coupling constant behaves asymptotically as (a + b log ε^{-1})^{-1/2}, for ε→0, with some positive constants a, b". This is the consequence of Theorem 2. It is used to motivate the R operation, not proved [inference].
- The "Theorem 2" in Sect. 2 of CMP 119 and the "proof of Theorem 2 in Sect. 2 [III]" cited at CMP 122 II p. 391 are a different theorem (bounds on renormalization expansions), not the running-coupling theorem [inference, from local text of CMP 119 lines on "The Proof of Theorem 2"].

## 2. Rigorous one-loop beta coefficient inside a block-spin / multiscale RG

No 4D lattice gauge result found. [not found]. Nearest rigorous analogues:

- Gawędzki–Kupiainen, "Continuum limit of the hierarchical O(N) non-linear σ-model", CMP 106 (1986) 533–550. Asymptotically free 2D model, hierarchical kinetic term. The OpenAI O(3) preprint describes it [quoted]: "Gawędzki and Kupiainen constructed a continuum limit with asymptotic freedom for a sigma model with a hierarchical kinetic term [16]. In that setting the hierarchical covariance reduces the flow to a single-spin recursion."
- Pordt–Reisz, "On the renormalization group iteration of a two-dimensional hierarchical non-linear O(N) σ-model", Ann. IHP Phys. Théor. 55 (1991) 545–587. [quoted-fetch, bibliographic only; content not read]
- Mitter–Ramadas, "The two-dimensional O(N) nonlinear σ-model: renormalisation and effective actions", CMP 122 (1989) 575–596. Perturbative only: Wilson renormalized trajectory "in perturbation theory, in terms of the effective charge". [quoted-fetch, IAS record of abstract]
- Gawędzki–Kupiainen, "Gross–Neveu model through convergent perturbation expansions", CMP 102 (1985) 1–30. 2D, fermionic, UV asymptotically free, block-spin; the flow uses the leading beta coefficient non-perturbatively. [bibliography quoted-fetch; content claim = memory]. Feldman–Magnen–Rivasseau–Sénéor did the same model in a multiscale scheme (CMP 103, 1986). [memory]
- Gawędzki–Kupiainen massless lattice φ⁴₄ (CMP 99, 1985) and Bauerschmidt–Brydges–Slade (4D weakly self-avoiding walk and φ⁴, 2014–2015): infrared asymptotic freedom with the one-loop coefficient controlled rigorously. Scalar, not gauge. [memory]
- Magnen–Rivasseau–Sénéor YM4 (1993), continuum with axial gauge and infrared cutoff, closest 4D gauge analogue. Their treatment of the flow is itself a sketch [quoted, local tex, Sect. V.E]: "These counterterms in turn generate a flow for the coupling constant of the theory which is the one of an asymptotically free theory such as the Gross-Neveu model in two dimensions and treated in the same way [FMRS2],[R]. In this way the ansatz (II.11) is justified." and "to control the flow we are not forced to construct the correct β function ... it is enough to get the two first terms correctly, plus a uniform bound of the correct order on the rest". They take β₂, β₃ "whose numerical value is given in standard textbooks like [IZ]" [quoted]. They compute one-loop A⁴ counterterm coefficients explicitly (Sect. III), but I found no explicit derivation of β₂ inside their scheme. [inference from grep]
- Unrefereed 2026 claim: OpenAI family 215 (O(4) exact mass, "m_lat(β) ~ 32 e^{π/4−1/2} √β e^{−πβ}") would need one- and two-loop flow control in a nearest-neighbor 2D block RG. The preprint intro says [quoted]: "Up to bounded positive multiplicative factors, the two-loop calculation identifies this choice with ..." and on prior work "These results are relevant predecessors of the block analysis; their hypotheses and conclusions do not supply the exact iterated O(4) density estimates required here." Bauerschmidt (Scientific American) called the first paper of the set "groundbreaking and comprehensible" and the second "horrible" [quoted-fetch]. Status: not verified by anyone I found.
