## 5. CMP 119 pp. 277–281 and CMP 109 (4.36): the intended meaning of "do not depend on Λ_{k+1}" [V text layer; P reading]
5.1 CMP 119 p. 278 [V], the full paragraph: "Finally, the operators in the first integral on the right-hand side of (3.37) are represented by the generalized random walk expansions. After the integration we get a representation of the form (3.47) … The important remark is that the terms E_0^{(k+1)}(Λ_{k+1}, X, z) of this representation, for X ⊂ Λ_{k+1}, do not depend on Λ_{k+1}, and coincide with the corresponding terms arising from the expressions defined on the whole lattice, i.e. in the framework of [I]." p. 279 [V]: "We put E^{(k+1)}(X, z) = E_0^{(k+1)}(Λ_{k+1}, X, z) for z ∈ Λ°_{k+1}, X ⊂ Λ_{k+1} … The remaining terms in the expansions (3.47) are included into the boundary term B^{(k+1)}." And for R″: "The terms with localization domains contained in Λ_{k+1} have the same important property as the corresponding E^{(k+1)}-terms, namely they do not depend on Λ_{k+1}."
5.2 CMP 119 p. 281 [V]: the coefficient level is treated differently: "The difference between the two sums contributes to the irrelevant terms only, by the bounds (3.48)", and Π^{(j)}_{μν,κλ} is computed "with E^{(j)}(X, U_j, z) replaced by E^{(j)}(U_j, z) defined on the whole lattice L^{−j}Z^d. This function is translation invariant".
5.3 Decision [P, from 5.1–5.2 and A15_WHOLE §4.1 (CMP 109 (4.36), [V] there)]. Balaban uses two different statements at two different levels.
 (T) Term level (p. 278): a statement about individual terms "of this representation", i.e. about the activities as functions. It is stated without an error term; terms that fail it (localization near ∂Λ_{k+1}) are moved, as whole terms, into B^{(k+1)}. Nothing is "booked as irrelevant" at this level. So the intended meaning is EXACT equality of the functions, for the interior class of X. The exponential-error reading is not supported: p. 278 has no bound, and the only mechanism on pp. 278–279 is reassignment of whole terms.
 (C) Coefficient level (p. 281; CMP 109 (4.35)–(4.37)): the relevant coefficient Π (hence β) is a Z^d sum over all X ∈ D_j^0. The difference between the finite (cube-local) sum and the Z^d sum is "exponentially small" and is booked into the irrelevant terms, with the bound (4.36). This uses (T): the cube-local sum is made of the torus terms E(X), X ⊂ □̃², and they are reused unchanged as Z^d terms (A15_WHOLE §4.3(c), VERIFY V4).
 So (Loc) is the term-level statement (T), read exactly; the propagator image sums belong to level (C) and to wrapping terms (Section 6 shows where they go).
5.4 Reading caveat [I]. "the whole lattice, i.e. in the framework of [I]": CMP 109 itself works on a torus T (A15_WHOLE §3.2 [V]). So p. 278 literally compares the Λ_{k+1}-restricted construction with the CMP 109 construction on the full lattice of the problem (no small-field boundary). It does not literally say "Z^d". The torus-vs-Z^d identity is the CMP 109 p. 264 claim "This limit exists by the localized representation (1.7)" plus p. 281 "defined on the whole lattice L^{−j}Z^d". Both readings need the same mechanism (Section 6): the activity of a non-wrapping X sees only walks inside X. Section 6 proves (Loc) for torus vs Z^4 directly; the Λ_{k+1}-boundary half stays the separate reading (f) of A15_WHOLE §7.

## 6. Proof of (Loc) in the exact form (part 1: setting and walk lemma)
6.0 Setting. Level k. T_N = Z^4/NZ^4 in level-k lattice units, π: Z^4 → T_N the projection. All partitions used at level k (π_k cubes of side M = L^m [V, CMP 109 text l. 534]; π_k(1) cubes of side M_1; σ cubes of side R_1M_1, R_1 "a power of L" [V, CMP 116 p. 3]; π_{k+1} cubes of side LM) are taken as π-images of the translation-covariant Z^4 partitions. This needs N divisible by every cube side (true for N = L^n with all sides powers of L) and the same origin [I: convention; M_1 a power of L is not checked on the page]. Let c_k := the largest cube side used at level k (one LM cube plus one σ cube, plus the 5-layer enlargement of (L2)).
 Definition (liftable). A set S ⊂ T_N is liftable if S ⊂ π(B) for an ℓ^∞ box B ⊂ Z^4 of side < N/2. Then π|_B is injective, and for x, y ∈ B, d_{T_N}(πx, πy) = |x − y|_∞ (each coordinate difference is < N/2, so no shift by γ ∈ NZ^4 \ {0} shortens it). So π|_B preserves distance, cube membership, adjacency of cubes (shared wall), and connectedness of cube families. Write Ŝ := (π|_B)^{−1}(S) and Û for the lift of a configuration on S. A domain X is non-wrapping if its collar X^♯ (X plus one layer of width c_k) is liftable. Sufficient: diam_∞ X ≤ N/2 − 3c_k.
 Definition (intrinsic family). A family F = (F_V(Y))_Y, defined in each volume V ∈ {T_N, Z^4} for the domains Y of V, is intrinsic if F_{T_N}(Y)(U) = F_{Z^4}(Ŷ)(Û) for every non-wrapping Y and every configuration U on Y (kernels compared at lifted points). (Loc) says: the activities E^{(k+1)}(X) form an intrinsic family.
6.1 Input (FI) — intrinsic walk factors [R/I]. The factors R_α(Y) of the generalized random walk expansions ((3.107) of CMP 99; CMP 116 (1.6)) for H, H_0, G, the operators "determining Δ_k", C^{(k)}(Z_0), and the resolvent G_3(x) of CMP 116 p. 13, form intrinsic families. Support: WG_FULL (L1)–(L2) and A1_WALK_FULL (L1)–(L2) prove that each factor has kernel in Y° × Y° and depends on U and on domain data only in Ỹ^5 [P there, relative to CMP 99 Thms 3.7–3.10 [R]]. (FI) adds: the formula that produces R_α(Y) from those data is the same on T_N and on Z^4. This holds when every factor is built from local operators restricted to Ỹ^5 (local inverses on cubes, block averages Q, the δ-elimination C, h, which are block-local, CMP 109 p. 267 [V]); a finite matrix built from the same entries is the same matrix. It fails only if some factor uses a global object (a global projector, a global inverse, a global constant). None is visible on CMP 116 pp. 2–3 or 13 [V]; the factor-level construction of CMP 99 Sect. C is not re-read here [I].
6.2 Lemma W (s-truncated walk sums are intrinsic) [P given (FI) and the absolute convergence (B1)–(B2) of WG_FULL / A1_WALK_FULL]. Let O = Σ_ω O_ω be one of the walk-expanded operators, and O(s) the s-dependent operator of the s-rule (§3.3). Let Y_0 be non-wrapping and s = s(Y_0) (s(Δ) arbitrary complex for σ-cubes Δ ⊂ Y_0, s(Δ) = 0 for the others). Then
 (a) O_{T_N}(s)(y, y′) = 0 unless y, y′ ∈ Y_0 (the same on Z^4 with Ŷ_0);
 (b) O_{T_N}(s)(y, y′) = O_{Z^4}(ŝ)(ŷ, ŷ′) for y, y′ ∈ Y_0, ŝ the lift of s;
 (c) O_{T_N}(s) depends on U only on Y_0, and on s only through s|_{Y_0}.
 Proof. A walk ω = ((α_0, X_0), …, (α_n, X_n)) keeps a nonzero weight at s(Y_0) only if every σ-cube that meets X_0^5 ∪ … ∪ X_n^5 lies in Y_0 or in int □^4 ⊂ Y_0 (s-rule, §3.3). So ∪X_i^5 ⊂ Y_0. Lift each X_i to X̂_i ⊂ Ŷ_0 (unique, since π|_{B} is injective on Y_0^♯). Consecutive domains of a walk overlap or are adjacent; π|_B preserves this, so ω̂ = ((α_i, X̂_i)) is a walk of the Z^4 expansion, localized in Ŷ_0, with the same set of σ-cubes, hence the same s-weight. Conversely each Z^4 walk localized in Ŷ_0 projects to a torus walk localized in Y_0. So ω ↦ ω̂ is a bijection between the surviving walks. For each pair, the kernel of O_ω is a matrix product of factor kernels; every intermediate summation point lies in some X_i° ⊂ Y_0 (L1); by (FI) each factor kernel equals its lift. So O_ω(y, y′) = O_ω̂(ŷ, ŷ′). Sum over the bijection; both series converge absolutely by (B1)–(B2), so the order of summation is free. (a), (c) follow from (L1), (L2). ∎
6.3 Where the image terms go [P]. On T_N, a walk whose domain chain winds around the torus (its lift is not a closed chain of the same domains) has ∪X_i^5 not liftable. It meets a σ-cube outside every non-wrapping Y_0, so it is deleted at s(Y_0). The full torus kernel O_{T_N}(1)(y, y′) = Σ_γ O_{Z^4}(ŷ, ŷ′ + γ) (periodization) is the sum over all walks; its γ ≠ 0 image terms are carried exactly by the winding walks. In the cluster formula (1.9)–(1.10) those walks contribute only to terms whose Y_0 is wrapping. So the periodization of propagators does not contradict exact (Loc): image terms sit in wrapping activities, not in non-wrapping ones.

## 7. Proof of (Loc) in the exact form (part 2: the assembly, induction)
7.1 Lemma N (nonlinear maps) [P given Lemma W and (U) below]. Fix a non-wrapping Y_0 and s = s(Y_0). On T_N and on Z^4, D(H(s), A′), A_0(H(s), G(s), H_0(s)B′) and H_k(B′)(s) restricted to Y_0 are the same functions of (U|_{Y_0}, B′|_{Y_0}, s|_{Y_0}) under the lift.
 Proof. By §3.4 [V], at s(Y_0) Eq. (1.3) reduces to D = C(A′) off Y(σ) and to separate equations on the components; Eq. (1.4) likewise, with A_0 = 0 off Y(σ). On the component Y_0 the equation is a fixed-point equation whose coefficients are the local analytic functions C, V_0, δV/δA′ (explicit local formulas, CMP 109 (2.5)–(2.8), CMP 116 (1.2)–(1.4)) and the kernels of H(s), G(s), H_0(s) on Y_0 × Y_0. By Lemma W the kernels are equal under the lift; the local formulas are the same. So the two equations on Y_0 and Ŷ_0 are the same equation. (U): it has exactly one solution in the analyticity ball used by Balaban ([15] Sects. C, E, quoted CMP 116 p. 3 "having all the properties proved in [15]") [R]. Hence the solutions coincide. Components other than Y_0 do not enter the Y_0 term (their s-derivatives give 0, §3.4 [V]). B′ = B′(B) is local (CMP 116 p. 3 [V]). ∎
7.2 Lemma G (Gaussian integrals, square root, normalizations) [P given Lemma W; R where marked].
