## 1. CMP 109 pp. 261–266: what the inductive assumption says, and the global objects of the step [V, text layer; PDF page = journal page − 248]
1.1 p. 261 (1.7) [V]: "The function E^{(j)}(g_{j−1}, U_j) is a result of an integration in the j-th step, after a subtraction of the previous action evaluated at U_j. We assume that it has the representation [(1.7) Σ_{X∈D_j}]. Again, the term corresponding to a domain X depends on U_j restricted to X. 1.2 p. 261 (1.9) / p. 262 [V]: E^{(j)}(X, g_{j−1}, U, J) are "functions defined on larger spaces of regular gauge field configurations", analytic on U^c_j(X, α_0, α_1); "U is defined at bonds of X … J is also defined at bonds of X". So the domain of the function is intrinsic to X [V]. ## 3. The final localization: CMP 116 Sect. 1, pp. 2–5 [V, text layer]
3.1 p. 2 [V]: the terms of CMP 109 Sects. 3–5 "are analytic and nonlocal functions of the configurations U, J, B. The nonlocality is, however, of a simple origin, it is introduced by the functions H_j, H_k, propagators and other expressions appearing after the expansions." Structure: "there exists a function E(X, U, J, A) analytic and localized to X in U, J, A, such that a given term is obtained substituting a proper nonlocal expression in the place of A". Substituting A = (tζ_□ + t_□ζ_□)H_k(B′) (1.1) "we introduce, through the function H_k(B′), a dependence on U, J, B on the whole lattice. Our problem is to localize the obtained function".
3.2 pp. 2–3 [V]: H_k(B′) = H_0B′ + A_0 − HD(H_0B′ + A_0) (1.2); D(A′) = C(A′ − HD(A′)) (1.3); A_0 + G(δ/δA′)V(H_0B′ + A_0) = 0 (1.4). "Eqs. (1.3), (1.4) are determined by local, analytic functions, and nonlocal propagators H, H_0, G." "The nonlocality of these solutions is a result of a nonlocality of the operators, hence we localize the solutions by localizations of the operators." Each propagator is the walk sum (1.6) = (3.107) of [13] (CMP 99); "Each factor … depends on external gauge field configurations restricted to the corresponding domain X_j^5, and its kernel vanishes in a sufficiently thick neighborhood of ∂X_j".
3.3 The s-rule, p. 3 [V]: cubes Δ of σ_0 disjoint from int □^4; "for a random walk ω localized in X_0^5 ∪ … ∪ X_n^5 we take the {Δ_1, …, Δ_m} of all cubes from σ_0 which intersect this localization domain, and we multiply the term … by s(Δ_1)···s(Δ_m). This way the s-dependent propagators H(s), G(s), H_0(s) are defined. They coincide with the original ones for s = 1."
3.4 (1.9)–(1.10), p. 4 [V]: fundamental theorem of calculus in s; Y(σ) = □^4 ∪ (∪_{Δ∈σ}Δ); Y_0 = the component containing □^4. "kernels of the operators H(s), G(s), H_0(s) vanish, unless both arguments are in the interior of one component of Y(σ) … Eq. (1.3) … reduces to the equality D(A′) = C(A′) on the neighborhood of Y(σ)^c, and to separate equations in components of Y(σ)." "Eq. (1.4) represents a system of separated equations in components of Y(σ). The solution A_0 … is equal to 0 on the neighborhood of Y(σ)^c". "If there are other components, then the derivatives with respect to s restricted to these components render the term equal to 0." p. 5: "In the term corresponding to Y_0 in the above sum all propagators and the function B are restricted to Y_0. This implies that this term depends on U, J restricted to Y_0".
3.5 Reading [P from 3.3–3.4]. At s = s(Y_0) (s = 0 on σ_0-cubes off Y_0), H(s) keeps exactly the walks whose localization domain meets only cubes of Y_0 ∪ int □^4. So the term for Y_0 is a fixed function F_{Y_0} of: (a) the local analytic functions E(□_0, U, A), C, V_0, B′(B) (explicit local formulas); (b) the finite family of walk terms localized in Y_0; (c) the s-variables of Y_0. The nonlinear objects D, A_0, H_k(B′) on Y_0 are the unique solutions (contraction / fixed point, [15] Sect. C, E) of equations whose coefficients are (a) and (b) only. This is the mechanism that makes a Balaban activity strictly finite-range: not truncated-domain propagators alone, but s-truncated walk sums inside nonlinear equations that decouple by components.
