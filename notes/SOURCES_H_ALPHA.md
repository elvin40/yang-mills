## 2. Test of the three ideas

2.1 Idea (i): rescaled field B = g_jB′. FAILS [P].
Let f(B) := E^{(j)}(X)(U_j(exp iB)), analytic on |B| < ρ (all bonds, sup norm), |f| ≤ E. Put h(B′) := f(gB′). Then h is analytic on |B′| < ρ/g, |h| ≤ E, and ∂²h(0) = g²∂²f(0). Cauchy for h gives |∂²h(0)| ≤ E(g/ρ)², hence |∂²f(0)| ≤ E/ρ². This is the bound for f. A linear change of variables does not change the Cauchy constant. In B′-units the printed radius α_{1,j}/g_j = C_1(log g_j^{−2})^{q_1} grows, but β is the B′-quadratic coefficient divided by g², so the gain cancels exactly. A better bound needs a better sup bound, not other units.

2.2 Idea (iii): adapt Lemma N3 to the shrinking domain. FAILS as a full route [P]; the M_β part alone survives.
Write u_j := g_j^{−2}, ρ_{B,j} := min(α_{0,j}, α_{1,j})/(6K_U), C_{Π,j} := K_2ρ_{B,j}^{−2} = 36K_U²K_2·C_min^{−2}u_j(log u_j)^{−2q} (frozen at x*_j; C_min := min(C_0, C_1) for the factor with exponent q). Then |β_j| ≤ M_j := cE_0C_{Π,j} (L1_F §5.5, corrected constants).
(a) The M_β part of N3 tolerates M_j [P]. In N3 Step 0 the complex correction to |∂β_j/∂u| is ½g_r³·|z − g_r|·8M_j/(ρ²g_r²) ≤ 4√2|y|g_r⁴M_j/ρ². With u*_j ≤ 2Re u_j (|Re u_j(c) − u*_j| ≤ 3r ≪ γ^{−2}, N3 Step 3) and t ↦ (log t)^{2q}/t decreasing for t ≥ e^{2q}, this is ≤ K′Y/(Re u_j(log Re u_j)^{2q}), K′ := 8√2·36K_U²K_2cE_0C_min^{−2}/ρ². By N3 Step 2, Re u_i ≥ γ^{−2} + (β/2)(K − i), so

finite and → 0 as γ → 0, because q ≥ 2 (P4). (b) The real input does not survive [P]. N3 Step 0 also needs the real bound |∂β_j/∂g_{j−1}| ≤ C_1 (R-037). Its small-field half is C_β = C_ΠS_0 (SOURCES_GGAIN §4.5: β = Π(E), ∂_g commutes with Π, the norm of Π on the (1.18) sup norm is C_Π), and its history half is (C_Π/C_V)S_0λ^{j−1−i} (SOURCES_HHIST l. 101). 2.3 Idea (ii): absolute constants. The constants E_0, κ are absolute (SOURCES_CTR_RESIDUE_VERIFY, quoted in the brief), but an absolute sup bound on a shrinking domain gives only C_{Π,j} (2.1). So (ii) helps only if the DOMAIN can be taken absolute. Whether it can depends on which variable the step cut acts on. Toy model [P, numerics inline]:
 Z_tot(b) := ∫_{|b+x|<δ} e^{−x²/(2g²)}dx (cut on the total field), Z_fl(b) := ∫_{|x|<δ} e^{−(1+b²)x²/(2g²)}dx (cut on the fluctuation, background in the integrand).
Z_tot(iη) = e^{η²/(2g²)}∫_{−δ}^{δ}e^{−y²/(2g²)}cos(yη/g²)dy. For η > δ the boundary part dominates and the cosine changes sign: at g = 0.1, δ = 0.5 the normalized Z_tot(iη) is 1.0000 at η = 0, 1.0038 at η = 0.45, and changes sign between η = 0.580 and 0.585 (trapezoid rule, 2·10⁴ nodes, run inline, no file kept). So log Z_tot has a branch point at distance ≈ 1.16δ: the analyticity radius in b is tied to the cut. In contrast Z_fl(b) = g(2π/a)^{1/2}erf(δ(a/2)^{1/2}/g), a := 1 + b², is zero-free for Re a > 0 (|b| < 1 suffices), independent of δ and g, because erf has no zeros in the sector |arg z| < π/4 [I, external: standard fact on the zeros of erf; the first zero is ≈ 1.4506 + 1.8809i, arg ≈ 52°]. So a cut on the fluctuation field allows an absolute background radius; a cut on the total field does not.
By Q1 the whole-lattice CMP 119 step is of the Z_fl type: Balaban introduces (3.16) precisely to have "characteristic functions depending on the fluctuation field only" (P7). This is the mechanism of route (ii), and it is the basis of §3.

## 3. (H-α) for the whole-lattice frozen chain: reduction to CMP 109 Theorem 3

3.2 Proposition A [R]. Let (κ, M, α_0, α_1, ε_0, ε_1, γ) be constants for which CMP 109 Theorem 3 holds (P3), chosen in the order of SOURCES_MC l. 104, and let SOURCES_MC (complex step, verified reduction) hold for them. Assume:

Then (H-α) holds with ᾱ_0 = α_0, ᾱ_1 = α_1 (CMP 109's absolute constants) and c the constant of SOURCES_MC §9 (Lemma MC), for γ small enough.

Proof.
Step 1 (step locality) [P given (A1)]. CMP 109 Theorem 3 is an induction on k. Its hypotheses at step k are the analyticity of E^{(j)} on U^c_j(X, α_0, α_1), the bound (1.18), and (1.19)–(1.22) (P2, P3). By (A1) none of them contains ε_1. So the induction step k → k+1 may use its own cut radius r_k, provided every condition that the proof of that step places on the cut radius holds for r_k.
Step 2 (the cut conditions hold for r_k := δ(x*_k)) [P given (A2), (A3)]. By SOURCES_MC l. 104 every condition on ε_1 is of one of two kinds.
 - Upper bounds given the earlier constants (B8, B12, B15, B16, B22): the admissible set is a down-set (0, ε̄_1]. δ(x*_k) = x*_kA_1(log x*_k^{−2})^{p_0} → 0 as x*_k → 0, so δ(x*_k) ≤ ε̄_1 for all k once γ is small (x*_k ≤ γ).
 - Lower bounds through ε_1/γ (B10, B11, B13, B14). Replace ε_1/γ by θ_k = A_1(log x*_k^{−2})^{p_0} ≥ A_1(log γ^{−2})^{p_0} =: θ_γ. B11: (1/20)γ_2θ_k² ≥ 4κ holds if θ_γ² ≥ 80κ/γ_2. B14 ⇐ e^{−γ_2θ_k²/20} ≤ δκ/24. B13: e^{−γ_2θ_k²/20} ≤ ε_2(δ(x*_k)); the left side is ≤ exp(−(γ_2A_1²/20)(log x^{−2})^{2p_0}), smaller than every power of x, and by (A3) the right side is at least a power of x. B10 is "γ small". So all four hold for γ small.
 - The complex step: SOURCES_MC replaces (ε_1, γ) by (ε_1/(4λ_V), γ/(8λ_V)) (MC_VERIFY corrections). The upper bounds use r_k/(4λ_V) ≤ r_k; the lower bounds depend on the ratio, which is unchanged (MC l. 120). So the same choice works [P].
Step 3 (composite configurations) [P]. The earlier E-terms are evaluated at composite configurations (background × real fluctuation, P7). With the cut r_k ≤ ε_1, the real curvature added by the fluctuation is at most what CMP 109 already allows with ε_1. So the domain conditions that CMP 109 checks for ε_1 hold a fortiori.
Step 4 (identification) [P given R-036 (verified) and Q1 (V)]. R-036 builds B_j factor by factor from CMP 109 (0.14)–(0.19) with frozen characteristic functions. ## 4. (H-α′) by a separate route: not proved

(H-α′) (SOURCES_L1_F_VERIFY V24) asks only for the mixed second derivative at B = 0. A direct proof would differentiate the step integral twice in the background at U = 1 and bound the result with no extension of the domain. The derivative hits (a) the Gaussian C*Δ^{(k)}C, (b) the vertex P^{(k)}, (c) the earlier E-terms at the composite configuration. The cut does not move (Q1), so no surface term arises. Items (a), (b) are one-loop and vertex correlations of size O(1) in Gaussian units. Item (c) is the history: it needs the same second derivative of all earlier terms, transported through the step, with a contraction factor, i.e. an (H-hist)-type induction for second background derivatives. That induction is the content of CMP 109 §5 (vacuum polarization, P3 "We will analyze this tensor thoroughly in Sect. 5"), which I did not read. So (H-α′) by itself stays an input; here it is obtained only as Corollary 3.4 of (H-α). The bidisc quadratic-smallness form of V24 would also follow from (H-α) (Schwarz lemma in each variable on the bidisc of radius ρ_B), so it is not a weaker target in practice.
