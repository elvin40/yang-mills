## 7. (W), (N), and the rates (items 5, 6)
- Scaling count rederived: one step maps f^m data of K-degree p to L^{2−p−2m} times itself; with evenness in K (§5) the f¹ sector has eigenvalues 1 (p = 0, spin coupling), L^{−2} (p = 2), …; f² sector L^{−2} (p = 0). In operator language n = p + 2m is (operator dimension − 2); the L^{−2} directions are the dimension-6 operators. Notation slip: §3b says "B_{10} maps degree-4 f⁰ data to degree-2 f¹ data"; with total degree conserved this means f¹ data of K-degree p = 2 (total n = 4). The logic uses the correct reading.
- (3b.3) rederived: e_j^{(1)} = Σ_k A_1^k B_{10} e^{(0)}_{j−1−k} + A_1^j e_0^{(1)}. With e^{(0)} = O(L^{−2j}) in n = 4 directions and A_1 = L^{−2} there, the sum is j·L^{−2j}; the marginal (p = 0) part of A_1^j e_0^{(1)} does not decay. Both statements correct.
- (W): at dimension 4 the only gauge-invariant, hypercubic-invariant term is Σ tr F_{μν}², so the spin coupling (f¹, p = 0) is tied to the Maxwell coefficient, which is exactly 1 at every level. This is the standard argument; it needs the derivative expansion of the nonlocal level-j kernel at K → 0 in the f¹ sector (analyticity at K = 0, cf. O5 for f⁰). Correctly labeled "argument, not proof". Plausible.
- (N): in operator language, (N) says the linearized step has no Jordan block in the dimension-6 sector. A gauge-invariant dim-6 operator has f⁰, f¹, f² components tied together; if each such operator were an eigenvector, P B_{10} v = 0 would follow from gauge invariance. But there are dim-6 operators with no quadratic (f⁰) part, e.g. tr F_{μν}F_{νρ}F_{ρμ}, whose f¹ component is p = 2. Tree-level mixing of the quadratic dim-6 lattice artifacts into such operators, with equal eigenvalue L^{−2}, gives exactly the Jordan block (3b.3). I see no reason for this mixing to vanish. So (N) is a real open statement and plausibly false; j·L^{−2j} (σ^{(1)}) and j²·L^{−2j} (σ^{(2)}, Jordan chain f⁰ → f¹ → f²) should be taken as the expected rates, not as a worst case only.
- (A): the gain argument (each K-derivative of the input carries L^{−1}) is correct for the input; the open part is that the one-step inverse 𝔨_0(K)^{−1} stays bounded in a complex strip uniformly in j. O5 (U3_REPAIRS) gives a uniform strip for Δ_M at f = 0, and O6 is closed under small perturbations (Neumann series), so a strip of fixed width follows at f = 0. For the f¹, f² coefficients (A) remains an assumption.
- Rates: "geometric convergence for any ρ > L^{−2}" follows from either j L^{−2j} or j² L^{−2j}. Correct.
Grades: (5) CONFIRMED as a reduction (the linear algebra is right; (W) plausible; (N) open and plausibly false, which the author does not claim either way). (6) CONFIRMED as a conditional statement (given one-step O6, now proved, plus (A) and (W)); the clean L^{−2j} should not be expected.
